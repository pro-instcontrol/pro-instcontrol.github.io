Why 4 mA is not zero

A live zero means a genuine zero reading still draws 4 mA, so 0 mA can only be a broken wire. Below 3.6 mA and above 21.0 mA the transmitter is allowed to report a failure instead of a value — which is why the two bands are coloured differently here.

02Differential-pressure flow

Square root, and why turndown hurts

Across an orifice, nozzle or venturi the differential pressure rises with the square of the flow (ISO 5167). Scaling from a known design point needs no discharge coefficient — but it does need an honest design point.

The trap

At 50 % of design flow the differential is only 25 % of span, so a ±1 % span error becomes ±2 % of reading. At 25 % flow the differential is 6.25 % of span and the same error becomes ±4 % of reading. This is why a DP meter is a poor choice for wide turndown, however good the transmitter.

Sizing rules of thumb

Aim for 20–80 % opening at normal flow, with the maximum at about 90 %. Below 10 % the plug is off its seat and control is poor; above 90 % there is no margin for a higher demand. Choked flow also means cavitation or flashing — check the trim material and consider an anti-cavitation cage.

04Hydrostatic level

ρgh, and the error you inherit from ρ

A DP transmitter does not measure level — it measures pressure and divides by a density you typed in once, years ago. Change the temperature, the product or the concentration, and the level reading moves without anything moving.

The dominant term

A 1 % density error is a 1 % level error — 50 mm on a 5 m tank, straight onto the operator's screen. If the product temperature swings, either compensate with a measured density or accept that the level is nominal.

05Loop error budget

Four errors, one number

The datasheet accuracy is only the first term. Installation, ambient temperature and the input card each add their own. Independent random errors combine as the root of the sum of squares; systematic ones add directly.

Which one to quote

Quote the RSS figure when the errors are genuinely independent and you are reporting a typical uncertainty. Quote the arithmetic sum when you must guarantee a limit, because it assumes every error lines up in the same direction — which is what a customer's auditor will assume if the number is challenged.

06Limits & caveats

Valve sizing is not a spreadsheet exercise

Kv from a formula gives you a number to compare against a catalogue. The final choice still depends on the trim characteristic, noise, cavitation, actuator thrust and what the purchasing department can actually deliver.

DP flow needs a real design point

The square-root scaling assumes the same fluid, the same density and the same orifice. Change the gas composition or the temperature and the relationship quietly stops being true.

Level is a density measurement in disguise

The uncertainty from ρ usually dominates the transmitter's own accuracy. If density is not measured, level is not measured — it is estimated.

Every number here is a starting point

These tools exist to catch mistakes early — a choked valve, a turndown that cannot work, a loop error larger than the tolerance. They do not replace the standard, the vendor's sizing software or a second engineer's check.
