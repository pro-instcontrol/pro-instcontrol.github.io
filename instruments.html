01Selection logic
01

Start from the variable

Range, normal operating point and required resolution come first. A meter specified on line size rather than flow range is the most common expensive mistake.

02

Then the fluid

Conductivity, viscosity, solids, entrained gas, corrosivity and temperature decide which principles are even possible before accuracy is discussed.

03

Then the environment

Hazardous area classification, ambient temperature, vibration, washdown and access for calibration. A brilliant instrument you cannot reach is a liability.

04

Then turndown

Check accuracy at the minimum flow you actually need, not at full scale. Orifice turndown is 3:1–5:1; Coriolis can be 100:1; that difference is often the whole business case.

05

Then the loop it sits in

A control loop needs speed and repeatability more than absolute accuracy. A custody measurement needs the opposite. The same fluid can justify two different devices.

06

Finally the lifecycle

Calibration interval, spare parts, local service and whether the diagnostics are readable by the people who will actually maintain it.

A note on accuracy claims

Vendors quote the best number under reference conditions. Compare like with like: % of span against % of reading, at the same ambient temperature, with the same turndown. A 0.05 % claim at 10:1 turndown is a different device from a 0.05 % claim at 1:1.
