From a physical quantity to a number you can trust

Every control loop begins with a measurement that is already imperfect. Understanding where the imperfection enters — and how it is expressed — is what separates a specification that works from one that only looks precise.

01The chain
01

Sensor

The element in contact with the process that converts a physical effect into a measurable one — a diaphragm deflection, a resistance change, a voltage.

02

Transducer

Converts that intermediate effect into an electrical quantity. Often the same silicon or ceramic body as the sensor; the boundary between the two is practical, not physical.

03

Transmitter

Linearises, compensates for temperature, applies damping, and drives a standard signal onto the wires. This is the device you specify, tag and calibrate.

04

Input & decision

An analogue input card digitises the signal; the controller compares it with the setpoint and computes an output. From here the value is data, not physics.

Each stage adds its own error. A transmitter specified at ±0.075 % of span mounted on a manifold with a 2 m uninsulated impulse line will not deliver ±0.075 % to the controller — the installation, not the datasheet, sets the real accuracy.

Practical consequence

Specify the loop, not the device. Write the accuracy requirement against the variable the operator actually needs — flow in t/h at the normal operating point, not pressure in bar at full scale.

03Why 4–20 mA survived

A live zero, a broken wire, and no voltage drop

The loop is a current source. Current is the same at every point in a series circuit, so a 50 Ω sense resistor at the far end reads the same value regardless of how much copper the signal travelled through. Voltage signals cannot say that — they lose a little at every joint.

Watch the fault band

Starting the scale at 4 mA instead of 0 gives a live zero: a genuine zero reading draws 4 mA, so 0 mA can only mean a broken wire or a dead transmitter. That single design choice is why a technician can diagnose a loop with a multimeter and no diagnostic software.

NAMUR NE 43 reserves the region below 3.6 mA and above 21.0 mA for fault signalling. A transmitter configured to saturate at 20 mA for a real over-range reading will be indistinguishable from a failure unless both ends agree on the convention.

04The vocabulary of error

Eight words that decide whether the number is true

Instrument datasheets are written in a private dialect. These are the terms that actually change a specification — and the traps hidden inside each.

TermWhat it meansThe trap
AccuracyCloseness of the indicated value to the true value, under reference conditions.% of span is not % of reading. At 20 % of span, ±0.5 % span becomes ±2.5 % of reading.
RepeatabilitySpread of readings for the same input, approached from the same direction.Usually much better than accuracy — quoting it instead of accuracy is a classic move.
HysteresisDifference between up-scale and down-scale readings at the same input.Mechanical linkages and valve packing dominate; invisible on a bench calibration.
DriftChange in zero and span over time, usually per year, at reference conditions.Drift sets the calibration interval. A ±0.1 %/yr device is a different asset from ±0.02 %/5 yr.
Dead bandInput change that produces no output change at all.In a control valve this is stiction, and it is the most common cause of a cycling loop.
ResolutionSmallest change the device can distinguish.A 12-bit input card resolves 1 part in 4096 of span — often coarser than the transmitter.
Response timeTime to reach 63 % (T63) or 90 % (T90) of a step change.Damping is often set to hide noise — and it adds dead time to the loop.
Turndown / rangeabilityRatio of maximum to minimum usable measurement within stated accuracy.An orifice is 3:1–5:1; a Coriolis meter can be 100:1. This decides low-flow performance.
Adding errors

Independent random errors combine as the square root of the sum of squares; systematic errors add directly. Do not average a datasheet accuracy with an installation error and call it the loop accuracy — treat the installation term as systematic and add it.

05Environment & certification
IP

Ingress protection

IEC 60529: first digit is solids, second is water. IP66/IP67 for washdown and outdoor; IP54 is not an outdoor rating in monsoon climates.

Ex

Hazardous area

IEC 60079 zoning: Zone 0/1/2 for gas, 20/21/22 for dust. Intrinsic safety (Ex ia) suits loops; flameproof (Ex d) suits high-power field devices. Entity parameters must match the barrier.

SIL

Functional safety

IEC 61508 defines SIL 1–4; IEC 61511 applies it to process SIS. A SIL rating is a property of the whole loop — sensor, logic solver, final element and proof-test interval — never of one device alone.

Also check

Ambient temperature range, EMC per IEC 61000, wetted-material compatibility, process temperature at the diaphragm, and whether the local display is readable at −5 °C in the morning.

06Installation decides the answer

Six rules that survive every project

A correct device installed wrongly reads wrongly forever. These are the failures that show up in commissioning, not in the datasheet.

Flow: respect the straight run

ISO 5167 asks for 5–10 D upstream and 2–5 D downstream for an orifice, depending on the upstream fitting. A 90° elbow two diameters upstream biases the reading by a few percent forever.

Temperature: immerse past the tip

A thermowell must reach far enough into the flow that the sensing element sees the process, not the well wall. Too short and you measure the pipe, with a time constant of minutes instead of seconds.

Pressure: slope the impulse line

Slope towards the process for gas, away for liquid, so condensate or gas bubbles drain and do not sit on the diaphragm. Add a condensate pot for steam and keep both legs at the same temperature.

Level: the reference leg is a measurement

On a closed tank the low-pressure leg is a second measurement. If it is wet and its density changes with ambient temperature, the level reading drifts with the weather.

Wiring: shield one end only

Grounding both ends creates a loop that picks up every drive in the plant. Ground at the cabinet, leave the field end floating, and separate signal from power in the tray.

Commission: prove the loop end to end

Inject at the sensor, read at the HMI, and record the deviation. Loop checking is not a formality — it is the only moment when the whole chain is verified at once.
