C4 Olefins, Dienes and Synthetic Rubber

The C4 cut is a small stream with an outsized product list: comonomers, fuels, solvents and the rubber that ties back to Unit 2's olefins.

Sources used. Chemistry of Petrochemical Processes (Matar, 2nd ed., 2000), ch. 2, ch. 9 and ch. 12; Wiley Critical Content: Petroleum Technology, Butadiene; Wiley Critical Content: Petroleum Technology, Isoprene; Petroleum Product Handbook, Section 12: Petrochemicals

The C4 cut and its isomers

The n-butenes are 1-butene, cis-2-butene and trans-2-butene. Matar notes that this mixture comes from the olefinic C4 fraction of catalytic and steam cracking after isobutene has been separated. The three isomers can be used together where they give the same intermediates, or split into a 1-butene stream and a 2-butene stream where different products are wanted.

About 70% of 1-butene is used as a comonomer with ethylene to make linear low-density polyethylene, LLDPE. Other routes give butylene oxide; n-butene can also be isomerized to isobutene, linking the C4 olefin pool to ethers such as MTBE and to higher-octane blending.

Butadiene and the 1,4 structure

Butadiene is a conjugated diolefin normally coproduced with C2-C4 olefins in cracking processes. Matar calls it the most important monomer for synthetic rubber, with large-volume links to MTBE, adiponitrile, 1,4-butanediol and polybutadiene. Conjugated dienes such as 1,3-butadiene give both 1,2- and 1,4-addition products; chlorination, for example, yields a mixture of 1,4-dichloro-2-butene and 3,4-dichloro-1-butene.

For synthetic rubber, coordination catalysts direct polymerisation toward predominantly 1,4-addition. That structural choice is what gives polybutadiene and styrene-butadiene rubber their elastomeric behaviour; it is a chemical decision with mechanical consequences.

Isoprene and the C5 side

Isoprene is the second major diolefin for rubber. Wiley identifies dehydrogenation of C5 olefins, particularly tertiary amylenes obtained by extracting a C5 fraction from catalytic cracking units, as its main source. Matar also lists alternative synthetic routes, reflecting the fact that extraction from cracking streams alone cannot meet demand.

The C4 and C5 stories are therefore linked by the same logic as the C2 and C3 stories: cracking creates a mixture, separation isolates a monomer, and polymerisation turns the monomer into a material. Each step adds a control problem and a specification.

Rubber as a petrochemical destination

Matar's Chapter 12 groups synthetic petroleum-based polymers into thermoplastics and engineering resins, thermosetting plastics, synthetic rubber and synthetic fibres. Styrene-butadiene-styrene is an important block copolymer rubber; the earlier C4 section explains where its butadiene block comes from, while the aromatics unit explains its styrene block.

This is a useful way to read a petrochemical complex: products are not isolated plants but chains that cross. A change in steam cracking severity moves ethylene, propylene, C4 and pyrolysis gasoline at the same time, and therefore moves polyethylene, polypropylene, rubber and aromatics together.
