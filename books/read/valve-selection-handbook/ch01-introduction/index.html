Introduction

Peter Smith & R. W. Zappe · Valve Selection Handbook, 5th ed. (2004)

Chapter 1 · 5 source pages · 1 figures · companion notes
Key terms. closure member · stop/start flow · throttle · divert · backflow · check valve · pressure relief valve · rupture disc · reclosing · simmer · chatter · deflagration · vent panel · piping specifying engineer.

The purpose of this book is to assist the piping specifying engineer in the selection of valves for a specific application that meet the design parameters of the process service. Valve selection is based on function, material suitability, design pressure/temperature extremities, plant life, end connections, operation, weight, availability, maintenance, and cost. The author deliberately placed cost at the end for a reason: if the valve does not meet the design criteria, then even if it is free, it is still too expensive — because of the costs to replace it when it fails. Just like life, valve selection is a series of compromises.

What valves are and what they do Van là gì và làm việc gì

Valves are the components in a fluid flow or pressure system that regulate either the flow or the pressure of the fluid. This duty may involve stopping and starting flow, controlling flow rate, diverting flow, preventing backflow, controlling pressure, or relieving pressure. These duties are performed by adjusting the position of the closure member in the valve — either manually or automatically. Manual operation also includes the operation of the valve by means of a manually controlled power operator.

In this book, valves that are manually operated for stopping and starting flow, controlling flow rate and diverting flow are referred to as manual valves, while valves for the prevention of backflow and the relief of pressure are referred to as check valves and pressure relief valves, respectively. Rupture discs are non-reclosing pressure-relieving devices which fulfil a duty similar to pressure relief valves.

Fundamentals (chapter 2) Cơ sở kỹ thuật (chương 2)

Sealing performance and flow characteristics are important aspects in valve selection; an understanding of these aspects is helpful and often essential in the selection of the correct valve. Chapter 2 deals with the fundamentals of valve seals and flow through valves. The discussion on valve seals begins with the definition of fluid tightness, followed by the sealing mechanism and the design of seat seals, gasketed seals and stem seals. The subject of flow through valves covers pressure loss, cavitation, waterhammer and the attenuation of valve noise.

Manual valves Van tay

Manual valves are divided into four groups according to the way the closure member moves onto the seat. Each group consists of a number of distinct types of valves that, in turn, are made in numerous variations. The way the closure member moves onto the seat gives a particular group or type of valve a typical flow-control characteristic. This characteristic has been used to establish a preliminary chart for the selection of valves; the final selection may be made from the description of the various types of valves and their variations that follow that chart.

Check valves Van một chiều

The many types of check valves are likewise divided into four groups according to the way the closure member moves onto the seat: lift, swing (single and double plate), tilting disc, and diaphragm. Their basic duty is to prevent backflow. However, the valves should also close fast enough to prevent the formation of a significant reverse-flow velocity, which on sudden shut-off may introduce an undesirably high surge pressure and/or cause heavy slamming of the closure member against the seat. In addition, the closure member should remain stable in the open valve position. Chapter 4 describes the design and operating characteristics of these valves and discusses the criteria upon which check valves should be selected.

Pressure relief valves Van an toàn áp suất

Pressure relief valves are divided into two major groups: direct-acting pressure relief valves that are actuated directly by the pressure of the system fluid, and pilot-operated pressure relief valves in which a pilot controls the opening and closing of the main valve in response to the system pressure.

  • Direct-acting valves may be provided with an auxiliary actuator that assists valve lift on opening and/or introduces a supplementary closing force on reseating. Lift assistance is intended to prevent valve chatter, while supplementary loading is intended to reduce valve simmer. The auxiliary actuator is actuated by a foreign power source; should it fail, the valve operates as a direct-acting pressure relief valve.
  • Pilot-operated valves may use a direct-acting pilot that controls the main valve by means of an internal mechanism, or an indirect-acting pilot that controls the main valve by means of the fluid discharged from the pilot.
  • A third type is the powered pressure relief valve, in which the pilot is operated by a foreign power source; this type is restricted to applications required by code.

Rupture discs Đĩa nứt vỡ

Rupture discs are non-reclosing pressure relief devices that may be used alone or in conjunction with pressure relief valves. The principal types are forward-domed discs, which fail in tension, and reverse-buckling discs, which fail in compression. Reverse-buckling discs can be manufactured to close burst tolerances; on the debit side, not all of them are suitable for relieving incompressible fluids.

While the application of pressure relief valves is restricted to relieving nonviolent pressure excursions, rupture discs may also be used for relieving violent pressure excursions resulting from the deflagration of flammable gases and dust. Rupture discs for deflagration venting of atmospheric-pressure containers or buildings are referred to as vent panels.

Units of measurement and size/class identification Đơn vị đo và quy ước định danh

Measurements are given in SI and imperial units. Equations for solving in customary but incoherent units are presented separately for solution in SI and imperial units, as presented customarily by U.S. manufacturers; equations presented in coherent units are valid for solving in either system.

The identification of valve sizes and pressure classes follows the recommendations of MSS Standard Practice SP-86: nominal sizes and pressure classes are expressed without units — e.g. NPS 2, DN 50, Class 150, PN 20. NPS 2 stands for nominal pipe size 2 in., DN 50 for diameter nominal 50 mm, Class 150 for class 150 lb, and PN 20 for pressure nominal 20 bar.

Not a control valve book Sách không bàn về van điều khiển

This book does not deal with control valves. For that field, the source directs readers to two ISA publications: (1) H. D. Baumann, Control Valve Primer, A User's Guide (3rd edition, 1998) — with new material on valve sizing, smart (digital) valve positioners, field-based architecture, network system technology, and control loop performance evaluation; (2) Guy Borden (ed.), Control Valves, Practical Guides for Measuring and Control (1998) — part of the ISA Practical Guide Series, whose last chapter also covers regulators and compares their performance against control valves.

Central idea: valve selection starts from the duty (stop/start, throttle, divert, prevent backflow, relieve pressure) and from the way the closure member moves onto the seat — that motion is what creates each family's flow characteristic and sealing mechanism. Cost comes last: a valve that fails the design criteria is too expensive even when free. The book follows exactly that order: sealing and flow fundamentals → each manual valve family → check valves → pressure relief valves → rupture discs → sizing → actuators → mechanical locking.
