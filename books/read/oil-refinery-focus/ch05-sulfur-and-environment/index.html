Sulfur Management and Environmental Control

Hydrotreating does not make sulfur disappear. It moves sulfur from hydrocarbon molecules into H2S, and the refinery then has to manage that H2S as a gas, a safety hazard and a product.

Sources used. Handbook of Petroleum Processing, ch. 8: Hydrotreating; Handbook of Petroleum Processing, ch. on refinery gas treating and environmental control; Handbook of Petroleum Refining Processes, KBR Refinery Sulfur Management and Gasoline Desulfurization; Petroleum Refining: Technology and Economics, ch. on hydrotreating

Reaction order, hydrogen use and heat release

The handbook ranks hydrotreating reactions in descending order of ease: removal of organometallic metals, olefin saturation, sulfur removal, nitrogen removal, oxygen removal and halide removal, with some aromatic saturation as well. Residue hydrotreating is essentially contaminant removal plus selective hydrogenation, and cracking normally stays below 20 vol% of fresh feed.

Desulfurization consumes 100 to 150 SCFB per weight percent change, equivalent to 17 to 25 Nm3/m3 per weight percent change. Denitrogenation consumes 200 to 350 SCFB per weight percent change, or 34 to 59 Nm3/m3. Heat released is about 0.02°F per SCFB of hydrogen consumed, or 0.002°C per Nm3/m3. Hydrogen consumption and heat of reaction are therefore linked, and reactor temperature control is a material balance problem as well as an energy balance problem.

Sulfur chemistry in four lines

Hydrodesulfurization, HDS, converts organic sulfur compounds to hydrogen sulfide. For mercaptans, R-SH + H2 gives R-H + H2S. Sulfides follow R1-S-R2 + 2H2 giving R1-H + R2-H + H2S, and disulfides need 3H2 to give two H2S. Thiophenes consume about 4H2 and return a hydrocarbon plus H2S.

Hydrodenitrogenation, HDN, runs alongside HDS but is slower and consumes more hydrogen. Sulfur is found across the whole boiling range in hundreds of different organic compounds, from naphtha to atmospheric residue. Treating a narrow cut therefore requires knowing which sulfur families dominate, not just the total sulfur number.

Recycle gas and injection water

Recycle gas contains H2S, which lowers hydrogen partial pressure and suppresses catalyst activity. The effect is stronger for high-sulfur feed and, for the same crude, for heavier cuts. The handbook gives a design trigger: recycle gas scrubbing is typically included when H2S in recycle gas is expected to exceed 3 vol%.

Water injection keeps H2S and NH3 in solution as the reactor effluent cools, preventing ammonium sulfide and ammonium chloride from precipitating. Boiler feed water is generally preferred. Reactor diameter is set by cross-sectional liquid flux, while multiple beds or reactors are chosen according to heat of reaction, capacity and the intended treating goal.

From acid gas to sulfur and clean products

The H2S leaving the hydrotreater is not a waste line to be overlooked. The Handbook of Petroleum Refining Processes devotes dedicated process descriptions to refinery sulfur management and gasoline desulfurization; in practice the acid gas goes through amine contact and a Claus-type sulfur recovery unit, with tail-gas treatment downstream where required.

The environmental chapters add the other half of the picture: effluent water treating, waste disposal, flare and emission control. Sulfur management, product specification and environmental compliance are one connected system, and the same instrument tags that protect the hydrotreater also protect the sulfur plant and the flare.
