Developments of Power System Protection and Control

Z. Q. Bo · X. N. Lin · Q. P. Wang · Y. H. Yi · F. Q. Zhou · XUJI Group Corporation, Xuchang, China · Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Wuhan, China

This chapter reviews the development history of power system protection, pays special attention to recent advances in wide-area and integrated protection, and then introduces the concept of integrated wide area protection and control (IWAPC), with a three-level hierarchy and a real-time information platform as its common basis.

ABSTRACT

Synchronized wide area communication has become a mature technology, which makes the real-time interaction between the substations and the wide area protection and control system possible. However, the present protection and control system for handling this real-time data has been recognised to be deficient. This chapter begins by reviewing the development history of power system protection, with special attention paid to recent developments in the field of wide-area and integrated protection, in order to look into the future of protection and control systems. The concept of integrated wide area protection and control is then introduced, where a hierarchical protection and control system provides protection and control for wide area or regional substations and plants and their associated power networks. The system is mainly divided into three levels: the local, the substation/plant and the wide area/regional levels. The integrated functions at each level are described in detail with the aim of developing an optimal coordination mechanism between the levels. The key element in the proposed system is the wide area real-time protection and control information platform, which not only enables the merger of the three lines of defence for power system protection and control, but also provides an excellent tool for the application of cloud computing in substations and power networks.

INTRODUCTION

Power system protection emerged at the beginning of the last century with the application of the first electromechanical overcurrent relay. The majority of the protection principles still in use today - overcurrent, directional, distance and differential protection - were developed within the first three decades of that century, as shown in Figure 1. Advances in modern science and technology, especially electronics and computing, promoted relay technology in materials, components and the manufacturing of hardware structures, while substantial theoretical progress was made in protection software and algorithms. From the emergence of protection to the end of the 1990s, relay protection passed through several stages, migrating from electromechanical to semiconductor, integrated circuit and microprocessor technologies. Today, microprocessor-based digital and numeric relays are replacing conventional relays in all areas of power system protection, although many of the original relaying principles still play a dominant role.

In the late 1960s, the application of a centralised substation protection system based on a centralised computer was proposed [1]. This constitutes an important milestone in the history of power system protection. The idea fits well with the concept of overall integrated protection, in which the protection package would oversee not only individual units of a plant but also a section of the network. However, the idea was not widely applied until recently, because no suitable computer hardware, software or communication technologies were available to support it. Since then, relay technology has developed successfully on the basis of digital techniques. The introduction of microprocessors into protection in the 1980s generally followed the conventional approach, with distributed processing platforms concentrating on protecting individual units of the system; only limited integrated protection was provided in the form of back-up protection, which therefore remained a secondary function.

Developments in the 1980s and 1990s provided new means to advance power system protection, especially adaptive protection and protection based on artificial intelligence (AI). Adaptive protection started with the application of Inverse Definite Minimum Time Overcurrent (IDMT) protection early in the history of protection. The concept played an important role in the 1980s with progress in computing technology and the associated control theory. It can be defined as a new type of relay protection that can change its performance, characteristics or set value according to the operating mode and fault condition of the power system. The basic idea is to protect the power system as fully as possible so as to improve protection performance. Adaptive protection has the advantages of improving the response of the system, enhancing reliability and improving economic benefits, with wide application prospects in distance protection, transformer protection, generator protection, autoreclosure and other areas. Research has found that, to achieve protection adaptive to the operating mode and fault status, more detailed system operation and fault information is required through a communication network.

The 1990s witnessed the rapid development of electronics and computing, together with the application of artificial intelligence techniques such as artificial neural networks, genetic algorithms, evolutionary algorithms, fuzzy logic and related research to the field of relay protection. For example, the artificial neural network (ANN) is used to achieve fault type identification, fault distance measurement and directional protection. The ANN has the characteristics of distributed storage, parallel processing, self-organisation and self-learning. The application of artificial intelligence improves the speed and accuracy of fault detection and analysis, and represents the future development of an intelligent diagnosis system. Although the performance of protection relays has been improved, these developments have focused on refining conventional relaying techniques, and no significant new relaying principles have been derived from adaptive and AI techniques.

Also in the 1990s, with the continuous expansion of the power network, the demand for fast fault clearance to improve system stability encouraged research into non-power-system-frequency fault detection techniques to increase the speed of relay response. This led to the development of so-called transient based protection relays, based on travelling waves and superimposed components, which use fault-generated transients for transmission system protection. Studies found that the high-frequency transients generated by a fault can be detected and quantified, creating the possibility of developing new protection principles and techniques [2]. Considerable effort has now been devoted to high-frequency transient detection. Another important milestone is the use of the global positioning system (GPS) in power system protection, as shown in Figure 1; in this respect a number of new techniques has been proposed. In particular, the newly proposed protection relay principle is able to provide protection for a wide area power network [3, 4]. Following this development, the concept of wide area protection focusing on the control aspect was presented [5].

In recent years, the dramatic growth in the signal processing capability of relay platforms and the availability of suitable communication schemes have provided a new opportunity to revisit the concept of centralised protection. Research [6] on the concept of integrated protection shows that information obtained from multiple power plants and components can be used to derive new protection principles and schemes, which could have significant advantages over existing protection techniques based on the individual plant or component. In this respect, substation area protection [7] has quickly become a practical area of development and application. Furthermore, the development of information technology has led to interest in using cloud computing [8] and big data techniques, as shown in Figure 1, to improve the performance of power system protection and control.

On another front, developments in substation integrated automation technology provide the technical basis for optimising the combination and system integration of monitoring, control, protection and measurement devices and systems. The implementation of relay protection and integrated automation is reflected in integration and resource sharing, remote control and information sharing. Taking the remote terminal unit and the microcomputer protection device as the core, the control, signal, measurement, billing and other circuits are integrated into the computer system to replace the traditional control and protection cabinet, reducing floor area and equipment investment while improving the reliability of the secondary system. With advanced computers and communication networks, the relay protection device is in fact a high-performance, multi-function computer, an intelligent terminal of the whole power system computer network. It can obtain from the network any information about the operation and fault of the power system, and can transmit any information about the protected components to the network control centre or to any terminal. Therefore each microcomputer protection device can complete not only the relay protection function but also measurement, control, data communication and other functions under normal operating conditions, realising the integration of protection, control, measurement and data communication.

RECENT DEVELOPMENT IN POWER SYSTEM PROTECTION AND CONTROL

With the rapid progress of high-speed communication networks and information technology, there have been significant developments in power system protection, power system control and wide area control in recent years, particularly in wide-area and integrated protection.

Wide Area Protection

In recent years, the rapid development of communication technologies has made wide-area information exchange possible. In this respect, the emergence of the wide area measurement system has provided a new idea for the design of power system protection systems. The first wide-area protection principle was derived from transient based protection in 1996 [2], in which GPS time synchronisation played a major role in the design [3]. This was immediately followed by a summary paper in 1997 that systematically outlined the concept of so-called wide area protection [4], focusing principally on the control aspect of the area. Wide area protection based on novel algorithms, derived from measurements at multiple information points, is able to provide fast, reliable and accurate fault clearance, to analyse the effects on system stability on the basis of fault system analysis, and to take the necessary control measures to perform the functions of relay protection, security and stability control in order to prevent voltage collapse. Wide area relay protection has quickly become a topical research subject, with many results published particularly in recent years.

Integrated Protection

With the development of digital technology, more and more protection functions for any given apparatus (line, transformer, generator, etc.) have been implemented within one protective device to achieve a certain degree of integration. For example, a numeric line protection relay may have a distance or current differential function as the main protection, and directional and overcurrent functions as the back-up protection. Recent developments in microprocessors and communication techniques have provided new means to derive protection principles and schemes based on information obtained from multiple power plants and components, which may have significant advantages over existing protection techniques based on the individual plant or component [5]. Unlike centralised protection (or substation area protection), integrated protection does not simply centralise the relay hardware and software, but concentrates on developing new concepts and algorithms based on multiple points of measurement; in this way it is hoped that protection performance can be significantly improved. There has also been research in the field of integrated wide area protection [9].

Wide Area Control

The increased deployment of wide-area measurements will significantly enhance wide-area power system operation and control. These measurements provide voltage and current phasor information synchronised with high precision to a common time reference supplied by GPS. Therefore a wide range of power system monitoring and control applications can be implemented to improve system awareness and reliability, including enhanced state estimation based on mixed Remote Terminal Unit (RTU) and Phasor Measurement Unit (PMU) measurements [10], online dynamic model estimation and validation [11], real-time congestion management, real-time stability estimation [12], and the detection and damping of inter-area oscillations [13]. However, the most important and challenging applications are the implementation of wide area real-time stability detection and control to prevent blackouts [14]. There has also been research on integrated protection and control [15].

Remote Terminal Unit (RTU) and Phasor Measurement Unit (PMU)

The RTU gathers local telemetry from devices in the substation and executes the control commands sent by the control centre; it is the foundation of traditional supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) systems. The PMU, in contrast, provides voltage and current phasors time-stamped against a common reference, enabling direct dynamic observation of system oscillations that conventional SCADA measurements cannot resolve. The combination of RTU and PMU measurements underpins enhanced state estimation [10] and opens the way to dynamic wide-area monitoring and control with greater detail and reliability. It is precisely this stream of synchronised data that feeds the wide-area protection and control schemes described in this chapter.

New Concept and Development

Based on the developments mentioned above, a new concept of integrated wide area protection and control (IWAPC) has been proposed recently. The main focus of the concept [16, 17] is the integration between protection and control, particularly at the wide-area or regional level, with the aim of bringing a number of benefits to future protection and control systems, for example the potential to merge the three lines of defence and to perform online self-healing decision making in order to prevent cascading tripping of a large area power network. The concept of integrated wide area protection and control is introduced as a three-level hierarchically coordinated system supported by a specially designed real-time synchronised wide-area communication network, providing protection and control for wide area or regional substations and plants and their associated power network. The key element in the system is the integrated wide area protection and control information platform, which receives real-time synchronised data from the communication network to support the integration of protection and control at the wide area/regional level.

The information platform also supports the application of a cloud computing system specially designed to implement a number of secondary functions for substations and power networks. In addition to the basic functions of relay protection, the platform should have a large capacity for fault information and data storage, fast data processing functions, powerful communication functions and other functions; it allows protection and control devices and the dispatching network to share the whole system data, information and network resources, and can also carry out remote monitoring together with the computer monitoring system of substation communication. With the proposed platform, the architecture of future substation equipment may be reshaped to provide a flexible framework for building an interactive grid, and subsequently to improve the reliability and security of power grids.

INTEGRATED WIDE AREA/REGIONAL PROTECTION AND CONTROL

Architecture of Integrated Wide Area Protection and Control

The proposed integrated wide area or regional protection and control system (IWAPC) is illustrated in Figure 2. There have been rapid developments in both power transmission and distribution networks, for example series compensation in AC lines and high-voltage DC lines in transmission systems, and distributed generation and energy storage in distribution systems. These developments result in far more complicated characteristics than those of conventional systems. Consequently, the existing protection and control system is no longer effective in coping with the new systems, which has led to the proposed IWAPC system. As shown, the IWAPC system consists of different equipment at different layers: from bottom to top, there is the integrated multiple-function intelligent equipment at the local level; the substation communication network and integrated substation protection and control at the substation level; and the wide area communication network, the integrated wide area information platform and integrated wide area (regional) protection and control at the wide area level. The key parts of the system are the high-speed wide area communication network and the real-time synchronisation information platform.

The IWAPC is further extended to dispatching in order to achieve the integration of dispatching automation, protection and control of the power grid, and, according to the three-level dispatching architecture (country, province, region), to implement regional protection, control and dispatching management functions.

Multiple-Function Intelligent Equipment at the Local Level

As shown in Figure 2, the intelligent equipment at the local level is an integrated multiple-function secondary device in the substation, mainly consisting of the MU, intelligent terminal, metering, PMU and local protection. The equipment is responsible for sampling all real-time data and sending information to integrated substation protection and control and to wide area protection and control. It also receives and carries out the control commands from the integrated substation protection and control and from the IWAPC. The equipment can be integrated into primary power apparatus and achieve local protection for about 90% of its associated line sections. It has a redundant configuration to ensure reliability, together with other integrated functions such as a fault recorder, data storage and network analysis.

Integrated Substation/Plant Protection and Control

Integrated substation protection and control combines the functions of line, busbar and transformer protection, breaker failure protection, autoreclosure, automatic bus transfer, under-frequency load shedding (UFLS), under-voltage load shedding (UVLS), overload inter-tripping and substation control functions. It uses information from the whole substation to achieve substation back-up protection and automatic safety control. The circuit breakers (CBs) are used as units to configure adaptive back-up protection, and current differential protection is used to replace the staged overcurrent protection, breaker failure protection and dead zone protection of the conventional protection system.

Integrated Wide Area/Regional Protection and Control

The IWAPC, specially designed for the protection and control of the power network, is able to offer fast protection. In addition, it integrates the functions of automatic UFLS and UVLS, voltage and frequency control, oscillation detection and out-of-step separation. The IWAPC also incorporates the function of transmission cross-section safety protection and control. Unlike conventional protection and control, which are separated in both design and operation, the IWAPC integrates protection and control into one optimally combined system, effectively coordinating wide area (regional) protection and control in order to achieve significant improvements in power system protection and control.

Synchronised High Speed Communication Network

The fast communication network is one of the most important elements of the IWAPC system. In this respect, the Packet Transport Network (PTN) may be a better choice for implementing such a task. The present power communication network is mainly based on a multi-service transport platform using the Synchronous Digital Hierarchy (SDH). Its advantages lie in high efficiency for carrying TDM services, low latency, high reliability and end management capabilities. However, with new trends in smart grid development, SDH technology has gradually revealed its limitations, such as low bearing efficiency and poor flexibility for data services. In contrast, PTN can realise statistical multiplexing and efficient transfer of packet services by using a packet-switched core, overcoming the weaknesses of rigid SDH bandwidth. In addition, it can provide good quality of service, operation, administration and maintenance.

A self-healing fibre optic network is used to connect a number of substations in the region, ensuring full sharing of dynamic and transient information for all electrical measurements, breaker status and protection operations; high-reliability IEEE-1588 technology is used to ensure the synchronisation timing of the shared data, providing data for integrated wide area protection and control. However, SDH remains an option for the task because it has been widely applied in power networks.

Synchronized Information Platform

A substation is installed with a wide range of electrical equipment with complex designs that is difficult to maintain. As the level of power system automation and intelligence continues to improve, the system network keeps expanding along with the huge amount of protection and control information. Since each piece of information is collected and stored by different devices in separate systems, the interoperability of internal power system data between systems is poor, while complex communication protocols tend to create information islands. Consequently, measurement data and protection control mechanisms cannot be shared, which restricts information integration. The protection and control of the smart grid needs to meet the demands of new application situations, to further improve the capabilities of the information platform for future key technologies, and to make the information platform system more open.

The real-time synchronised information platform accurately collects wide area information and conducts data mining to investigate the logical relations between real-time information, so as to increase sensitivity, reliability and fault tolerance. The data received from the platform includes static, dynamic and transient measurements and the states of circuit breakers. Valuable information is extracted from the data and allocated to specially designed computation algorithms in the platform to perform advanced protection and control functions for the power network. The transfer speed depends on the application: slow speed for contingency analysis, near real-time speed for monitoring, real-time speed for control and high speed for wide area protection, in particular time synchronization. The information can also include other types of data, such as the oil and ambient temperature of the transformer, wind speed and direction, and sun intensity. On the other hand, the information is stored in a hierarchical rather than a centralised manner, in keeping with the hierarchical protection and control system. Equipped with the latest high-speed synchronised communication technology and integrated with advanced protection techniques and the latest control system developments, the system offers not only fast protection but also complete control of the entire power network.

Advanced computing technology is introduced to establish a synchronised information platform for wide area protection and control, to build a panoramic operation and maintenance data collection network, to provide a standardised interface to terminal devices, and to form an information platform that is resource-sharing, flexible, interactive, open and ordered. In summary, advanced computing technologies are used to build a distributed collaborative intelligent information platform, simplifying terminal data collection equipment and breaking down the barriers between protection and control systems at different substations through the specially designed synchronised information platform.

Wide Area Power Cloud

Based on the information platform mentioned above, a distributed cloud system is designed to implement functions at the substation and regional levels, such as wide area fault location, fault line selection, power quality monitoring and protection settings. The extended functions also include equipment monitoring, life cycle and operation management, as shown in Figure 3.

Currently, many kinds of secondary equipment implementing different functions are installed in each substation, and an increasing number of small-capacity distributed energy resources added to the system greatly increases the number of devices. Implementing the complex functions of these devices in a specially developed distributed cloud system will greatly reduce equipment investment. The cloud at the substation level receives data from the process level, while the regional cloud receives data from the information platform, including static, dynamic and transient measurements and the states of circuit breakers; it extracts valuable information and allocates it to specially designed computation algorithms in the platform to perform advanced functions, identifying the faulted line, the accurate fault location and the harmonic content.

The cloud computing platform can make full use of the processing ability of the cloud to reduce the burden on terminal secondary equipment. Based on big data techniques, the computing clouds enjoy strong on-demand processing power. There is no need for endless upgrades to improve the processing capacity of the equipment, and no need to update the software in order to carry out a variety of tasks. The cloud system also brings many further advantages, such as wide area information sharing, standardisation of software and algorithms, and reductions in equipment investment, substation floor area and the workload of operation and maintenance.

CONCLUSIONS

This chapter presents an integrated wide area protection and control system based on a hierarchical structure, integrating protection and control at the local, substation and regional levels. Covering both transmission and distribution networks, the system is supported by the proposed high-speed synchronised communication network and the real-time protection and control information platform. Integrating advanced protection techniques and the latest developments in control systems, the system offers not only fast protection but also complete control of the entire power network. It offers the potential to merge the three lines of defence into a unified system so as to ensure more effectively the reliable and safe operation of the power grid. Based on the system information platform, a distributed power cloud system is also designed to support many advanced applications for integrated wide area protection and control.

With the continuous advances in measurement, communication and information technologies, the system has a bright future for practical application, and an overall improvement in protection and control performance can be expected. However, for the system to become useful in power system applications, it is equally important that its practical implementation be readily manageable, user-friendly and cost-effective. The authors hold that, to achieve these goals, the proposed integrated protection and control at the wide area level offers an appealing way forward.
