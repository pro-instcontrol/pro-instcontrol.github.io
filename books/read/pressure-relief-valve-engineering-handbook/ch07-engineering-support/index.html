Compressibility Factor, Z Hệ số nén Z

The compressibility factor Z corrects the ideal gas law when calculating mass or volumetric flow at relieving conditions. For a real gas the inlet density depends on the reduced pressure Pr = P/Pc and reduced temperature Tr = T/Tc (Pc, Tc from Table T7-7). If Z is unknown, use Z = 1.0.

Capacity Correction Factors for Back Pressure Hệ số hiệu chỉnh áp suất ngược

Back pressure is the pressure at the outlet of a relief valve caused by pressure in the discharge system or by flashing in the valve body. Without proper consideration of back pressure the valve may experience (1) a change in set pressure, (2) a change in closing pressure, (3) a decrease in relieving capacity, and (4) dynamic instability.

Back pressure in process service Áp suất ngược trong công nghệ

  • Built-up back pressure is the additional pressure at the valve outlet resulting from discharge-system resistance or flashing in the valve body.
  • Superimposed back pressure is the static pressure already present at the valve outlet when the valve is required to operate, from other sources in the discharge system.

The effect of back pressure depends on several coexisting conditions: constant vs. variable back pressure; valve closed vs. open and relieving; gas/vapor vs. liquid phase; and conventional vs. balanced valve construction.

Conventional Valves (Style JOS, Series 800/900) Van thường (Style JOS, Series 800/900)

Valve closed: constant superimposed back pressure raises the opening pressure by the amount of the back pressure, so the spring setting (cold differential test pressure) is based on set pressure minus back pressure. Variable superimposed back pressure shifts the opening pressure as it varies — a balanced valve is usually recommended. Valve open and relieving: built-up back pressure rapidly reduces the lifting forces; it must be limited to no greater than 10% of the set pressure. Excess requires a balanced valve.

For gases and vapors including steam, capacity is unaffected if back pressure is below the critical pressure (generally 55% of absolute relieving pressure). Above critical pressure the flow correction factor Kb (Table T7-1) must be applied; below it, Kb = 1.00. For liquids, conventional valves need no correction: Kw = 1.

Balanced Valves (Style JBS, Series BP) Van cân bằng (Style JBS, Series BP)

Because the effective area of the bellows or piston equals the seat area, the set pressure of a balanced valve is unaffected by back pressure and it opens within the ASME set-pressure tolerances. However, while open and relieving, back pressure gradually reduces the lifting force, so a flow correction factor for the maximum expected back pressure must be used in sizing.

For gases and vapors use the Kb factor from Figure F7-2 for Style JBS or Series BP valves. For liquids use the Kw factor from Figure F7-3. When back pressure conditions do not apply, Kb = Kw = 1.00. For back pressures beyond the Figure F7-2/F7-3 limits, consult your local Crosby representative.

Pilot Operated Valves Van vận hành pilot

Style JPV (snap-acting): back pressure does not affect set pressure or flow capacity except when flow is subcritical (ratio of absolute back pressure to absolute relieving pressure exceeds 55%); then Kb (Table T7-1) must be applied. Below 55%, Kb = 1.00. Style JPVM (modulating): set pressure and operability are unaffected by back pressure up to 70% of set pressure (use a backflow preventer if back pressure may exceed inlet pressure). Capacity is affected only when flow is subcritical (ratio exceeds 55%); then Kb (Table T7-1) applies, otherwise Kb = 1.00.

Capacity Correction Factor for High Pressure Steam, Kn Hệ số hiệu chỉnh hơi nước áp suất cao Kn

When the relieving pressure of steam exceeds 10,340 kPaa (1,500 psig), ASME requires an additional factor Kn for dry saturated steam. Figure F7-4 gives Kn versus relieving pressure. See Chapter 6 for its use in the steam sizing formula.

Capacity Correction Factor for Viscosity, Kv Hệ số hiệu chỉnh độ nhớt Kv

When sizing a valve for viscous liquid service, first size it as for nonviscous service to obtain a preliminary required effective area A. Select the next larger standard orifice and calculate the Reynolds number R from the formulas below, then obtain Kv from the graph (Figure F7-5) to correct the preliminary area. If the corrected area exceeds the chosen orifice area, repeat with the next larger orifice.

U.S.C.S. units:   R = 12,700 · GPM · G / √A   (GPM = flow at flowing temperature, gal/min; G = specific gravity referred to water = 1.00 at 70°F; A = effective discharge area, in²; U = viscosity at flowing temperature, Saybolt Universal Seconds, SSU).

Metric units:   R = 85,225 · Q · G / √A′   (Q = flow, L/min; A′ = effective discharge area, mm²; µ = absolute viscosity, centipoise).

Noise Level Calculations Tính toán mức tiếng ồn

The following formulas calculate the noise level of gases, vapors and steam discharged from a relief valve, derived from API Recommended Practice 521. Table T7-4 lists relative noise levels.

L100 = L + 10 log10 (0.29354 W k T/M)
  • L100 — sound level at 100 feet from the point of discharge, decibels.
  • L — noise intensity (sound pressure level) at 100 ft, from Figure F7-7.
  • W — maximum relieving capacity, lb/hr (k and M from Table T7-7; for steam k = 1.3, M = 18 if unknown).
  • T — absolute temperature at the valve inlet, degrees Rankine (°F + 460).
  • M — molecular weight (Table T7-7; for steam M = 18).

When the noise level is required at a distance other than 100 feet, use:

Lp = L100 − 20 log10 (r/100)

where r is the distance from the point of discharge, feet. The pressure ratio PR = absolute relieving pressure / absolute back pressure is used to obtain L from Figure F7-7.

Fire Conditions — Sizing for Vessels Containing Gases and Vapors Only Điều kiện hỏa hoạn — định cỡ bồn chứa khí/hơi (bồn khô)

The following method (API Recommended Practice 520, Fifth Edition) calculates the required effective discharge area for relief valves on vessels containing gases exposed to fire.

A = F′ A′ / √P1
F′ = 0.1406 (Tw − T1)1.25 / (C K T10.6506)
  • A — minimum required effective discharge area, in²; A′ — exposed surface area of the vessel, ft².
  • P1 — relieving pressure, psia.
  • Tw — vessel wall temperature, °R; API recommended maximum is 1100°F for carbon steel.
  • T1 = P1·Tn/Pn (Tn — normal operating gas temperature, °R; Pn — normal operating gas pressure absolute).
  • C — coefficient from page 7-9 or 7-26; K = 0.975. Recommended minimum F′ = 0.01; if unknown, use F′ = 0.045.

Two-Phase and Flashing Flow Dòng hai pha và dòng hóa hơi

Two-phase flow describes a stream containing both liquid and gas/vapor phases. Flashing flow occurs when, due to a pressure decrease, all or part of a liquid flow changes to vapor. Both conditions can occur simultaneously. The formulas provided for liquid, gas, vapor and steam may not be suitable for two-phase and flashing flow.

Work by DIERS (Design Institute for Emergency Relief Systems) and others has shown this subject is complex and no single universally accepted method handles all applications. When sizing for two-phase and flashing flow:

  • Estimate the increase in body-bowl pressure due to flashing, together with expected built-up back pressure.
  • A balanced valve (Crosby Style JBS bellows or Style JPVM pilot) may be necessary when the flashing-induced body-bowl pressure increase is excessive or unpredictable.
  • If the two-phase mixture at the valve inlet is 50% liquid or more by mass, use a liquid service valve construction; if the vapor content exceeds 50% by mass, use a valve designed for compressible fluid service.
