Appendix — ASME Section VIII, Division 1 Excerpts

Crosby Valve Inc. · Technical Document TP-V300

Chapter 8 · 24 source pages · 4 figures · companion notes
Key terms. MAWP — maximum allowable working pressure · PRV — pressure relief valve (reclosing) · nonreclosing devices — rupture disk, breaking pin · MDR — manufacturing design range · KD/K — measured discharge coefficient / design coefficient · C — gas constant as a function of k · blowdown — reclosure pressure reduction · telltale — indicator in the space between a rupture disk and its valve.

This appendix excerpts the essential paragraphs of ASME Section VIII, Division 1 (1992 Edition through the 1994 Addenda) governing pressure relief devices — paragraphs UG-125 through UG-136 — together with Appendix 11 (capacity conversions) and Appendix M (installation and operation). It gives the handbook's user direct insight into Code requirements. The ASME Code is revised annually and reissued every three years; Crosby recommends that the proper edition and revision be applied whenever the Code is used.

Scope. This is a structural condensation that keeps every mandatory number (overpressure allowances, tolerances, coefficients) — it does not replace the Code text itself.

UG-125 — General UG-125 — Quy định chung

(a) All pressure vessels within the scope of this Division, irrespective of size or pressure, shall be provided with protective devices per UG-125 through UG-136. The user is responsible for proper installation before initial operation; the devices need not be supplied by the vessel manufacturer. Definitions in Appendix I of ASME/ANSI PTC 25.3 apply. (b) An unfired steam boiler, per U-1(g), shall be equipped with pressure relief devices required by Section I.

(c) All vessels other than unfired steam boilers shall be protected so that pressure does not rise above the following allowances (see UG-134 for pressure settings):

  • Single device: ≤ MAWP + 10% or + 3 psi, whichever is greater;
  • Multiple devices set per UG-134(a): ≤ MAWP + 16% or + 4 psi;
  • Supplemental fire protection (UG-125(c)(2)): ≤ MAWP + 21%;
  • Nonrefrigerated liquefied compressed gas storage with no permanent supply connection (UG-125(c)(3)): fire-only devices are exempt from (c)(1)/(c)(2) provided pressure stays ≤ MAWP + 20%, set pressure ≤ MAWP, the vessel has sufficient ullage against liquid-full conditions, MAWP exceeds the vapor pressure of the stored gas at the maximum anticipated temperature (normally not less than 115°F), and the valves comply with UG-129(a)(5), UG-131(c)(2) and UG-134(d)(2). (Gas = substance with vapor pressure > 40 psia at 100°F.)

(d) Devices shall be accessible for inspection and repair, not readily rendered inoperative (see Appendix M), and selected for the intended service. (e) Pressure relief valves (reclosing) or nonreclosing devices may be used — the latter alone or in combination with safety or safety relief valves; rupture disk devices may be advisable where very rapid rates of pressure rise may occur, where the medium would render a valve inoperative, or where loss of valuable or noxious material must be avoided. (f) Vessels operating completely filled with liquid shall have liquid-service devices. (g) Where the pressure source is external and under positive control such that vessel pressure cannot exceed MAWP, the devices need not be mounted on the vessel — but pressure-reducing valves and similar control instruments are not considered positive in action (except pilot-operated valves per UG-126(b)). (h) Steam-service valves shall meet UG-131(b).

UG-126 — Pressure relief valves UG-126 — Van xả áp

(a) Safety, safety relief and relief valves shall be of the direct spring loaded type. (b) Pilot-operated valves may be used provided the pilot is self-actuated and the main valve opens automatically at not over the set pressure and discharges its full rated capacity if any essential part of the pilot fails. (c) The spring shall not be set more than ± 5% from the marked pressure unless within the manufacturer's spring design range; initial adjustment is performed by the manufacturer, its authorized representative or an assembler, with a data tag (set pressure, capacity, date) and a seal. (d) Set-pressure tolerance: ± 2 psi up to and including 70 psi, ± 3% above 70 psi.

UG-127 — Nonreclosing pressure relief devices UG-127 — Thiết bị xả không đóng lại

Rupture disk devices Đĩa nứt (rupture disk)

Every disk carries a stamped burst pressure established within the manufacturing design range (MDR) at the specified disk temperature, and a lot number. Burst tolerance: + 2 psi up to and including 40 psi, + 5% above 40 psi. The stamped pressure is established by bursting: (1) at least two sample disks per lot (one at room temperature), rating = average burst at coincident temperature; (2) at least four disks (≥ 5% of the lot) at four temperatures to build a burst-pressure–temperature curve, then interpolating; or (3) for prebulged solid-metal or graphite disks, a percent-ratio curve from one size per material lot plus two room-temperature samples. All lot tests use a holder of the same form and dimensions as the installed disk.

Capacity rating: not more than the applicable theoretical formula (UG-131) multiplied by K = 0.62; area A is the minimum net area after complete burst (never exceeding the nominal pipe size area). A manufacturer may instead have capacity certified per UG-131. Application: a disk may be the sole relieving device; or installed between the valve and the vessel provided (i) the combination is ample per UG-133, (ii) the stamped capacity of a nozzle-type valve is multiplied by a factor of 0.90 (or the combination is tested per UG-132), (iii) the space between disk and valve carries a pressure gage, try cock, free vent or telltale to detect burst or leakage, and (iv) the post-burst opening passes the valve capacity and is at least the valve inlet area. On the valve outlet side: the valve must open at its set pressure regardless of accumulated back pressure (space vented/drained), the valve capacity is ample, disk burst pressure plus outlet piping pressure stays within the outlet design pressure and never above the vessel MAWP or the valve set pressure, contents are clean and free of gumming matter, and the bonnet is vented.

Breaking pin devices Chốt gãy (breaking pin)

Breaking pins are used only in combination with a safety or safety relief valve, never alone. The space between pin and valve carries a pressure gage, try cock, free vent or telltale. Each pin has a rated pressure and temperature, a lot number, and a break guarantee within these tolerances:

Spring-loaded nonreclosing devices Thiết bị không đóng lại tải lò xo

Opens at the set pressure and remains open until manually reset; if the actuating means fail, full opening occurs at or below set pressure. It may not be combined with any other pressure relief device. Opening tolerance ≤ + 5%. Capacity ≤ theoretical value × K = 0.62 (A = flow area through the minimum opening), or certified per UG-131.

UG-128 & UG-129 — Liquid relief valves; marking UG-128 & UG-129 — Van xả lỏng; ký hiệu đánh dấu

UG-128: any liquid relief valve shall be at least NPS ½. UG-129: each valve (including the main valve of a pilot-operated valve) NPS ½ and larger is marked so as not to be obliterated in service with: (1) the name of the manufacturer and the assembler; (2) design or type number; (3) NPS inlet size; (4) set pressure; (5) certified capacity — lb/hr saturated steam, or gal/min water at 70°F, or SCFM/lb/min air (60°F, 14.7 psia) — all at 10% or 3-psi overpressure whichever is greater; valves certified per UG-131(c)(2) are marked “at 20% overpressure”; (6) year built or a coding; (7) the ASME symbol of Fig. UG-129. Valves smaller than NPS ½ may carry a metal tag. Valves bearing the Section I symbol are usable on vessels per (b).

Valves combined with rupture disks (c) additionally carry the 0.90-factor capacity or the tested combination capacity factor; breaking-pin combinations (d) mark the rated pressure on the pin and its housing. Rupture disks (e) are marked on the flange or a metal tab: manufacturer's name, type number, lot number, disk material, NPS size, stamped burst pressure, coincident disk temperature, and capacity (lb/hr saturated steam or ft³/min air); items (1), (2) and (5) also go on the holder. Spring-loaded nonreclosing devices (f) are marked per (a), with the Code Symbol applied only when capacity is certified per UG-127(c)(3).

UG-131 — Capacity certification UG-131 — Chứng nhận công suất

(a)–(c) Before the Code Symbol is applied, capacity must be certified. Compressible-fluid tests use dry saturated steam (98% minimum quality, 20°F maximum superheat), air, or natural gas; incompressible-fluid tests use water at 40–125°F. Test pressure does not exceed set pressure by more than 10% or 3 psi (except valves for UG-125(c)(3): up to 120% of stamped set pressure); the minimum test pressure is 3 psi above set; reseating pressure is recorded. Valves with adjustable blowdown are adjusted to ≤ 5% of set pressure or 3 psi before testing. Pilot-operated valves may be certified without the pilot if testing demonstrates that the pilot opens the main valve fully at not more than set pressure + 10% or 3 psi.

(d) Per size–design–setting: three valves per set, each within ± 5% of the average; stamped capacity ≤ 90% of the average. For compressible fluids the slope W/P of each test point is averaged; all values must fall within ± 5% of the average slope (otherwise up to four more valves are tested); the stamped capacity does not exceed 0.90 × average slope × absolute accumulation pressure [1.10 × set + 14.7 or set + 3 + 14.7, whichever is greater; valves per (c)(2) use 1.20]. For incompressible fluids, capacity versus differential pressure is plotted log–log and a straight line drawn through the four points (a point departing > 5% is unsatisfactory — two more valves per bad point); stamped capacity ≤ 90% of the line value.

(e) Design coefficient of discharge K: at least nine valves (three sizes at three set pressures) are submitted; each test run yields KD = measured actual flow / theoretical flow computed from:

(UG-131(e)(2) — hơi bão hòa khô)WT = 51.5 A P
(UG-131(e)(2) — khí)WT = 356 A P √M/T
(UG-131(e)(2) — khí/h)WT = C A P √M/(Z T)
(UG-131(e)(2) — n)WT = 2407 A √(P − Pd) w

where A is the actual discharge area at developed lift; P = (set × 1.10) + atmospheric, or set + 3 psi + atmospheric, whichever is greater; Pd is the discharge pressure; M the molecular weight; T the absolute inlet temperature (°F + 460); C the constant for k = cp/cv; Z the compressibility factor; w the specific weight of water at inlet conditions. For dry saturated steam above 1500 psig up to 3200 psig, WT is corrected by (0.1906P − 1000)/(0.2292P − 1061).

(UG-131(e)(2))KD = actual flow / theoretical flow
(UG-131(e)(2))K = 0.90 × KD,avg ≤ 0.878

All experimental KD values must fall within ± 5% of the average; water capacity converts to gal/min by × 1/500. K applies to valves with beta ratio (throat/inlet) 0.15–0.75 unless tests demonstrate otherwise; lift-determined-area designs share one nominal lift-to-seat ratio (L/D).

(f)–(g) Tests are conducted at facilities meeting ASME/ANSI PTC 25.3 under an Authorized Observer accepted by ASME; test data reports go to the ASME designee (valve capacities are published in the National Board's “Pressure Relief Device Certifications”). Design changes require retesting. (h) Safety valves at absolute pressures up to 1500 psia may be rated per PG-69.1.2 at a 103% flow pressure and raised to the 110% permitted flow pressure by the multiplier:

(UG-131(h) — 103% → 110% rating multiplier)1.10 p + 14.71.03 p + 14.7

This multiplier must not be used as a divisor; above 1500 psig it does not apply. (i) Nozzle-type valves may be rated for saturated water per 11-2. (j) Any design change affecting flow path, lift or performance requires new tests.

UG-132 — Certification of combinations with nonreclosing devices UG-132 — Chứng nhận tổ hợp với thiết bị không đóng lại

Rupture disk at the inlet: test the smallest disk device size with the equivalent-size valve carrying the largest orifice of that inlet size, on saturated steam, air or natural gas; the stamped burst pressure is 90% to 100% of the valve set pressure. Procedure: test one valve alone at 10% overpressure, then with a disk installed ahead of it and burst, again at 10%; repeat with three disks total — results within ± 10% of the average, otherwise retest to find the cause. The Combination Capacity Factor = average combination capacity / individual valve capacity multiplies the ASME rated capacity of the valve design in all sizes; it shall not exceed one and applies only to the tested designs of valve and disk. Optional tests may extend the factor to the next two larger sizes and to higher pressures (factor ≤ 1). Breaking-pin combinations are capacity tested per UG-131(d) or (e) as a combination.

UG-133 & UG-134 — Relieving requirements and pressure setting UG-133 & UG-134 — Nhu cầu xả và áp suất thiết lập

UG-133: the aggregate capacity of the devices on a vessel (or system of vessels) shall carry off the maximum quantity that can be generated or supplied without permitting more than a 16% rise above MAWP while blowing; supplemental fire protection holds the rise to 21%. Vessels interconnected by adequate piping without isolating valves count as one unit. Heat exchangers and similar vessels get devices sized for internal failure. The official rated capacity is the stamped capacity. Non-steam/air ratings convert per Appendix 11. To prorate to a relieving pressure above 1.10p, multiply by:

(UG-133(g) — capacity prorating multiplier)P + 14.71.10 p + 14.7

P = relieving pressure (psig), p = set pressure (psig); not applicable to steam above 1500 psig. UG-134: a single device is set at ≤ MAWP; with multiple devices only one need be at ≤ MAWP and the others at ≤ 105% of MAWP; fire-protection devices ≤ 110% of MAWP (if serving both roles, ≤ MAWP). Set pressure includes static head and constant back pressure. Set tolerance: ± 2 psi (≤ 70 psi) or ± 3% (> 70 psi); valves per UG-125(c)(3): −0%, +10%.

UG-135 — Installation UG-135 — Lắp đặt

Vapor-service devices connect to the vapor space above the contained liquid; liquid-service devices below normal liquid level. All pipe and fittings between vessel and device have at least the area of the device inlet, with direct, unobstructed flow (Appendix M). Two or more devices on one connection: the connection's internal cross-sectional area is at least the combined inlet areas. No intervening stop valves between vessel and protective devices or between devices and the point of discharge, except (1) when closing the maximum number of block valves still leaves the required relieving capacity, or (2) under Appendix M conditions. Discharge lines lead to a safe place, drain freely, and are sized so that any pressure that develops will not reduce relieving capacity or adversely affect operation:

UG-136 — Minimum requirements for pressure relief valves UG-136 — Yêu cầu tối thiểu cho van xả áp

(a) Mechanical (a) Cơ khí

  • (1) Guiding arrangements ensure consistent operation and tightness.
  • (2) Spring: full-lift compression ≤ 80% of nominal solid deflection; permanent set ≤ 0.5% of free height (measured 10 min after the third solid compression).
  • (3) Valves on air, water over 140°F, or steam carry a substantial lifting device releasing the seat force at ≥ 75% of set pressure; pilot-operated valves get a lifting device or pilot pressurization means to verify free movement.
  • (4) The seat is fastened to the body so it cannot lift. (5) Body design minimizes deposit effects.
  • (6) Screwed-connection valves have wrenching surfaces. (7) All initial adjustments are sealed; the seal identifies the adjuster (valves above NPS ½).
  • (8) A drain is provided at the lowest point where liquid can collect on the discharge side of the disk.
  • (9) Diaphragm-type valves: the space above the diaphragm is vented; diaphragm failure must not impair rated relieving capacity.

(b) Materials (b) Vật liệu

Cast iron seats and disks are not permitted. Adjacent sliding surfaces (guides, disks, disk holders) are corrosion resistant; springs are corrosion resistant or coated. Body, bonnet and yoke materials are Section II listed; carbon and low-alloy steel bodies, bonnets, yokes and bolting below −20°F meet UCS-66 unless the coincident ratio of Fig. UCS-66.1 is ≤ 0.4 or impact testing is exempted. Nozzle, disk and internal-part materials come from Section II, ASTM specifications, or controlled by the valve manufacturer.

(c)–(f) Production testing, inspection, design, welding (c)–(f) Thử sản xuất, kiểm tra, thiết kế, hàn

Each valve is pressure tested (steam valves tested with steam for sufficient time) and seat-tightness tested. The manufacturer/assembler demonstrates that random production samples perform like the capacity-certified valves; manufacturing, assembly, inspection and test operations are subject to ASME inspection at any time. UV Code Symbol permission runs in 5-year cycles: two sample production valves are selected and operationally and capacity tested at an accepted laboratory; adjustable blowdown is set ≤ 7% of set pressure or 3 psi; failure of replacement valves revokes the authorization within 60 days. Assemblers use original, unmodified parts; marking adds the manufacturer and assembler names, with the assembler's Code Symbol. Design requirements are submitted with detailed drawings; all welding follows this Division.

ASME Appendix 11 — Capacity conversions ASME Phụ lục 11 — Quy đổi công suất

11-1. The capacity of a safety valve in terms of a fluid other than the officially rated medium follows: steam Ws = 51.5 KAP; air Wa = CKAP √(M/T) with C = 356, M = 28.97, T = 520; any gas or vapor W = CKAP √(M/T). Where the k of hydrocarbon vapors is unknown, the conservative k = 1.001 (C = 315) is commonly used:

(Appendix 11 — hydrocarbons, k = 1.001)W = 315 K A P √M/T

where K is the discharge coefficient per UG-131(d)/(e), A the actual discharge area (sq in), P = (set × 1.10) + atmospheric (psia). The formulas also work in reverse — from a required flow to the equivalent steam or air rating.

Example 2. Relieve 5000 lb/hr propane at 125°F inlet, k unknown → use C = 315: KAP = 5000 √(44.09/585)/315 = 57.7; equivalent steam Ws = 51.5 × 57.7 = 2970 lb/hr. Example 3. Relieve 1000 lb/hr ammonia at 150°F with k = 1.33 → C = 350: KAP = 17.10; Ws = 51.5 × 17.10 = 880 lb/hr steam. Example 4. A valve rated 10,000 cu ft/min air (60°F, 14.7 psia) = 45,960 lb/hr; KAP = 546; Ws = 51.5 × 546 = 28,200 lb/hr steam at the same setting (UG-131(b) must be met before converting to steam).

11-2 — Saturated water. Because saturated-water capacity is configuration sensitive, the rating applies only to nozzle-type valves (throat-to-inlet ratio 0.25–0.80 with continuously contoured change, KD above 0.90). Reading method: enter the chart at the valve set pressure, move vertically to the saturated water line, and read horizontally the relieving capacity — a theoretical isentropic value assuming equilibrium flow and calculated critical pressure ratio. If after initial relief the medium becomes quality steam, the valve is rated on dry saturated steam; valves on steam–water mixture service are rated on dry saturated steam.

ASME Appendix M — Installation and operation ASME Phụ lục M — Lắp đặt và vận hành

M-1. These rules are general information only: installation and operation are the responsibility of the jurisdictions enforcing Section VIII. M-2. Vessels subject to external corrosion get inspection access; manholes and cover plates stay accessible; the dished bottom head of vertical cylindrical vessels is preferably concave to pressure for complete drainage. M-3. Vessel markings remain accessible after installation, not covered by insulation. M-4. Device installation is fully covered by UG-135; the paragraphs below detail stop valves and piping.

M-5 — Inlet stop valves: for inspection and repair only, arranged to be locked or sealed open, closed only by an authorized person who remains stationed there while it stays closed and re-locks it open before leaving. A vessel fed exclusively from an outside source may have a per-vessel stop valve that need not be locked open, provided it also closes that vessel off from the pressure source. M-6 — Discharge-side stop valves: permitted where the discharge joins a common header so closing prevents backflow from operating vessels; locked or sealed in either position only by an authorized person; when closed in operation, an authorized person remains present and the inlet stop valve is closed first.

M-7 — Inlet pressure drop: piping, valves, fittings and vessel components up to the valve are at least the device inlet size; the cumulative non-recoverable inlet loss must not exceed 3% of set pressure, based on nameplate capacity corrected for the fluid. Multiple valves on one connection: inlet area ≥ combined areas, verified with all valves relieving. M-8 — Discharge lines: prefer short pipes or vertical risers through long-radius elbows blowing to atmosphere, at least the valve outlet size; telescopic (“broken”) discharge lines with drip pans are recommended; common headers serving simultaneously-relieving devices are sized on the total outlet areas with downstream pressure-drop allowance; static pressure at the discharge flange of a conventional direct-spring valve must not exceed 10% of set pressure at stamped capacity; provide drainage everywhere. M-9. Nonreclosing relieving systems are sized so vessel pressure never exceeds allowable overpressure. M-10. Back-pressure effects of common-header discharge differ per valve type — consult manufacturer data.

M-11 — Pressure differentials: consider start-up/shutdown, upsets, instrument response, surge from quick-closing valves; toxic, corrosive or valuable fluids warrant larger differentials. Blowdown is the first consideration: a valve set at 100 psig with 7% blowdown closes at 93 psig — operating pressure must stay below that; large spring-valve blowdown cannot be considered accurate unless verified on the actual installation. Pilot-operated valves can be set to blowdowns as short as 2%, but the main valve may lag the pilot; pilot-to-main signal piping must not drop excessive pressure. Avoid pilot valves in abrasive or dirty service, where coking, polymerization or corrosion of wetted pilot parts can occur, or where the lading fluid can freeze or condense. Tightness should be specified to comply no lower than the normal operating pressure (procedure per ANSI B146.1) and never treated as permanent. General recommendations:

Small-seat valves need extra maintenance as the differential approaches these values. M-12. Spring-loaded valves normally install upright (vertical spindle); other positions are acceptable if the design suits, the medium will not accumulate at the inlet, and discharge drainage is adequate. M-13. Reaction thrust and externally applied loads (thermal expansion, unsupported piping weight) create bending moments — compute them in installation design. M-14. Fire sizing references: API RP 520 Part I and API Standard 2000, AAR M-1002, CGA S-1.1/S-1.2/S-1.3, NFPA Codes 30/59/59A, and the Phillips Petroleum fire-test studies. M-15. A pressure gauge (if provided) spans the set pressure with an upper limit of not less than 1.25 × device set pressure and not more than 2 × vessel MAWP.
