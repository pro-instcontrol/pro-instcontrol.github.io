This chapter presents the recording and display systems used in industrial instrumentation: self-balancing potentiometric recorders, circular, strip and fanfold charts, magnetic and digital recording, X-Y plotters, programmable and paperless recorders, and the LED, LCD, Nixie, liquid-vapour and electrophoretic display technologies, followed by an eleven-question exercise.

7.1 Recorders

7.1.1 Strip Chart, Magnetic and Digital

An indicating instrument having facility for continuous recording of a process variable is known as a 'recorder'. This is quite different from a usual tape-recorder used in the music industry. Recording instruments record continuously the variation in the value of variables which are required to be observed for a definite period of time.

7.1.2 Description of the System

Continuous recording system has passed through several phases of its development. Earlier deflection or moving coil type recording systems were in use which operated on the basic principle of permanent magnet moving coil instrument. These recorders, however, are still in use in some of the older process plants, but limitations imposed on this design due to inferior input impedance and poor sensitivity are making them obsolete for coming process plants.

Potentiometric recorder, which is basically a null type system, improves overall performance of the recording system. Basic operation is similar to self-balancing potentiometer as shown in Figure 7.1. The unknown potential which is proportional to a measured process variable is compared with respect to the standard potential available across the potentiometer. The resulting out of balance potential is converted, amplified and used to balance the potentiometer to a new setting by means of the mechanical linkage and wiper running on the potentiometer. The amplifier is a two-stage amplifier (preamplifier and servo-amplifier) to have a higher-level signal to operate the servomotor. Further details of this recorder are available in Chapter 3 of this book.

The chart of the recorder being an important part of the system is made up of fine quality, chemically quoted paper especially fabricated for recording of process or other variables.

The chart is of mainly three types:

  1. Circular chart
  2. Strip chart
  3. Fanfold chard.

While the basic principle of actuation of the recorder remains the same the operational construction of recording mechanism changes according to the type of chart.

(i) Circular chart

As the name suggests, the chart is circular in shape as shown in Figure 7.2. The scale is marked from 0 to 100 linear for recording of linear variables, e.g. pressure, level, etc. and 0 to 10 square root for recording of non-linear variable of Flow (Flow ∝ √Differential Pressure). The scale of the chart starts from the centre (0 mark) and ends near the periphery (100 or 10 marking) of the chart.

The recording pen arm is connected to the process variable indicating mechanism and the tip of the pen rests gently (against the action of a light spring) on the chart. The pen is filled with slow drying ink of higher quality. The entire recording system is run by a chart drive motor. The speed of this chart drive motor is very low. It has to complete one full revolution in 24 hours time. The chart is marked from 8 A.M. of a particular day to 8 A.M. next day, i.e. 24 hours. This means the chart has to be replaced after every 24 hours. This is the greatest disadvantage of circular chart system and therefore has been more or less obsolete nowadays even then a brief description on circular chart recorder system is mentioned here for academic interest.

The measuring assembly is connected mechanically to the pen mounting arrangement so that change in measurement input can be indicated by the pen arm as shown in Figure 7.3. The chart is fixed on a supporting flat plate and is secured properly by a spring clip. The complete recorder mechanism is operated by means of a combination of three levers attached A, B and C which actually transmit the motion generated by the measurement input to the recorder system.

(ii) Strip chart recorder

Strip chart recording system is completely a different type of graphic recording system. In this system, a long roll of scale-printed chart paper is allowed to rotate continuously. In one design of recorder of size 6″ × 3″, the chart is installed vertically and the rotation is effected horizontally as shown in Figure 7.4. In the other design of recorder of larger sizes (potentiometer recorders) as shown in Figure 7.5, the chart is installed horizontally and the rotation is effected vertically. The chart is moved with the help of two rollers and a drive gear system connected to a chart drive motor as shown in the diagrams. One of the rollers is called a dummy roller which is used only for loading the fresh chart. The second roller, the active roller, is used to wind the used part of the chart after recording or printing is over.

In the vertical installation of the chart, the marking is dual, i.e. one linear (0 to 100) and the other one square root (0 to 10). This is true for pneumatic type of recorder. In the microprocessor based recorder where linearisation is a part of signal conditioning system, the square root or any other non-linear marking is not required. The pen is filled with slow drying ink supplied by a small ink reservoir as shown in the diagram.

In another design of ink-pad combination system, a small felt pen supplied with fixed amount of disposable ink is used.

In yet another design which is applied in the case of multi-point null type of potentiometric recorder having dot printing mechanism, an ink-pad of six different colours are used. This ink-pad is of circular cross-section having six small, bucket-like shape (casted inside) and is filled with felt which is full of ink. The ink is completely absorbed by the felt and is ready to be used. A six bucket felt ink-pad has been shown in Figure 7.6.

The pen used for this ink-pad is of peculiar construction as shown in Figure 7.7. This is a circular pen-tip and number of points are impregnated on the surface having pointed teeth-like shape and put on the periphery of the circle. The chart drive motor for 6″ × 3″ recorder is having speed of 1/3 revolution in one hour whereas for larger size potentiometric recorder the speed of the chart drive motor is switch selectable.

(iii) Fanfold chart

In this type, the chart paper is folded according to a designed shape for both

the sizes of recorder described earlier and arranged systematically one fold on the other and pressed hard to give the perfect shape. The installation of such chart is easy. It is placed physically on the section of the recorder meant for it. Grooves are punched on both the extreme corners of the chart paper which are used for fixing the corners of the chart on the roller teeth as shown in Figure 7.8. The roller is connected to the chart drive motor through gear system. When recording starts, chart moves through the teeth of the roller and unwinds on other section of the recorder after printing.

7.1.3 Magnetic Recording System

Whereas the recorders described above are of physical motion drive recorder, the magnetic recording system utilizes the magnetic property of magnetic material. A thin sheet of plastic tape of sufficient width is coated with magnetic material preferably with oxides of iron. The complete reel of this plastic tape is moved linearly between the two rollers. While moving, it passes across a magnetization head that puts a residual magnetic dot upon the magnetic surface. The input signal which has to be recorded is amplified by a write/record amplifier. This signal when applied to the write head creates a change in magnetization of the magnetic particles on the tape. For reading the signal content, on the other hand, the read head captures reluctance-change of its magnetic circuitry and induces a proportional voltage in the winding located on the read head. Since the induced voltage is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux, the system cannot work on D.C. The read head amplifier amplifies the read signal and gives output. The system has been shown in block diagram representation in Figure 7.9. This system can work between frequency range 100 Hz and 2 Mega Hz.

There is two great usefulness of this recording system. One is that it can be used to record spectrum of vibration information of high-speed machines contained in between the relation of frequency of vibration and amplitude of vibration. Another important usefulness is that it can record audio signals quite efficiently.

Frequency Modulation Recording System

This system is also essentially a magnetic system but modulation allows the lower frequency range of operation to extend upto D.C. as well. Control frequency fc is chosen in comparison to tape speed and the deviation expected for the tape is ± 40% around the carrier frequency. Other things in the system remains the same as write head or read head, etc. First of all carrier frequency fc generated by an oscillator is modulated according to the input signal level. When the input signal is completely absent (zero input range), modulation shall be at the control frequency. If the input signal is D.C., the modulated output shall have constant frequency but for an A.C. input signal the modulated output shall have variable frequency range. The demodulator, on the other hand, converts the differential frequency of the central and that of the tape to a voltage which is proportional to the differential frequency.

7.1.4 Digital Magnetic Recording System

Digital magnetic recording system has been originally designed for computer digital data processing works. Now since most of the instrumentation systems are digital data based or computer based, this recording system is being used for recording of instrumentation digital signal as well. In this system of recording, a tape is kept under complete magnetic saturation in either direction (positive or negative). The digital data in character form is coded on the tape in the right track in combination of 1-bit. Industry standard IBM designed recording format is used for recording of instrumentation related digital data. This format is known as NRZ (Non-Return Zero). The NRZ uses the change of direction of flux on the particular location on the tape for indicating 1-bit and in opposite to that a constant flux for indicating 0 bit. Based on this format, a binary number 11101001 has been coded by the flux pattern of NRZ system in Figure 7.10.

Digital recording on tapes functions in two ways, one in incremental way and the other in synchronous way. In incremental form of recording, the system increments each step after recording one character at a time. In this way if the data is in discontinuous form or even slow in arrival speed, the system places each character along the recording tape in equal space.

In synchronous form of recording, on the other hand, the recorder tape is allowed to move at a constant speed and data is recorded in blocks. Two blocks are separated by a gap (empty space). In this way large blocks of data are recorded within no time because the recorder tape takes instant start and is stopped also instantly.

The most important facts about magnetic recording are that the problems of distortion and non-linearity are completely absent here. This is because the magnetic recording needs only correct polarity of current for correct magnetization. Two kinds of error that can be expected from magnetic recording system are loss of a pulse and addition of a spurious pulse in the normal data block. To have a check on these types of error parity bit checker system is used.

7.2 X-Y PLOTTER

In addition to research and development work, process industries also need to have record of variable in the form of Y = f (x). The combined plot of two-time dependent variables is called an X-Y plot. For example, in feedforward control system, water flow-steam flow plot is an X-Y plot. Similarly, in vibration monitoring system of high-speed machines, the combined plot of vibration frequency-vibration amplitude is another example of an X-Y plot. These plots give direct variation with respect to one another and is an important parameter for higher level of analysis and design decisions.

In one such design of X-Y plotter of Analog type, the writting arm and pen assembly is directed to move linearly in X-direction on a fixed graph-like chart paper. The chart is held in its proper place electrostatically or under the influence of vacuum. There are two types of movement of the assembly. The arm along with the pen moves in X-direction and is controlled by the servomotor with respect to X-input variable. The other movement of the system is in Y-direction where the complete assembly upon which the arm and pen are installed. This movement is controlled by another servomotor with respect to Y-input variable. Thus, the combined movement gives X-Y plot, i.e. the plot of Y with respect to X of the system as shown in the block diagram in Figure 7.11.

Both the input after proper conditioning are fed to their respective balancing circuits where an error voltage is generated on comparison of input signal with respect to the standard supply fed separately to the balancing circuit as shown. The output signals obtained from balancing system are converted to A.C. and then after amplification are fed to the respective servomotors which ultimately control the recorder functioning and balancing of the system as well. The respective input are recorded on the chart paper simultaneously as long as there are variations in the inputs and thus a record of one variable with respective to another variable is obtained. The accuracy and sensitivity of X-Y plotter are ± 0.3% and 10 microvolt/millimetre respectively.

This recorder is utilized for obtaining of stress-strain relationship as well while testing strength of material in mechanical laboratory. Also this is utilized for obtaining a graph of change in resistance versus change in temperature in instrumentation laboratory or even for obtaining a graph of change in milli-volt output of thermocouple with respect to change in temperature.

7.2.1 Multifunction Programmable Recorder

Conventional industrial recorders since their inception have been used primarily for monitoring and recording process data obtained from the field transducers.

In order to extract more information that may otherwise prove to be critical and valuable, data recorders need to be intelligent, should have advanced information processing capabilities and should support appropriate data communication protocols. This requires seamless data networking amongst various instrumentation sites, e.g. field (where transducers are installed), local control station (where I/O termination points are located), central control room (where recorders and control generation instruments as well as PC-based operator stations are installed) and the various MIS (Management Information System) offices where management decisions are taken.

Data recording system allows accessing of records from anywhere in the world through internet. The type of recording can be either on ink-pen-chart recording or a paperless recording. Both the recorders are capable of displaying real time trend, instantaneous value in digital form, trend in bar-graph form. In addition to these, these recorders display data, time, channel number, alarm points status, etc. A complete system has been shown in Figure 7.12.

7.2.2 Modern Ink-Pen-Chart Recorder

In comparison to other forms of ink-pen-chart recorders, the modern recorders have many special features. In addition to provide simultaneous analog and digital displays, it can be programmed for any input (universal input ranging) and for any four-alarm generating settings out of six being available in the system (low, high, very low, very high, low rate of change and high rate of change). Memory cards are attached to save calibration data and other set-up data entered into the system. Real time mathematical operations also are done for any related calculations and results are recorded at programmed intervals or are communicated to described locations through network connectivity.

Most of the special features of a modern recorder are the especialty of the manufacturers, based on their ASIC (application specific integrated circuits) technology. For example, the amplifier unit with gain programming facilities and analog-to-digital converter are combined together on a single chip. Also the slide-wire contacts are replaced by ultrasonic pen position sensing device. In this system, the position of recorder pen is sensed through ultrasonic pulse timing system. A piezoelectric transducer through a magnetostrictive element do the job as shown in Figure 7.13 in a simplified way.

Another feature is the design of brushless D.C. servomotor. Sliding of pen carriage continuously creates mechanical wear resulting in servomotor failure. In the brushless design, however, the coils are fixed and the magnets are allowed to turn and switching of current through the coil is carried out electronically whereas the required current supply is done through brushes in conventional recorders.

In modern recorder scanning of input for recording is carried out through especially designed semiconductor relay. Scanning of input for a multi-point recorder system really needs techniques. In conventional recorder, it is done through signal selector microswitch operated continuously by a signal selector motor. When this micro-switch is operated upon by a particular input, entire dotting and printing mechanism is energized resulting in recording of that particular input. In modern recorder, however, application specific high voltage solid-state scanner is used. A photo coupler having output in voltage form and a high voltage operated MOSFET (a few kilovolt and leakage current of the order of a few nanoampere) combine together to work as input scanner as shown in Figure 7.14 in a simplified way.

7.2.3 Paperless Recording System

The paperless recording systems do not use chart. In fact the entire records are available on the monitor screen. The input channels are passed through usual signal conditioners including amplifiers and analog-to-digital converters and then are recorded on memory bank. The system is controlled by recording control circuit as shown in Figure 7.15. The entire memory bank are used for multi-channel input recording. The analog inputs usually in the form of voltage are sampled at very high sampling rate to match the interval of waveshape of the process variables to be recorded. The waveshape of the individual process variables sampled in this way is stored as it is in the memory bank and then can be copied to other instruments. An output controlling device is added to the system to select the type of output (analog or digital) through appropriate converter.

Monitor and the connected communication systems have not been shown on the diagram. The monitor is a conventional monitor having capability of creating realistic images and graphics with the help of especially designed software packages for the purpose. To construct the monitoring screen a builder softwave (which runs on Microsoft Power Point) can be easily used. The runtime software, on the other hand, shall display all the graphics and others when it is started. Ethernet connectors are used for this system easily.

7.3 DIGITAL DATA DISPLAY DEVICES

7.3.1 Introduction

We are aware that a closed instrumentation system is consisting of essentially measurement devices, display devices and control devices as indicated in the block diagram presentation of Figure 7.16. The scope of display devices is fairly large, however, in the present discussion we are concerned with digital display systems only. That means other forms of display systems such as analog indicators, recorders shall remain out of discussion for the time being. In this discussion, however, we have already seen the versatile display device and a great number of different recording systems.

During manufacturing of LED, the N-type layer is in fact grown upon the substrate. On the other hand, the P-type layer is deposited upon the N-type layer by diffusion method. The metallic connection on the anode is made on small region. This allows more light to escape out of the surface. In contrast a large cathode made of preferably gold film is capable of providing maximum possible reflecting area. The complete unit is sealed in an enclosure for protection. This type of design has been shown in Figure 7.17.

Another typical construction has been shown in Figure 7.18(a) in which phosphor layer is used as coating on the LED surface to increase the visibility. Figure 7.18(a) shows the internal construction of such LED and its representative symbol is on figure 7.18(b).

The most popular LED based display system is the seven-segment display system. This is because minimum seven LEDs are required to cover all the digits from 0 to 9. These are numbered as a, b, c, d, e, f and g as shown in Figure 7.19.

The diagram shows complete scheme of seven-segment display unit in common anode configuration style. The seven number of switches connected through resistors create different digits by closing themselves according to the input digits. For example, to indicate 0, all switches should close except g and to indicate 9 all switches should close except, e. Other digits follows similar pattern. The above configuration can be changed to common cathode configuration also.

Another digital data display based on LEDs is called Dot Matrix Displays. Several dots are arranged in a matrix format and a LED is connected on every dot. This means every dot represents one LED for lighting that particular dot. The matrix arrangement of dots of this kind can be of several combination, e.g. 5 and 7 or 5 and 8 or 7 and 9. A combination of 5 and 7 dot matrix with LED installed on each dot has been shown in Figure 7.20. Electrical connection to this arrangement can be done in three different ways:

  • (a) Common cathode connection,
  • (b) Common anode connection, and
  • (c) XY array connection.

The XY array connection being economical and more popular has been used in the diagram in figure. For displaying 4 the LEDs which are required to be in ON state has been shown in Figure 7.21.

The common anode or cathode connection for dot matrix display is not economical because number of wires are tremendously increased and connections become complicated.

A third type of display can be made out of LEDs called Bar Graph Displays which is exactly a digital display, but are used on process controllers for indication of process values and set values. In this, LEDs are allowed to be ON one-by-one starting from 0 upto 10 for indicating the values in bar graph shape as shown in Figure 7.22. The LEDs used in this are of dot shape and are very small (miniature LEDs). These LEDs are independently driven and are arranged linearly and closely for indicating the values in vertical way or horizontal way. For indicating the values in this display system analog signal is normally employed.

Advantages of LED

  • (i) It is very small in size, therefore, very useful in fabrication of miniature instrument.
  • (ii) Since it is a low voltage (upto 2 volt) and low current (upto 20 millampere) operated device, the light output can be very effectively controlled by controlling the current.
  • (iii) LED is a fast operating device in comparison to other indicating device. It operates within a nanosecond.
  • (iv) LED produced coloured light is rugged in constructional feature and have very long life if operated within norms.
  • (v) Its operating temperature ranges from 0 to 70C.

Disadvantages

  • (i) LED is very sensitive to over-voltage, over-current and higher temperature.
  • (ii) In strong ambient light, LED becomes invisible.

(ii) LCD-based display

Liquid crystal is a peculiar material having crystalline structure like solids and at the same time having characteristics of fluidity like a liquid. They do not emit light like LED but modulate the light when signal in electrical form is applied to them. These liquid crystals are made of organic molecules having elliptical shape of comparatively smaller minor axis as shown in Figure 7.23.

The molecular arrangement of the liquid crystal, define three different types of these crystals. Out of these three only two are useful for fabricating the display system. The SMECTIC crystal structure is systematically layered to one another but is of no use for display system. The other two, i.e. NEMATIC and the CHOLESTERIC structures though are not layered systematically but are useful for display system.

Nematic liquid crystal is more popular in fabricating display devices. Normally this crystal is transparent but when electric field is applied, the crystal structures get disturbed and the crystal become opaque. Liquid crystal displays are of two types, one is dynamic scattering type and another is field effect type.

7.3.2 Dynamic Scattering Type LCD

The nematic liquid crystal is the most popularly known dynamic scattering type LCD. In this a thin layer usually of a few microns of the crystal is placed between the two glass plates. One glass is transparent and the other one is reflective like a plane mirror. The glass plates are equipped with required electrodes for electrical connections. The conducting layer is made of tin oxide coating. The indicating surface with oxide coating is etched to produce pattern of digits which are effectively insulated to each other. Figure 7.26 shows the crystal when no voltage is applied. In this condition, the light passes completely through the system and behaves as transparent object as indicated.

When a voltage (between 6 and 20 volt) is applied across the metal oxide coating as shown in Figure 7.27, the usual structure of the crystal get disturbed. Because new areas are formed having varying refractive index in between the conducting surfaces of the system. The normal light after being incident upon the surface is scattered in different directions at the interface between the areas of varying refractive index. Scattering of light in this way is called dynamic scattering.

7.3.3 Field Effect Type LCD

In field effect type of LCD, an additional component called light polarizer is used. The polarizers made of typical glass are placed on the surface of the parallel glass plates in between which the liquid crystal of twisted nematic type are placed. These parallel glass plates are fixed with proper sealing in between them. The transparent conductors are placed just below the two parallel glass plates. The light polarizers are placed 90 out of position to each other. When the transparent conductors are not energized by the applied voltage that means in the condition of zero voltage the twisted nematic type liquid crystal completely twists the light passing through the system and the polarizers allow all the light to cross through. In this condition, the system shows dark pattern across the entire display device.

On the other hand, when the voltage between 2 and 8 is applied across the conductors, the crystal structures align amongst themselves with respect to the electric field. The resulting polarized light causes the digits to appear on the background.

Liquid crystal display is normally a seven segment type of display with a common connection as shown in Figure 7.28. Power consumption of this display system is low (1 microwatt per centimetre of length) in comparison to LED display system. This is also less costly.

Advantages of liquid crystal display (LCD)

  1. This is a low voltage (upto 15 volt), low current (less than 1 microampere/cm2 surface area) and low power (upto 1 microwatt/cm linear length) operated device.
  2. Visibility of LCD is superior to that of LED even in poor ambient light.
  3. Low power consumption characteristic of LCD makes it useful for watches and small portable instruments.

Disadvantages

  1. This is a very slow device compared to LED.
  2. Due to chemical degeneration, life of LCD is limited.
  3. Operating range of temperature is from 0 to 60C only.

(iii) Gas discharge-based display

The most important digital display system based on the gas discharge is the Nixie (TM) tube. The gas discharge tube is filled with inert gas like neon. There are 10 numbers of cold cathodes and one anode plate installed inside the discharge tube as shown in Figure 7.29 (a representative diagram of Nixie tube).

The cathodes look like dots but it is not like that. It is made of very thin but strong wire wound in the shape of digits ranging from 0 to 9 as shown. The positive supply higher than the breakdown voltage of the Neon gas (filled in the Nixie tube) is applied. When a particular switch of the cathode is closed that cathode glows and the appropriate number or digit is displayed through the Nixie tube. Neon gas has characteristic colour of orange when it glows. Under the glowing condition, the particular cathode glows brightly and the digit on the cathode is indicated clearly.

For indicating alphabet, another version of Nixie tube has been designed by the original manufacturer consisting of 15 segments as shown in Figure 7.30. When it is required to use the display for decimal as well as alphabets, the fifteen segment Nixie tube is employed.

The supply voltage requirement for operating the Nixie tube is higher than most of the other display system being upto 200 volt D.C. Similarly, current requirement is also slightly higher being upto 5 milliampres. One important characteristic of Nixie tube display system is that the digits are of larger size being upto 30 millimetres in height.

(iv) Liquid vapour-based display (LVD)

Liquid Vapour based display system is the latest addition to the digital display technology. The arrangement is consisting of a transparent and volatile liquid filled in between two parallel glass plates. The front and the rear glass are sealed with the help of suitable spacers. Internal surface of the front glass is made rough whereas the outer surface of the rear glass is painted black to provide dark background. The liquid in contact with the rough surface wets the glass surface. The refractive index of glass as well as that of the liquid in contact are same. A thin transparent electrode dipped inside the liquid is heated by supply voltage as shown in Figure 7.31.

When there is no voltage applied to the electrode the rear glass with dark background is seen black through the front glass and the liquid combined. When the voltage is applied to the electrode, heating of electrode takes place resulting in starting of evaporation of the liquid that is in contact with the electrode. The evaporation presents thin vapour film as well as its bubble around the rough surface of the glass. Since the refractive index of the vapour is different, a discontinuity is seen near the interface of the glass plate and the liquid resulting in scattering of light. This action is utilized for making display device. The display system made on this principle is superior to LCD-based display device.

(v) EPI-based display

Research has revealed that when an electric field is applied across a liquid having pigment particles in suspension gets charged and results in movement of these pigment particles. This is called electrophoresis. The principle of electrophoresis has been depicted in Figure 7.32. As is seen, under the influence of electric field, the charged pigment particles migrate towards either electrode depending upon the polarity of the charged particles.

The liquid (coloured black) is placed in between two parallel glass plates, one glass being transparent and the other one being reflective. When the migration of pigment particles takes place electrophoretically, colour of reflected light of the particles changes indicating a potential for designing a display system.

As shown in the diagram, the common electrode is transparent and the other electrode has been divided into two segments. Varying potential is applied to the segmented electrodes as indicated. If the particles get positively charged the movement is towards common electrode due to application of positive voltage upon the segment and the particles appear white when viewed from the viewers end. On the other hand, when the particles get negatively charged application of negative potential upon the segment immerses the particles into the dark suspension. As a result the reflection coming out of the dark liquid gets to the viewer. Thus, we observe that the colour of liquid suspension and the particles when combined together a display based on colour can be obtained successfully.

It has been observed that the particles which has been deposited near or upon the electrode remain unmoved even when the electric field is withdrawn. The analysis of this observation reveals possibility of design of a potential memory element.
