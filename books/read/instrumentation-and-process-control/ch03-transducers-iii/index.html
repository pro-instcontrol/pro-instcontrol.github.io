3.1 Transducers and Measurement of Temperature

3.1.1 Introduction

Temperature variable can be considered to be a fundamental variable like mass, length or time. Almost all the processing of this universe is temperature-dependent. Chemical processing systems, e.g. pharmaceuticals, petroleum refinings, Fertilizers, etc. are all temperature dependent processes. Even our body has temperature ranging in between 96°F to 98F which is being continuously controlled internally by means of sophisticated mechanism. Measurement of temperature, therefore, is an important aspect of instrumentation system.

The basis of temperature measurement is related to zeroth law of thermodynamics which states that if two bodies are in thermal equilibrium with a third body individually than all three together are in thermal equilibrium amongst one another. The scales used for measurement of temperature are Celsius (100C) and Fahrenheit (180°F). These two variety of measurement scales are related with one another for conversion purpose according to following expression:

(3.1)F − 32 = (9/5) C

In absolute temperature scale, the unit is known as Kelvin which is related with Celsius or Fahrenheit according to the following expression:

  1. Temperature in Celsius + 273.15 = Temperature in Kelvin
  2. Temperature in Fahrenheit + 459.69 = Temperature in Kelvin

This is because in Celsius unit, the absolute zero is at 273.15°C and in Fahrenheit unit this is at 459.69°F. The absolute zero temperature is that temperature where there in no motion at all in the molecules of the gas under consideration.

3.1.2 Measurement Methods

  1. (a) Mechanical methods
  2. (b) Electrical methods
  3. (c) Radiation methods.

(a) Mechanical methods

Under this category, there are five most popular measurement devices which depend upon the change in physical state or properties of the medium employed for the measurement. These are:

  1. (i) Liquid-in-glass thermometer
  2. (ii) Mercury-in-steel thermometer
  3. (iii) Bimetallic thermometer
  4. (iv) Vapour pressure thermometer
  5. (v) Constant volume thermometer

(i) Liquid-in-glass thermometer. Liquid-in-glass thermometer is essentially a liquid-expansion system which is characterized by narrow spans, uniform and linear scale, high accuracy and fast response. When the liquid which shall ultimately expand due to temperature changes is filled in a graduated glass tube having capillary length (capillary is a narrow passage, looking like a thin line under the transparent glass) for expansion, the arrangement is called liquid-in-glass thermometer. The glass bulb working as a reservoir for the measurement liquid is located at the bottom of the shape as shown in Figure 3.1. The basis for graduation of measurement scale depends upon the temperature characteristics of the liquid and corresponding range of measurement available. Normally, the freezing point of the liquid used is the lower range of the measurement scale and the boiling point is the upper range. The actual measurement span is fixed in between these two keeping in mind that the liquid should neither freeze nor boil out during measurement. Medical thermometer (used to measure the temperature of human body) in which mercury is actually kept inside the glass bulb and the expansion takes place inside the glass capillary also comes under this category.

The operation depends upon the resultant difference in thermal expansion of the liquid as well as the glass. Normally, the rate of expansion of liquid is more than the glass. To obtain maximum linear expansion for accuracy consideration, the cross-sectional area is minimized and therefore, the capillary construction becomes very important.

Another important consideration for accuracy is immersion techniques used. Deeper the thermometer is immersed, the more accurate the reading shall be. Three types of immersion techniques are employed for liquid-in-glass thermometer:

  1. Partial immersion
  2. Total immersion
  3. Full immersion

Partial immersion thermometer is immersed upto a fixed mark indicated by an immersion ring installed on the body of the thermometer. Obviously, this is less accurate.

Total immersion type is immersed upto the full height of the liquid column and therefore, the immersion ring marking is not required for this arrangement.

Full immersion thermometer is completely submerged inside the measurement liquid. The measurement scale is observed through a glass window made specifically for the purpose. Accuracy range for this instrument is in between 0.01C and 1°C. Application specification of liquid-in-glass thermometer comes under SAMA (Scientific Apparatus Makers Association) class IA and B.

Advantages of this thermometer are simple in construction, inexpensive, portability and clear visual indication. Disadvantages are that it is fragile by nature and remote transmission becomes difficult.

(ii) Mercury-in-steel thermometer. When the bulb (small reservoir) of the thermometer and the long capillary in which the expansion takes place is made of steel and the fluid used is mercury under pressure, then the arrangement is called mercury-in-steel thermometer. The capillary is normally connected to a bourdon tube element which is further connected to usual pointer-scale system for temperature indication. This arrangement can be used for temperature transmission as well as recording system since sufficient power is available for operation of the system as shown in Figure 3.2. This system can be employed over a practical range of 0 to 500°C.

The accuracy available is almost ± 1°C.

The most vulnerable point of mercury-in-steel thermometer is that as we go on increasing the length of the capillary, error introduced due to cooling effect of metallic length also goes on increasing. However, compensation techniques are applied to remove the error. Application specification of mercury-in-steel thermometer comes under SAMA class V.

Advantages of mercury-in-steel thermometer are that it has very good working range, becomes more accurate after application of compensation technique, remote transmission is easily possible and lastly if designed properly temperature in the lower scale including minus temperature can also be measured. Disadvantages are that it is very expensive and also inaccurate if compensation is not employed.

(iii) Bimetallic thermometer. Bimetallic thermometers are constructed using two different metals having different thermal coefficient of expansion. The metal having lower thermal coefficient of expansion is welded on to another metal having higher thermal coefficient of expansion and is fixed as a cantilever system as shown in Figure 3.3. As the temperature of the surrounding is increased the combined metal structure bends as shown in Figure 3.4. The movement so obtained due to bending can be utilized to move a pointer-scale system and can be calibrated to indicate the temperature. Invar having 35 % of nickel has very low temperature coefficient of expansion and hence is used as the low expansion coefficient metal in some of the construction. Another iron-nickel alloy having 36% of nickel is used for high expansion coefficient metal.

Industrial bimetallic thermometer uses helical shape of the bonded metal combination. In one configuration, one end is fixed and the other is free to move and is attached to pointer-scale system for indication as shown in Figure 3.5. The range of measurement is same as that of mercury-in-steel thermometer.

This is a very important temperature element which is very effectively used in combined system where sensing as well as control is required. For instance, in thermostatic control the small movement obtained is capable of actuating a control switch. The operation of these control switches control domestic ovens, electric irons, car winker lamps and refrigerators.

Advantages of this type of instrument are inexpensive having no maintenance at all, very stable operation. It is a self-generating type of instrument and recording feature can be added very easily. Disadvantages are its inability to measure rapidly changing temperature and disturbances in calibration due to rough handling.

(iv) Vapour pressure thermometer. Vapour pressure thermometer contains a volatile liquid along with its associated vapour inside a closed boundary. While in operation, the pressure of the liquid along with its vapour increases with increase in temperature irrespective of volume change. Since the pressure change due to change in temperature is the combined result of vapour as well as liquid, the measurement is affected at the interface between the vapour and the liquid.

To ascertain proper measurement of the interface temperature, the interface should remain inside the bulb, but this is not always possible practically. In this respect, we can envisage three different operating conditions:

  1. (a) First is when the bulb temperature is higher than the temperature of capillary as well as the bourdon tube (normal service). Thus, the process temperature shall vaporize the volatile liquid and consequently shall condense in capillary as well as bourdon tube.
  2. (b) Second is when the bulb temperature is lower than the temperature of the rest of the system (this case is observed in cryogenic services). Thus, the capillary as well as bourdon tube shall be filled completely with vapour.
  3. (c) Third is when the measurement system is designed to measure higher as well as lower temperature of the system. Thus, if bulb is at lower temperature, it will be filled with liquid mostly and the capillary and the bourdon tube both shall have vapour. On the other hand, if bulb is at higher temperature, it shall be filled with vapour mostly and the capillary and the bourdon both shall have liquid.

The three operating conditions described above come under application specification SAMA class II A, II B and II C respectively. Vapour pressure thermometer is a versatile equipment available for almost all conditions of measurement. But measurement range of vapour pressure thermometer is narrowfrom 255°C to 315°C only. However, these limits are decided by the nature of the fluid employed (being hydrocarbon or organic liquid mostly).

(v) Constant volume thermometer. Constant volume thermometer is yet another important equipment of temperature measurement in the hierarchy of liquid filled system. In principle this method is, in fact dependent upon pressure measurement wherein volume remains constant.

The bulb is exposed to the environment in which the temperature is to be measured. The entire system, i.e. the bourdon tube, capillary, the bulb, etc. are all filled with the measurement liquid. Due to a change (increase/decrease) in the temperature, the measurement liquid tends to expand or contract. Since the system is completely filled with liquid which is non-compressible by nature, any expansion or contraction causes the pressure of the liquid to increase or decrease which ultimately is the measure of temperature.

(b) Electrical methods

Under this category, there are three most popular measurement devices which are related to electrical behaviours, e.g. change in resistance due to change in temperature and generation of emf due to differential temperature. These devices are:

  1. (i) Resistance temperature detector
  2. (ii) Thermocouple and
  3. (iii) Thermistor.

(i) Resistance temperature detector (RTD). As we have discussed earlier, the basic characteristic of change in resistance of a conducting material due to change in temperature has been utilized in the development of RTD. The internal view of an industrial RTD has been shown in Figure 3.6 whereas complete assembled (ready for installation) view has been shown in Figure 3.7. The resistance temperature detector bulb consists of a coil of pure platinum wire. This wire is wound on a frame of mica, steatite or porcelain in such a way that it is completely free from strain. The strain gives rise to non-linearity in the measured value. After winding, the wire is annealed at a temperature above the maximum temperature which it would face during practical use. This annealing relieves the stress and ensures enhancement in characteristic of reproducibility for later resistance readings. While annealing care must be taken to prevent the wire becoming contaminated during process of annealing.

For industrial use the wire is wound on a steatite spool about three inches in length and 0.25 inch in diameter. It is wound non-inductively on groove and is covered by means of ceramic for protection from contamination. It is then immersed in an RTD inset (a kind of protecting tube). The inset uses magnesium oxide (MgO) as insulating powder packed tightly between the sheath and the element so that element cannot move. The inset is flexible, durable and mechanically strong and the thermal response of the RTD is fast. The ends of the platinum coil are connected to copper leads by means of gold wire.

Sometimes a small make-up resistance (called ballast) is also added in the RTD to make the overall resistance of bulb, leads and that of make-up to some standard value. The make up resistance is made up of manganin which has negligible temperature coefficient of resistance.

Selection of material of protecting tube and choice of immersion length depend upon service temperature, pressure as well as velocity of the fluid under measurement. For lower temperature and pressure and velocity (100°C, 10 kg/cm2, 1 metre/sec). Mild steel with immersion length upto 500 mm or 700 mm are sufficient. Above this stainless steel in required. From ambient to 200°C and 6 kg/cm2 aluminium material is used.

In addition to the above, long immersion length RTD upto 6000 mm (approximately 20 feet) are also designed for some specific measurement scheme. For instance, to measure the average temperature of storage heavy oil tank (approximately 30 metres in diameter) such long immersion length RTD elements are employed. One more special RTD called annular element RTD has been designed to measure fast changing temperature. This necessitates quick response of RTD element employed. These RTD elements are made of annular elements and are tightly fitted against the exterior of the thermowell (another kind of protecting tube) to minimize the lag involved in such measurement scheme.

There is yet another application where degradation of temperature measuring element is of prime concern, a combination of two different measuring elements have been employed. This is called RTD-thermocouple combine element. Both the elements are enclosed in the same sheath enabling two simultaneous measurement schemes for the same temperature. In such situation if one element degrades, still another shall be in operation.

As far as measurement range is concerned, the platinum RTD is used between the range of temperature 190°C to 630.5°C. Normally at 0°C, the platinum RTD is standardized to have a resistance value of 100 ohms. The variation in resistance between the temperature 0°C and 630.5°C is governed by the expression:

(3.2)Rt = Ro (1 + At + Bt²)

Similarly, the variation in resistance between the temperature 190°C and 0°C is governed by the expression:

(3.3)Rt = Ro [1 + At + Bt² + C (t − 100) t³]

In these two expressions, Rt and Ro are the resistance at the temperature t°C and 0°C respectively. A, B and C are constants. The values of Ro, A, B and C are obtained by measuring the value of resistance of RTD at ice-point, steam-point, sulphur-point and oxygen-point respectively.

  1. Ice-point is the melting-point of pure ice which is equal to 0°C.
  2. Steam-point is the boiling-point of pure steam which is equal to 100°C.
  3. Sulphur-point is the boiling-point of liquid sulphur which is equal to 444.6°C.
  4. Oxygen-point is the boiling-point of liquid oxygen which is equal to 182.97°C.

The following derived expressions give the value of Ro, A, B and C directly:

(3.4)A = (R100 − Ro)/(100 Ro)

where R100 is the value of resistance of RTD when it is kept in steam at 100°C;

(3.5)B = (R444.6 − Ro)/(444.6² Ro)

where R444.6 is the value of resistance of RTD when it is kept in boiling liquid sulphur at 444.6°C.

(3.6)C = (R182.97 − Ro − 182.97 A Ro − 182.97² B Ro)/(182.97³ Ro)

where R182.97 is the value of resistance of RTD when it is kept in liquid oxygen at 182.97°C.

3.1.3 Readout Instrumentation Scheme

Measurement of temperature by RTD is in fact the measurement of resistance of RTD element. Hence, the readout instrumentation scheme corresponds to calibration of RTD element in terms of temperature. This is achieved by D.C. bridge – RTD transducing system. For usual application, first of its kind is conventional Wheatstone bridge, a 2-wire measurement system. In this scheme, RTD element having unknown resistance Rx forms one leg in addition to usual 3 legs of the Wheatstone bridge. Output is obtained as out of balance current which indicates proportional temperature. Though the system is very simple and inexpensive, but not so accurate. The RTD elements are installed in the field and is quite far away from the central control room where readout instruments are located. The lead wires through which the RTD element is connected to the control room instrument, represents substantial amount of extra resistance in series with the RTD resistance. Resistance of these copper leads thus becomes significant with respect to RTD element which results in error in temperature measurement. The solution to this problem is use of suitable lead compensation technique. However, if the readout instrument is located just near to the RTD element then no compensation is required and fairly accurate measurement is obtained. But the practical situation is different and therefore, two types of compensation techniques are applied for this. One is called a 3-wire (Siemens 3-wire system) and the other one is called a 4-wire (Callender 4-wire system) measurement system.

Figures 3.8 and 3.9 show the 3-wire and 4-wire measurement systems respectively, whereas Figure 3.10 shows the 2-wire measurement system.

3.1.4 Accuracy Consideration of RTD

In addition to the effect of lead wire on the accuracy, following are the other factors affecting the accuracy of measurement by RTD system:

  • There are many calibration standards for RTD element which are based upon different values of temperature coefficient of resistance for the selected metal. Though they differ marginally in values but present accuracy problem on measurement by all means.
  • As far as manufacturing tolerances are concerned, these also differ from one manufacturer to the other. Hence, accuracy problem can crop up if the existing RTD element is replaced by another of different manufacturer.
  • The current flowing through RTD element while in operation generates heat and this heat can be additive to the process heat of which the temperature is being measured. In this situation effect of self-heating of RTD element due to current flowing can present accuracy problem. This can be minimized by restricting the current flow to the bridge (0.003 ampere).
  • Physical deformation in shape due to thermal or mechanical shock or vibration and chemical contamination due to migration of iron into platinum at elevated temperature can also affect the accuracy of measurement. This effect sometimes can be specified as well by the manufacturer (0.05° per year).

Standards for the Specifications

Standards for the specifications of three most widely used RTD elements are:

  • Copper 53 – SAMA, range: 0 to 150°C.
  • Nickel – SAMA type II, range: −200°C to +315°C.
  • Platinum – SAMA 100 or DIN 43760, range: −190°C to 630°C.

(ii) Thermocouple

As discussed earlier, the basic principle behind thermocouple measurement system is that if two ends (which are fused together) of dissimilar thermoelements are maintained at two different temperatures, an emf is produced which is proportional to difference in temperature between two ends. In practice, however, one end is maintained at fixed temperature which is called reference junction temperature and the other end, which is called measuring junction is immersed inside the process of which the temperature is to be measured. Maintenance of reference junction at fixed temperature can be done by keeping the reference junction at melting ice or by setting a refrigeration unit where all thermocouples of one particular area can be connected for the sake of convenience. Another approach is to keep the reference junction at ambient temperature and the variation in ambient temperature is well compensated by suitable means by the measurement circuit itself. In addition to above, it is essential that thermocouple emf is transmitted without any loss to the indicating/recording/control instruments located inside the central control room. Accuracy requirement needs that the cable to be used to transmit the thermocouple emf should be of the same material as the thermocouple element itself. But due to economic reasons, the extension cable of special alloys is required to be used which should have similar thermoelectric properties as the thermocouple element at atmospheric temperature. The extension cable virtually extends the reference junction of the thermocouple to the instrument and thus compensates for the temperature variation between the thermocouple junction and the instrument.

When extension cables are found to be expensive, other less expensive material of different types from the thermoelement combinations are used. These materials need to have equivalent thermo-emf characteristics. These cables are known as compensating cables and normally give adequate compensation for generated emf at the junction. The extension/compensating cables being of different polarity are colour coded individually for correct polarity identification. For identification of the type of cable, the overall colour coding is also done. 3.1.5 Constructional Features of Thermocouples

Thermocouple elements are fused together at elevated temperature and the individual elements are passed through two in-bore insulators called beads as shown in Figure 3.11.

The beads are made of refractory material.

Normal length of the bead is from 50 mm to 75 mm only. In special cases where temperature to be measured is extremely high, an additional refractory cover may be required to be put on the existing beads for mechanical reasons. However, it tends to slow down the response of the thermocouple. The thermocouple elements after coming out of the insulator are terminated at the strip on the thermocouple head. Thereafter, the extension cable/compensating cable are connected through the terminal strip and taken to central control room through cable routing on aluminium trays. The terminal strip is fixed inside the thermocouple head. A complete thermocouple ready for installation has been shown in Figure 3.12.

The choice of refractory material for thermocouple insulation depend upon particular operating condition. In oxidizing atmosphere fused silica withstand thermal shock and can be used upto 1000°C. Alumina-Silica mixture can withstand 1700 °C and pure alumina upto 1850 °C but are less resistant to thermal shock.

In reducing atmosphere or under vacuum, silica refractories are not used because it embrittles the thermocouple element. In this condition, pure alumina is used.

3.2 Other Considerations

Mathematical Expression

The mathematical equation that governs emf generation of a particular type of thermocouple is given by:

(3.7)e = a + bt + ct²

Here a, b and c are constants found by measuring the emf produced by the same lot of thermocouple at freezing point of antimony (630.5°C), boiling point of silver (960.8°C) and boiling point of gold (1064.43°C) respectively.

Annealing of Thermocouple

Thermocouple element when put in the hot process for measurement purposes, the parts of the wire element come under strain. The effect is disturbance in homogeneity of the grain structure of the element resulting in measurement error.

To minimize this effect, the thermocouple element is annealed before put in operation. Annealing process is carried out in presence of air upto the maximum temperature to which the thermocouple shall be used while in operation. The annealing process continues for a period of 2 to 3 minutes only. The annealing is done by passing electric current through the thermocouple element which is kept hanging in the air.

Life of Thermocouple

The life of thermocouple cannot be predicted with full confidence because in some cases it has been seen that it failed during commissioning period itself. Therefore, every thermocouple should have checking schedule for checking the same on regular interval against a standard thermocouple kept for this purpose only. Any degradation in thermocouple element shall be immediately known by irregularities in measurement reading during checking. Degradation in thermocouple is loss of homogeneity caused by strain or contamination.

Generally, the solution to this problem is replacement of the thermocouple itself. However, sometimes strain can be removed by reannealing of the entire thermocouple and sometimes changing the method of installation as well. Contamination, on the other hand, can be avoided by careful handling of thermocouple element and by use of proper refractory. Failure of thermocouples have been subject of discussion and also research in almost all type of process industries using thermocouples.

Installation Practice

Proper installation of thermocouple provides protection of thermocouple as well as accuracy of temperature measurement. Selection of thermocouple accessories and extension wire, selection of thermonozzle and thermowell and selection of installation – representative point of measurement, i.e. location of thermocouple are the normal considerations that come under installation practice. We have already discussed at length regarding thermocouple and its extension wire. Here we shall start our discussion with thermonozzle and thermowell.

To protect the thermocouple from the effects of rapid oxidation or other forms of deterioration due to action of difficult fluids in which these thermocouples are immersed, thermocouple sheaths/thermowells are used. For temperature measurement scheme of below 500°C where fast response can be an important consideration, the use of thermowell is avoided. Upto a temperature of 1000°C sheath of 316 stainless steel is used. Above 1000C refractory sheathing is recommended. Care must be exercised in selecting the shape of the thermocouple to keep the response time minimum, yet provide adequate strength to prolong the life of thermowell as well as thermocouple element. A taper shank construction of thermowell is necessary to provide mechanical strength needed to prevent vibration caused by the velocity of the fluid flow. A thermowell has been shown in Figure 3.13.

In normal cases, where diameter of the main pipeline is larger and allows sufficient immersion length, this thermowell is directly welded to the pipeline (socket-weld type). Another alternative is to weld a threaded socket of approximate size to the main pipeline and then the thermowell is screwed properly. For the pipeline where such allowance for sufficient immersion length or depth is not available, a thermonozzle is first welded onto the pipeline.

Thermonozzle is usually made of 316 stainless steel for oxygen service and of carbon steel for other services. Typical installation have been shown in Figure 3.15 whereas Figure 3.14 shows actual construction of thermonozzle.

Thermowell which has been shown in Figure 3.13 is tightened properly onto this thermonozzle so that the bottom of the thermowell reaches slightly below the centreline of the main pipeline. The entire installation (all three components) has been shown in Figure 3.16.

Installation of this type gives the effective temperature in the middle of the fluid flow pattern. This is because the mass velocity is highest at this point. This ensures better heat transfer by convection. However, when the velocity of the fluid flow is extremely high (for instance, superheated steam of very high pressure), deep immersion of thermocouple may not be required at all.

Proper point of installation should be selected on the pipeline so that a representative reading of temperature can be obtained. One method for finding best installation point is to use a portable thermocouple for taking measurement of temperature at several places.

Positioning of thermocouple is also an important consideration. Thermowells should be installed perpendicular to the pipeline or the vessel whatever it may be. If perpendicular installation is not at all possible, installation at an angle or in an elbow may be considered as shown in Figures 3.17 and 3.18.

In temperature measurement scheme of high temperature furnace, vertical installation from top of the furnace is recommended. This will prevent bending of the element.

In all the installation mentioned above, grounding of thermocouple leads at the instrument end is not required, because the thermocouple body is already grounded through the metallic pipe where it is installed.

Special Class Thermocouple

In majority of the cases, the failure analysis of thermocouples installed in severe operating conditions has prompted to enhance the existing design consideration. Special class thermocouples are result of such design innovations. These are constructed for the measurement of temperature for difficult processes as well as for some typical operating environments. These services sometimes call for refinement and changes in the original shape of the thermocouple as well. We shall describe following four types of special thermocouples which are in use in process industries:

  1. Gasifier thermocouple.
  2. Surface temperature thermocouple.
  3. Superheater metal thermocouple.
  4. Quick immersion thermocouple.

1. Gasifier thermocouple

Gasifier is a unit operation reactor vessel where partial oxidation of hydrocarbons (naphtha or fuel oil or LSHS) or of raw crushed coal takes place along with mixture of pure oxygen and superheated steam (of 64 kg/cm2), resulting in formation of raw gas. Raw gas contains mainly hydrogen and carbon monoxide and constitutes the main inputs for the production of anhydrous ammonia as well as urea fertilizer.

The three ingredients described above are injected into the gasifier through special gun called gasifier-gun. 2. Surface (Skin) temperature thermocouple

The process reactor vessels which are designed non-insulating type are normally operated at higher pressure and temperature. At elevated temperature and pressure, damage or crack in the lining of the internal wall of the process reactor vessels may lead to creation of hot spots on the outer surface. This results in reduction of strength of the vessel and sometimes complete destruction creating safety hazard for the operating personnel in addition to the plant. Measurement of skin temperature and continuous monitoring thereof can immediately point out the area in which there is possibility of creation of hot spot.

Thermocouple used for this purpose is of ordinary K-type only but the construction is somewhat different from the usual one. The tip of the thermocouple assembly is attached to the surface of the process reactor vessel by means of cylindrical shaped hollow magnets. The two magnets are bolted symmetrically to a common rigid fixing plate and is hanged in vertical position through the outer surface of the process reactor vessel. The magnets have

one end of a 3/8 inch size flat of steel is welded or screwed onto the main plate and the other end of the flat is attached to a small thermocouple housing. The tip of the thermocouple is securely fixed to the lower portion of the housing which rests on the outer surface of the process reactor vessel. The lead wires come out of the housing and are connected to the temperature recorder or monitoring system. This type of thermocouple can be moved on the outer surface of the process reactor vessel here and there through the magnet to know the skin temperature at different points by moving the complete assembly. The magnets hold the surface wherever the assembly is moved. This arrangement is used on the gasifier reactor vessel as well.

3. Superheater metal thermocouple

In high pressure and high temperature steam producing boilers, superheaters are located in high flue gas temperature zone of the boiler furnace to absorb high rate of furnace radiation. In such situation the superheater tubes must be ensured of continuous and uninterrupted supply of feed steam. Even if the total load on the boiler have been reduced, the superheater must be fed with certain minimum amount of steam to keep the superheather metal temperature within reasonable limit. This is advantageous for economic selection of superheater material. Therefore, the temperature of superheater metal has to be monitored by applying suitable method of measurement for metal temperature.

For this purpose, especially constructed K-type thermocouples are used. The tip of the thermocouples is constructed in rectangular arc shape as shown in Figure 3.21.

The size of the rectangular metallic grip is normally 3″ × 2″. The rectangular arc shaped tip is welded onto the superheater metal so that it grips the surface of the metal. Since the area of grip being approximately 6 square inches is quite large, the response of the measurement shall be faster. The thermocouple inset which is mineral insulated is very long, may be 6 metre or even more (capillary-like construction), the length being dependent upon the actual distance between the point of measurement and the installation point where the thermocouple head shall be installed (out of the furnace). The increased length of the thermocouple inset makes the thermocouple costly but the safety and the economy of the superheater tube are such requirements that overall design becomes economic.

(4) Quick immersion thermocouple

The temperature of liquid steel is measured by quick immersion thermocouple. In this thermocouple, the element is of R-type from platinum/rhodium group. The thermocouple is protected by means of vitreous silica sheathing. The sheath is strong enough to withstand the mechanical stress and the thickness of the sheath is small enough to have response time of 15 seconds. It is unfortunate that one sheath is used for every spot measurement and for next measurement a new sheath has to be utilized.

The body of the thermocouple assembly is made of steel tube. One end of the assembly where the tip of the thermocouple is located is bent over for easy immersion. This portion is covered with refractory or graphite material for extra protection for the tube. The thermocouple insulation used for this purpose is aluminous porcelain and near the hot junction alumina is used. The tip of the thermocouple and one inch extra wire is left uninsulated to increase the speed of response.

The thermocouple assembly must not possess any trace of oil or grease, otherwise the thermocouple will be spoiled the moment it is dipped into the liquid steel before even the measurement is taken. This is because of the presence of traces of sulphur. This method has been successful for measurement of temperature upto 1700°C during steel making process.

In addition to the above four types of special thermocouples constructed for specific purposes, there are other typical measurement problems which call for not special construction but special connection. These are measurement of average temperature and differential temperature. The former is useful for knowing the overall temperature of a large duct, e.g. combustion air inlet duct in steam boiler house and final outlet flue gas temperature near the chimney. The latter is useful for measurement of temperature difference between heavy oil and the atomizing steam in start-up circuit of burner atomizing and scavenging system in steam boiler house. For average measurement, any number of thermocouples can be used. More number of thermocouples used, more representative measurement can be obtained. For accurate measurement, the resistance of all thermocouples taken together with that of the extension wire circuits should be identical. Since the resistance of the actual thermocouple shall vary with temperature and also the lengths of the extension wire, the effect of these variations are minimized by using swamping resistor. The value of swamping resistor shall be high (approximately 1500 ohms) with respect to change in resistance involved. To prevent ground loop current flow, grounding of thermocouple should be avoided. Parallel connection of thermocouples for measurement of average temperature is advantageous since the calibration of receiver instrument is same as that for a single thermocouple.

Similarly, two thermocouples may be connected as shown in Figure 3.23 to measure differential temperature between two processes. Again the two thermocouples must be similar in all respect along with their extension wires (of same material).

While in operation, the emf developed by the two thermocouples oppose each other. In this condition if the temperature of the two processes is equal, then the net emf developed shall be zero. If, on the other hand, a differential temperature exists, then the emf developed shall be proportional to the differential temperature.

The temperature indicator can be provided with center zero scale if process conditions demand, else a normal scale is sufficient to provide the indication. For this measurement, there is no need of reference junction compensation for the instrument. Also grounding of thermocouple is not required. But the resistance of both the thermocouples should be same.

(iii) Thermistors

As we have discussed earlier, the thermistors are made of semiconductor materials and have positive as well as negative temperature coefficient of resistance (PTC and NTC). Thermistors having NTC behaviour are used for temperature measurement purposes.

Being a solid state device, it conducts exactly like transistors. Because of its NTC behaviour (decrease in resistance due to rise in temperature), the current flow through it is directly proportional to the temperature being measured. Since the temperature coefficient of resistance of thermistor is large with respect to its counterpart RTD element, it is more sensitive than RTD. Therefore, where fast response and precise measurement are required, thermistors are the ideal selection. Resistance value of a thermistor is more than 10,000 ohms but that of RTD is only 100 ohms. But range of operation of thermistor is too narrow because of its non-linear relationship between resistance and temperature. However, if the thermistor is applied within its normally specified range of application then it provides stable and repeatable performance. That is why, this is used in domestic refrigerator cut-off system.

As in RTD, there is no need of 3-wire or 4-wire construction system in this case because the nominal resistance value of thermistor is very high with respect to lead wire resistance.

As far as readout instrument is concerned, linearization is required to be done because of non-linear behaviour of thermistor. The simplest voltage divider circuit shown in Figure 3.24 does this job of linearising the voltage vs. temperature relationship. More than one thermistor can be added to this circuit to achieve more accurate linearization.

3.2.2 Accuracy Consideration

The most common problem associated with accuracy of measurement by thermistor is its interchangeability. The resistance vs. temperature curve of thermistor is dependant upon material composition. The material of construction is practically difficult to monitor and therefore, required tolerance may not be maintained. However, selection of required product from a manufactured batch is done carefully and thereby a tolerance within 0.1 °C over a particular specified range of temperature can be achieved.

The tolerance curve indicates a stable range of 20°C to 85°C. The curve has been shown in Figure 3.26.

Another problem encountered in thermistor system of measurement is due to self-heating. The heating problem in this case is more than is encountered in case of RTD measurement system because of high resistance value. To counter this the voltage applied to thermistor should be low.

Surface contamination of thermistor also affects accuracy by creating shunting effect. If some small particles from the thermistor chip are lost due to physical damage, the resistance gets changed thereby affecting the accuracy of measurement.

Soldering of extension wire to thermistor lead wire can create error. If solder material gets diffused into the lead, then it can create thermocouple effect resulting in production of thermal emf and hence measurement error. Hence, the soldering should be thermally immune.

3.2.3 Development of Potentiometric Temperature Recording System

Continuous temperature recording system has passed through several phases of its development. Earlier, deflection or moving coil type of recording systems were in use which operated on the basic principle of permanent magnet moving coil (PMMC) instrument. These recorders, however, are still in use in some of the older process plants. Limitations of these recorders have been inferior input impedance and poor sensitivity.

Potentiometric recorder which is basically a null type system improves overall performance of the recording system. Basic operation is similar to self-balancing potentiometer. Off late this has been probably the most versatile arrangement in the field of potentiometric measurement system for temperature.

For the last few decades, almost all temperature measurement-cum-recording systems utilizes this arrangement for simplicity of operation and also fast response. The scheme has been shown in Figure 3.27.

In an open loop system of thermocouple response, the deflection in galvanometer system is the result of out of balance potential produced across the slide-wire of the potentiometric circuit. But the potentiometric measurement-cum-recording system of temperature is a closed loop system. The out of balance potential so obtained is utilized here to balance the potentiometer to a new setting automatically by means of servomotor mechanism as shown. At the new balance point, the unknown potential produced by thermocouple-cum-slidewire system equals the standard potential applied across the slide-wire.

Figure 3.28 shows the internal details of the potentiometer-slide-wire circuit arrangement. The too many resistances have been used in the circuit for the sake of Ambient Temperature Compensation.

The change in ambient temperature has two effects:

  1. The resistance of instrument coil will change.
  2. Increase in ambient, i.e. increase in cold junction temperature will decrease the emf of thermocouple. This decrease will be equal to the emf of a thermocouple with one junction at ambient and another at which the instrument have been calibrated previously.

The first effect is minimized by using a ballast resistance made of manganin having negligible change in resistance with respect to temperature. To minimize the second effect, the usual practice is to use ambient temperature compensation techniques. For this, arrangement is made to add the amount of emf which has been reduced. The thermocouple slide-wire combination circuit (shown in Figure 3.28) have three resistances of manganin and fourth resistance of nickel having high temperature coefficient. The out of balance potential due to ambient change shall be added to thermocouple emf. Here R2 (zero adjustment resistance), R3 (span adjustment resistance) and R5 are made of manganin having small temperature coefficient and R4 is made of nickel having high temperature coefficient.

When the temperature of the instrument increases, the potential drop across R4 shall increases. The point X shall thus become less positive with respect to P, driving the point of balance Y towards upper scale. This has the effect as if a small potential has been added to the existing emf of the thermocouple, thus creating compensating effect to the increase in ambient temperature. If the resistance R4 can be put somehow at the same point where the effective cold junction of the thermocouple exists and temperature-emf relationship of thermocouple is linear then this compensating effect can be made more accurate.

The shunting resistance Rs connected across the slide-wire has been used to bring the overall resistance of the network to a suitable value. For instance, the overall resistance of the network for which the above circuit has been drawn has a value of 1400 ohms.

The D.C. output of the system is first converted to A.C. by the convertor circuit. The A.C. signal so obtained is amplified in two stages. Two-stage amplification in some of the designs is required to meet the high level power requirement for the operation of servomotor. The amplified signal is applied to the control winding of the A.C. servomotor. The servomotor changes its position by the action of control winding voltage with respect to reference winding voltage. The servomotor is mechanically coupled to the sliding contact which moves on the potentiometer to find a balance point. The balance point is a point where two potentials become exactly equal. When the balance point is achieved, the movement of sliding contact stops and a printing mechanism is triggered. This printing mechanism is synchronized with respect to selector switch mechanism for a multipoint temperature recorder. Such complicated mechanism is, however, not at all required in a single point temperature recorder.

The selector switch rotates at its axis and selects a particular point, i.e. a particular thermocouple for measurement of temperature. The emf produced by the particular thermocouple goes through measurement, amplification and balancing sequence as described and finally triggers the printing mechanism. The printing mechanism consists of multi-function operation that prints a dot which is the record of the particular measurement point of temperature and it prints the serial number of the point as well one-by-one. Other function such as date, time, etc. are also incorporated for printing once in a while say after every one hour or so.

The paper upon which the recording takes place is a roll type chart. The chart paper is driven by a chart drive motor at fixed speed. In addition to this there are two more fractional horse power motors. One is called selector motor that selects the multipoint (one point connected to one thermocouple) one-by-one. This motor runs the printing mechanism as well through different gear combination. The second motor is the balancing motor, i.e. servomotor. When a particular point has been selected, the selection of second point is not taken up unless the selection-cum-measurement-cum-recording cycle for the point already selected is completed.

In some of the industrial temperature recorders (e.g., clear span J.N. Marshall recorder) the balancing motor/servomotor is not of the continuous type. They use linear motor for carrying out the balancing job. In this system two permanent magnets which are fixed parallel to one another are arranged in between a straight iron rod and a soft iron flat piece. A coil, which is called control coil and which is energized by the potentiometer out of balance DC signal is wound around the iron rod and is free to move linearly along the rod in the magnetic field created by the pair of permanent magnets. When the D.C. signal from the potentiometer is applied to this control coil, linear movement results and that is why it is known as linear servomotor. The direction of this linear movement is changed by changing the polarity of the control signal. The control coil rests on the slide-wire against the action of a spring. Advantages of this control motor is easy maintenance, faster response and higher accuracy with respect to conventional servomotor. Figure 3.29 shows the arrangement of a linear motor.

The modern temperature recorder (e.g., Yokogawa make) uses especially designed D.C. brushless servomotor for carrying out balancing operation. In that case there is no need of convertor circuit because high performance instrumentation amplifier amplifies the potentiometer signal directly.

For recording operations, several types of pen and ink sets are used. Disposable felt pens or plotter pens are used nowadays in modern recorders. These are placed in a six-colour ribbon cassette and securely fixed on the moving printing head.

(c) Radiation Method

Thermal radiation is a basic property of any material. It is a continuous process in nature. It ceases only when the material is brought under absolute temperature condition because in this condition there is no movement at all of the particles contained in the material.

Radiation method of temperature measurement system is used very effectively when the medium is corrosive and abrasive or the object is inside a furnace, e.g. an ingot of hot metal which is in molten state etc. This method is applied to measure the temperature above the gold point i.e. 1063°C and internationally accepted scales are in fact based for this portion of the range only. The scale above that of 1063°C is based upon visible radiation (monochromatic) upto 3900°C. The following expression relates scale of measurement of monochromatic visible radiation from 1063°C to 3900°C.

(3.8)log_e(I_v/I_b) = (C₂/λ_v)[1/1336 − 1/(T_b + 273)]

where I_v is the visible radiation (monochromatic) of wavelength λ_v cm emitted by a black body at temperature T_b, I_b is the radiation emitted by the black body at gold point and the value of constant C₂ is assumed to be 1.4384 cm-degree.

When a body is heated it changes colour because the distribution of radiation energy amongst different wavelengths changes as the temperature varies. At temperature 500°C, the body is red, orange at 1100°C and white above 1400°C. At this temperature the body radiates all colours in the visible spectrum. Radiation pyrometer measurement techniques are based upon measurement of radiation energy emitted by hot bodies between the wavelengths 0.1 micron and 10 micron (1 micron equals 10⁻³ mm.) The intensity and wavelength of the radiation is dependent upon the emittance and temperature of the hot bodies.

Like wavelength, emittance also varies from one body to another, and therefore, a standard has been devised as reference for calibration purposes of the pyrometers. This is black body which is considered a perfect emitter since it absorbs all heat and emits that later on as radiation energy. Consequently, the black body has absorption strength as unity and therefore, its emissivity is also unity. All other bodies have absorption strength as well as emissivity less than unity.

A black body can be realized in practice as a large enclosure having a constant temperature with a very small opening. A ray of radiation energy entering the enclosure through the opening is reflected back a number of times inside and by the time it tries to reach near the opening, it is completely absorbed. Since the enclosure acts as a black body, it is neither hot nor cold.

The total energy E_t radiated per second per square centimetre by a perfect black body is given by:

(3.9)E_t = σ T_b⁴

where T_b is the absolute temperature and σ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant. The value of σ is 5.672 × 10⁻¹² watts/cm² sec degree⁴.

However, for a particular wavelength, radiation energy can be proportional to T_b⁵ instead of T_b⁴, but for all practical measurement system, the expression 3.9 is applied.

Practical Measurement System

The practical measurement system consists mainly of three parts:

  1. (i) Sensing lens which receives the radiation from the hot body.
  2. (ii) Detector unit which converts the radiation into proportional electrical signal.
  3. (iii) Indicator unit which finally indicates the temperature in terms of electrical signal.

There are altogether five different pyrometers which work on the above principle. These shall be described one-by-one.

(i) Land surface pyrometer

When the hot body of which the temperature has to be measured by the pyrometer is open, the theory applied is called land surface pyrometer theory. Obviously the main function for this method of measurement is the correction factor applied due to difference in emissivity of the open surface and that of the perfect black body.

The radiation from the hot surface is made to approximate much more close to black body radiation by placing a concave reflector on the surface. If the reflectivity of the reflector is β, then the intensity of radiation I_e passing through the small opening on the reflector is given by:

(3.10)I_e = [e / (1 − β(1 − e))] σ T_b⁴

where e is the emissivity of the hot surface. By applying this system, effective emissivity of the hot surface is increased by almost 60%, i.e. from 0.6 to 0.96.

(ii) Optical pyrometer

Optical pyrometer (also called brightness pyrometer) is dependent upon human adjustment as seen by the eyes through the viewing windows. This is the reason that it is designed to work for a very narrow bandwidth of radiation wavelength (within visible light only). An optical filter is used to obtain the desired wavelength. Optical pyrometer uses a red filter since it radiates all wavelengths above 0.63 micron very effectively and blocks all the smaller wavelengths. This is because the sensitivity of human eyes starts decreasing above 0.63 micron wavelength of radiation. Hence the red filter together with human eye act as a bandpass filter (optical type) for the wavelength of radiation that enters the instrument.

The basic configuration of a practical pyrometer used to measure the temperature of flame inside the process boiler has been shown in Figure 3.30. The object lens focuses the radiation emitted by the hot body on to the lamp. The lamp itself emits radiation which is the reference radiation. The lamp current is supplied by the measuring circuit. The hot body is seen through microscopic lens and appears as background behind the lamp. The brightness of lamp is adjusted by varying the lamp current until lamp disappears from the background. In this condition lamp current is proportional to radiation emitted by the hot body. Since the lamp current has been calibrated against the temperature of a black body, measured current provides temperature indication of the hot body.

The automatic version of this radiation pyrometer uses a photodetector in place of human eye. The light from the hot body enters through object lens and focuses onto a mirror (modulator) that oscillates at faster speed and allows the radiation energy from the hot body and the lamp alternately to a filter. The filter allows radiation energy of selected wavelength from the hot body as well as lamp the photodetector. The photodetector after comparing the two radiation energy adjusts these radiation to equalize by using a null-balance system. The adjusted lamp current provides temperature indication of the hot body. Since the lamp current is adjusted continuously, this arrangement can record temperature continuously of a process. Optical pyrometer is a versatile instrument. Its performance reliability and accuracy are satisfactory.

(iii) Total radiation pyrometer

Total radiation pyrometer is not specific for any particular wavelength range because it is not basically optical instrument that responds only to a visible range. In fact total radiation pyrometer responds to all wavelengths because measurement is related to the heating effect of the total radiation. Hence, any loss of radiation whatsoever can give erroneous results. For instance some radiations are filtered out by the glass windows through which the radiations are allowed to pass. Similarly, any obstructions in the path such as vapours or fumes present in between the hot body and the pyrometer detector can result in attenuation effect to certain wavelength of the radiation.

In practical arrangement used in the industry, radiation emitted by the hot body passes through a lens and focuses onto a heat-sensitive element which works as a detector. The detector used is a thermopile (It is the number of thermocouples connected together to add-up the emfs produced by the individual thermocouple.) The temperature indicator is connected to the thermopile circuit and indicates the temperature of the hot body.

In this design, thermal detector is the same but optical components are different for different temperature ranges. For high temperature measurement, lenses made of glass are used, for medium temperature ranges, quartz lenses are used and for lower temperature, ordinary mirrors are used. The total radiation pyrometer works on the Stefan-Boltzmann law as described earlier.

The error presented due to attenuation of radiation by obstruction as described above is overcome by using an optical pyrometer for calibrating the total radiation pyrometer under actual operating conditions.

Tinsley version of total radiation pyrometer measures the temperature of small objects moving at high speed, e.g. temperature of narrow steel strip in a steel plant. Another version called Brown Low Range Radiamatic Unit measures surface temperature of a moving object in the lower range (50°C to 175°C). In this system an image of the object is focused through calcium fluoride lens into a sensitive thermopile and the temperature is measured by potentiometric circuit.

(iv) Ratio pyrometer

Ratio pyrometer is used for measurement of surface temperature. The instrument uses a photon detector for measurement of radiation of two different wavelengths in visible regions. The temperature is inferred from the ratio of these two measured radiations.

The principle of ratio pyrometer is based upon the fact that the radiation at one wavelength increases with temperature at a different rate from radiation at another wavelength.

In practical arrangement of the system, the two different wavelengths of radiation are provided by a rotating wheel filter. The rotating wheel filter consists of red windows and blue windows. Red windows allow red wavelength and blue windows allow blue wavelength only. As the rotation continues, the detector is faced with alternate red and blue wavelength of radiation. The resulting pulse of the two wavelengths are amplified, separated and compared in amplitude. The ratio so obtained is proportional to temperature of the hot body.

Other practical arrangements are also available in the market but all share the same principle and measure the temperature to almost same accuracy.

(v) Photoelectric pyrometer

The photoelectric pyrometer consists of mainly a photocell and an optical system. The photocell consists of a disc made of iron, coated on one side with a photosensitive layer of selenium.

The optical system consists of a lens. In the complete instrument, the photocell and the lens are put together. The radiation from the hot body is directed through lens to the photocell by means of the stops put between the lens and the photocell. The photocell is provided with a variable shunt resistance. During installation, the shunt resistance is adjusted for making correction in the emissivity of the radiating surface. The current produced by the photocell is proportional to the temperature of the hot body.

The complete instrument is enclosed in a water jacket. A heat-resistant steel tube of approximately 50 mm diameter projected before the water jacket. Filtered and compressed air is passed through this tube that provides an optical path free from fumes and other similar obstructions. An extension tube is fitted before the lens as well to protect the lens from oil and moisture.

3.3 Measurement of Flow

3.3.1 Introduction

Flow meter is a device that measures the rate of flow/quantity of a moving fluid in an open/closed channel/pipe. This is one of the four basic process variables of any process industry because measurement and control of fluid flow is the essential feature for smooth operation of the process plants.

Flow meter primary devices, mounted externally or internally on the pipeline produce measurement signal proportional to flow which vary according to interaction of the fluid to the primary device. Flow meter secondary devices respond to the signal obtained from the primary device and convert these to suitable displays or present a proportional output signal for further manipulations.

Primary devices include orifice, upstream/downstream piping, flow conditioners and tapping details. The differential pressure transmitter, manifold valves and flanges/fittings, connecting tubing or wiring, required pneumatic or electrical supply system are all elements of secondary devices.

Basically flow is measured in two ways, one in the form of flow rate, i.e. flow per unit time and the second in the form of total volume flow or total mass flow for a specified duration.

In the measurement of process flow, many properties of the fluids are taken into account, e.g. Reynold number, viscosity, density, specific gravity, compressibility, temperature, pressure, fluid velocity, etc. Type of flow i.e. laminar or turbulent is decided by Reynold number. Reynold number (Rd) is 'Inertia force per unit viscous force' for any fluid flow stream. For laminar flow Rd is upto 2100 and for turbulent flow it can be from 4000 to 1000,000. The velocity of fluid where characteristic of laminar flow just ends and initial level of turbulence is about to start is called critical velocity. Flow characteristics, in general, are full of turbulence in nature.

Measurement rangeability of laminar flow is usually 100:1 but in case of turbulent flow, it is generally restricted to 4:1. Differential pressure created by an orifice is directly proportional to flow rate for laminar flow and accuracy of measurement is significant for whole of the range of scale. But in case of turbulent flow, the relation is completely different. The flow rate here is proportional to the square root of differential pressure. Obviously, the accuracy of measurement cannot be ascertained in the lower range of the measurement scale where the scale gets shrinked with respect to upper scale. Accuracy of measurement for such an arrangement can be expected in the range of ± 0.5 to 1.0%.

3.3.2 Derivation of Universal Flow Formula

Whenever a flow stream passes through a restriction, the effective cross-section of the stream is reduced resulting in increase in velocity and hence the kinetic energy at a corresponding loss of pressure energy. The loss or drop in pressure can be inferred as a measure of flow, refer to the Figure 3.31.

If the fluid stream is parallel to the channel (flow velocity here is considered averaged through the channel) the flow is said to be streamlined or viscous (no motion of fluid stream across the stream). Such flow occurs only at low fluid velocity or with high viscosity liquids.

If the flow velocity in raised the flow pattern gets disturbed and as a result some part of the stream might acquire velocity components across the channel as well. Such change in the transverse velocity is seen from a very small amount to violent swirlings. The type of flow associated with this effect is named as turbulent flow. The swirling action associated with the turbulent flow at times may become so rapid that the flow path of the fluid become helical. This may be called swirling flow. This effect may be minimized by means of dampening vanes installed in the flow path. The most general type of flow is the turbulent one and therefore, the treatments shall be related to that only. The disturbances in case of the turbulent flow where pressure as well as velocity change fast can be minimized by installing a reservoir of large capacity as well. For practical measurement accuracy, flow fluctuation upto the extent of ± 20% however, is acceptable as industry standards.

Refer to Figure 3.32, and assume that 1 ft³ volume of liquid having density ρ₁ = 62.4 lbm/ft³ cross the section indicated in the diagram.

In this assumption mainly three kinds of energy are involved:

  1. (i) Potential Energy = ρ₁Z₁g (where Z₁ is the height with respect to a reference and g is acceleration due to gravity)
  2. (ii) Kinetic Energy = ½ρ₁V₁² (where V₁ is velocity at inlet end)
  3. (iii) Pressure energy = p₁v₁ (where p₁ and v₁ are inlet pressure and volume respectively)

All these variables shall represent that of outlet condition when the subscript used shall be 2.

In above assumption, there may be other forms of energy, e.g. heat energy, friction (which also is ultimately converted to heat itself). The resistance due to friction in fluid flow can also be represented in terms of viscosity, a physical property of liquid in motion. It is expressed as POISE which can be represented as dynes/cm² per unit of velocity gradient across the layer between the pipe and the flowing liquid. A fluid in motion shall have an absolute viscosity of 1 poise when an applied force of 1 dyne tends to move a plane of cross-sectional area 1 cm² at a velocity of 1 cm/sec., parallel to a reference plane placed 1 cm away from the moving plane. The effect of temperature on viscosity is marked. As the temperature is increased, viscosity is decreased for a liquid, but for gas the effect is just the reverse. It is the viscosity which is blessing in disguise that is solely responsible for suppression of pulsation created by pipeline bends and valves.

Consider 1 pound of fluid entering the pipe through inlet and if no accumulation is perceived, the quantity shall cross through the outlet completely. Hence, we can write,

(3.11)1 × Z₁ × g + ½ × 1 × V₁² + p₁v₁ + I₁ = 1 × Z₂ × g + ½ × 1 × V₂² + p₂v₂ + I₂

(where I₁ and I₂ are internal energy at the inlet and the outlet respectively) or, after dividing by the unit mass, Z₁g + V₁²/2 + p₁v₁ + I₁ = Z₂g + V₂²/2 + p₂v₂ + I₂.

If the temperature of the system remains same, then internal energy becomes same. Hence, Z₁g + V₁²/2 + p₁v₁ = Z₂g + V₂²/2 + p₂v₂.

To apply this equation for a liquid, we can assume that v₁ = v₂ = 1/ρ₁ = 1/ρ₂ = 1/ρ (since the liquid is incompressible and hence density and specific volume remain constant). Hence, the equation takes the form Z₁g + V₁²/2 + p₁/ρ = Z₂g + V₂²/2 + p₂/ρ, or Z₁ + V₁²/(2g) + p₁/(ρg) = Z₂ + V₂²/(2g) + p₂/(ρg).

Now since (Z₁ + p₁/(ρg)) − (Z₂ + p₂/(ρg)) is equivalent to h, the differential head created and therefore, (V₂² − V₁²)/(2g) = h, or

(3.12)V₂² − V₁² = 2gh

Based on this equation of liquid flow, the volumetric flow per second Q through the section is given by Q = A₁V₁ = A₂V₂, or V₁ = A₂V₂/A₁. Putting this in equation 3.12 we have V₂² − V₁² = V₂² − V₂² × A₂²/A₁² = V₂²[1 − A₂²/A₁²] = 2gh. Now A₂/A₁ = m (say); hence V₂²(1 − m²) = 2gh, or

(3.13)V₂ = √(2gh)/√(1 − m²)

The expression 1/√(1 − m²) is known as velocity of approach factor and is designated as E, and so the equation 3.13 takes the form of V₂ = E√(2gh), and thus,

(3.14)Q = A₂V₂ = A₂E√(2gh) ft³/sec
(3.15)Mass flow = w = ρQ = ρA₂E√(2gh) pounds/sec

In deriving the above expression, the effect of viscosity was neglected. So the calculated flow shall have error. Due to inertia of the fluid contraction in the stream continues even after crossing the orifice. This effect creates virtual reduction in downstream cross-section at pressure P₂. This also introduces error in calculation. For effecting correction due to these two considerations a constant C (discharge coefficient) is introduced into the flow formula. Hence,

(3.16)Q = CA₂E√(2gh)

and

(3.17)W = ρQ = ρCA₂E√(2gh)

Another important consideration in derivation of flow formula is the Reynold number (Rd). Conceptually, the Reynold number is related to flow pattern of the orifice. If the two orifices of same design when installed properly are geometrically similar (flow pattern same), they are dynamically similar as well and hence have the same Reynold number. Mathematically, the Reynold number is given by:

(3.18)Rd = dVρ/η

where d is the diameter of the throat, V the velocity, ρ the density and η the viscosity.

In case of compressible fluid, i.e. gases, yet another correction for volumetric expansion is applied which can be designated as ε₀. In normal calculation, ε₀ and correction due to Reynold number are taken together as follows: ε₀ × Rd correction = Z. Hence, the final flow formula becomes,

(3.19)Q = CA₂ZE√(2gh)

for gas as well as superheated stream.

3.3.3 Different Types of Primary Elements

1. Orifice Plate

When the measurement has to be carried out on mixed phase fluid, e.g. saturated steam and similar other fluid containing its vapour as well, the existing orifice plate has to be modified. A small hole is made below the main orifice. This hole which is known as weep hole, shall allow the liquid to continuously flow and will not allow the vapour to collect on the upstream side. Figure 3.33 shows three types of orifice plates: concentric, eccentric and segmental.

We know that the orifice plate or any other similar primary element creates a differential pressure in the main pipe which is a measure of the flow rate. This differential pressure has to be measured first so that the flow rate can be inferred. Location of tapping to measure the differential pressure constitutes an important consideration. In this respect, a point in the downstream side of the orifice where velocity of the fluid is maximum (consequently minimum pressure) is called vena contracta. Low pressure tapping location near vena contracta is ideal but is difficult to realize. Because this point gets always changed due to various reasons. This location of vena contracta depends mainly upon fluid velocity through the orifice and the ratio d/D (which is called β ratio). Practically, the velocity of the fluid goes on changing every time. Even d/D ratio goes on changing due to even slight accumulation of foreign particles on the internals of the orifice or the main pipes. However, an ideal location for vena contracta tapping can be found out for a particular set of values of velocity, orifice size and the main pipe size.

In practical application, one pipe diameter (1D) upstream (high-pressure tap) and one half-pipe diameter (0.5D) downstream (low-pressure tap) has been considered the nearest tapping point equivalent to the ideal vena contracta point. This tap is called radius tap. The biggest advantage of this standard tapping is that the orifice plate can be combined along with companion flanges having this type of tapping already fabricated on the flanges. Due to this availability, installation of orifice plate becomes easier. This is a very important point in installation practices because requirement of exact tap location and also their fabrication for accuracy of measurement are so stringent that preparations at shop floor itself for installation is very much advantageous.

Types of tapping used in flowmeter engineering

  1. Flange taps: 1 inch on the upstream side and 1 inch on the downstream side of the orifice location.
  2. Corner taps: 0.02D at the upstream and also 0.02D on the downstream is called corner tap.
  3. Radius taps (D and D/2 tap): This is 1D upstream and D/2 downstream of the orifice location.
  4. Full Flow tap (2.5D and 8D): In this tapping the high pressure end shall be located at 2.5D upstream and low pressure end will be at 8D downstream of the orifice.

All the three tappings can be provided along with the manufactured orifice assembly if ordered. Advantages of orifice plate are low cost, high accuracy, installation and removal easy and reproducibility high. Disadvantages include erratic response when used on viscous fluids, unsuitability for dirty fluids and reduced performance on mixed phase fluids.

2. Pitot Tube

If a tube is put inside the stream of a flowing fluid with one of its open end directly facing the stream, then the fluid abruptly comes to rest and results in conversion of its kinetic energy into pressure energy. This pressure build-up inside the tube shall have a greater value than that of the undisturbed portion of the stream. The difference in pressure can be represented mathematically by:

(3.20)h = V₂²/(2g) − V₁²/(2g)

where h is the difference in pressure, V₁ and V₂ are the two velocities and g is the acceleration due to gravity.

Since V₂ = 0, h = V₁²/(2g), or

(3.21)V₁ = √(2gh)

Practically it is not possible to bring the value of V₂ equal to zero because some part of the flow stream may get deflected across the edge also and therefore, it will affect the value of V₁. A constant C can however, be introduced as Pitot tube constant which shall look after the inaccuracy brought about by the above consideration. The value of C shall be decided by the design factors at the pitot tube. The above equation takes the form,

(3.22)V₁ = C√(2gh)

This equation can be directly applied to gas of low velocity also, but at higher velocity a suitable correction factor (adiabatic correction factor) shall be needed.

The pitot tube which has been shown in Figure 3.34 is an impact device. It does not offer any resistance to the flow of stream practically. It is differential pressure measuring device of which the main entrance faces the pressure as well as flowing energy. The auxiliary or the static entrance measures pressure only and therefore, the arrangement does not have any loss of pressure and produces greatest differential output with respect to any other flow element. But, unfortunately it is not so accurate measuring system since it is a spot measurement device, whereas other flow elements present average flow rate of the fluid, a pitot tube can measure the flow rate at a point only inside the stream.

Main function of a pitot tube is to find the velocity distribution of the flow stream. Such function needs to have the flow stream to be streamlined. The cost of this measurement system is very low.

3. Venturi Tube

Venturi tube has been designed for the services where orifice plate cannot be used easily and with full advangates. The most annoying problem with orifice plate is its large pressure loss. Orifice creates great turbulence in the downstream part of the flow element and the loss of pressure due to this can never be recovered. Since the construction of the venturi tube as shown in Figure 3.35 is such that the inlet section is streamlined, outlet section is not abrupt but gradually expanding and long enough to recover the loss. In this way, the flow can achieve greater velocity without experiencing turbulence. The upstream section is called approach section, the downstream section is called recovery section and the portion lying in between is called throat section. The inlet radius is D/2, the throat radius is d/2, with D the pipe diameter and d the throat diameter; the inlet angle α ≈ 21° and the outlet angle ≈ 5° to 8°.

Since this has low pressure loss characteristics, this can be successfully used in applications where the available differential is low. This is very useful for measurement of flow of slurries as well as suspensions of solids in liquids. This is an expensive instrument. It is supplied along with pipeline itself, that means once installed, the range of application cannot be changed. Also it is difficult to install it on existing pipeline.

Low pressure venturi tubes of dimension less than 50 mm are made of brass and bronze. Larger dimensions having operating pressure upto 15 kg/cm2 are made of cast iron. For higher pressure applications it is made of cast steel with a liner of gun metal to minimize the erosion. For making ease of installation the entry portion of it is designed to have the same dimension as the pipeline.

4. Flow Nozzle

A flow nozzle is a modified version of a venturi tube and also very much similar to an orifice plate. The entry part of the flow nozzle is gently curved in such a way that it is tangent to a short parallel section as shown in Figure 3.36. Almost all of its characteristics lie in between venturi tube and orifice plate. The pressure recovery is just like venturi tube where as the expenses of fabrication and installation are slightly more than orifice plate but bearable comparatively. It is generally made of strong metal since this is installed on normally higher pressure services. The dimensions shown in the figure are d, 1.5d, D, 0.6d and 0.1D.

Gun metal, stainless steel (AISI 304, 316 or 316 L varieties) or monel metal are some of the materials that a flow nozzles are made of. For steam flow measurement services, this is the only primary flow element which is used invariably. Its discharge coefficient applies over a wide range of pressure ratio. This is less expensive than a venturi tube. The reinstallation on existing pipeline is not difficult. The pressure loss is less than an orifice plate.

5. Variable Areameters or Rotameter

Rotameter was invented by ROTA who was the main person behind this principle of flow measurement. Rotameter is a simple, low flow measurement equipment. The accuracy is however poor but very useful for purgemeter where accuracy is not a deciding factor.

Theory of Rotameter

  • Q = Flow rate of fluid through rotameter (m3/hr)
  • C = Discharge of coefficient (depends upon float shape and Rd)
  • A = Sectional area between tube and float (m2)
  • A_f = Pressed area of float (m2)
  • g = Acceleration due to gravity (m/hr2)
  • r_f and r₀ = sp. wt. of float and fluid respectively (kg/m3)
  • p₁ and p₂ = Upstream and downstream pressure (kg/m2)
  • V = Average fluid velocity (m/hr)
  • h = Pressure differential across float
  • L_f = Length of cylindrical float (m)
  • μ = Viscosity of fluid (Poise)
  • R_d = Reynolds number
  • D_eq = Equivalent diameter of the annular space on top of the float for maximum flow (m)
  • D = Diameter of tube at maximum flow mark (m)
  • d_f = Diameter of float = Diameter of tube at zero (0) flow mark
  • Weight of the float = V_f r_f
  • Upward thrust due to the weight of fluid displaced by the float = V_f r₀
  • Differential force acting on the surface of the float (upper side and lower side) = (p₁ − p₂) A_f

Refer to Figure 3.37 where the float hangs in equilibrium under the actions of above forces. At equilibrium, V_f r_f = (p₁ − p₂) A_f + V_f r₀, or

(3.23)(p₁ − p₂) = (V_f/A_f)(r_f − r₀)
(3.24)v² = 2gh
(3.25)h = (p₁ − p₂)/r₀

Hence,

(3.26)V = √[2g(V_f/A_f)((r_f/r₀) − 1)]

Based on above, the flow rate Q shall be given by:

(3.27)Q = C A √[2g(V_f/A_f)((r_f/r₀) − 1)]

This expression gives the proportionality relations:

(3.28)Q ∝ [(r_f − r₀)/r₀]^(1/2) or W ∝ [r₀(r_f − r₀)]^(1/2)

If we assume the float to be cylindrical, L_f = V_f/A_f. The Reynolds number R_d shall be given by:

(3.29)R_d = (D_eq × V × r₀)/μ = 3.54 W/(μ D_eq)
Here D_eq is in mm and W is in kg/hr.
(3.30)R_d = (3.54 × Q × r₀)/(μ D_eq)

The discharge constant C for an areameter instrument varies in between 0.6 and 0.8. Another important point is that for constant mass flow rate, r_f = 2r₀, which can be obtained by differentiating expression 3.28 with respect to r₀ and equating the same to zero as follows: dW/dr₀ = (r_f − 2r₀) = 0 (Since W is constant), or

(3.31)r_f = 2r₀

A rotameter is generally calibrated and applied for a particular fluid. If the fluid changes, a correction factor shall apply which is given below:

(3.32)Q₂/Q₁ = √(r₁/r₂)

where Q₁ and Q₂ are flow rates of original fluid and the new fluid respectively. Similarly, r₁ and r₂ are the specific weights of original fluid and the new fluid respectively. For changing capacity of rotameter, the float has to be changed only. The shape of the original float is kept same, the material of construction is changed (material of higher or lower specific weight) only. The capacity of practical rotameter ranges from a low value of 2 to 20 litres per hour of waterflow upto 1500 litres per hour of airflow. The nominal pipe size is from 3 mm to 150 mm.

6. Magnetic Flowmeter

Magnetic flowmeter works on the principle of Faradays law of induction. The relative motion of conductor with respect to the magnetic field located at right angle to one another induces an emf. The induced emf can be collected by means of suitable arrangement as shown in Figure 3.38.

The actual instrument consists of a portion of an insulating pipe along with a pair of electrodes placed at the opposite end of the pipe. The electrodes are mounted flush with respect to the inner wall of the pipe. The main coils are wound arround the pipe so that the magnetic field is produced on a plane which is mutually perpendicular to the axis of the instrument and the electrodes. The flowing fluid is considered here a bundle of parallel conductors moving under the influence of magnetic field with different velocities that change across the entire cross-section of the pipe. The emf induced by the moving imaginary conductors is in fact the average emf and the total instantaneous voltage thus produced is directly proportional to the average velocity of the flowing fluid. This represents a true picture of volume flow rate.

(3.33)e = d v B

where V is the average velocity of the fluid crossing the electrode; B is the magnetic flux density and d is internal diameter of measuring tube.

Since the output signal is proportional to the average velocity, it does not matter whether the flow is laminar or turbulent. The measurement is independent of viscosity, density, temperature and pressure. It is also independent of the conductivity of the fluid once a minimum conductivity value has been reached. The minimum value varies from 20 micro mho down to 0.1 micro mho, depending upon the capacity of the interfacing equipment to handle various signal strength.

The measurement tube is given ceramic lining to have superior corrosion resistance and mechanical strength. Electrodes (wetted type) become leak-free due to minimum clearance between electrode and the tube-wall. The non-wetted type capacitance electrodes are installed outside the ceramic measurement tube. The available sizes of magnetic flowmeters are from 0.1 inch to 100 inches diameter with wide choice of material of construction. Obtainable accuracy is from ± 0.5 to ± 2% and rangeability from 20 : 1 to 30 : 1.

On industrial application point of view, following are the salient features of magnetic flowmeter:

  1. There is absolutely no obstruction to the flow of fluid.
  2. There has been minimum possible loss of pressure across the instrument.
  3. Measurement of difficult fluids like slurries, corrosives, abrasives and others is easy.
  4. Since the measurement is based upon average velocity concept, piping geometry is not critical.
  5. Measurement of flow in both the directions is possible by reversing the connections only.
  6. Viscosity, density, temperature, pressure and also turbulence have absolutely no effect on the accuracy.

However, certain precautions also have to be followed:

  1. Conductivity of the flowing fluid must be between 0.1 and 20 μ (micromho).
  2. The instrument must be completely filled during the metering.
  3. There must not be any gas bubble entrained otherwise error in measurement will result.
  4. Electrodes must be cleaned periodically.

7. Turbine Flowmeter

Turbine flowmeter consists of turbine blades fixed on the periphery of a wheel and this assembly is free to rotate inside a flow tube about its centreline as shown in Figure 3.39. The velocity of the fluid flowing inside the flow tube creates tangential force and acts on to the blades resulting in a rotatory motion. The speed of the blade is directly proportional to the rate of flow. A magnetic pick-up coil is arranged on the flow tube in such a way that as the blade crosses the pick-up coil, one pulse of A.C. voltage is induced which represents a fixed/known quantity of the fluid crossing through. A suitable counter can count these flow pulses and a totalized flow can be presented very easily.

Accuracy of such instrument depends upon the relationship between the construction of the blade and the flow pattern. The number of pulses generated per gallon of flow is a factor which describes the capacity and the flow characteristics of a turbine meter. This is called K-factor and the related curves which is called K-factor curve has been shown in Figure 3.40. The curves indicate satisfactory relationship between flow and other variables as shown in the diagram. The accuracy is from ± 0.25% to ± 0.5%.

Rangeability of measurement is in between 10 : 1 and 20 : 1 for average flow ranges in industrial application. For military application it can go upto 100 : 1. The average repeatability happens to be ± 0.25% but can attain higher value upto ± 0.02% as well for special designs if required.

In practice, engineers face a lot of problems in maintaining the turbine flowmeters. The entry of a rotating measurement equipment into the pipeline presents some obvious maintenance problems. The complete rotating mechanism faces the attack of dirts, erosive and corrosive actions of the fluids, solid particles entrained in the fluid. Another important point is that the bearing of the rotor blade gets lubrication from the lubricating property of the fluid. All fluids may not have that much lubricating property and therefore, for those fluids, highly polished surfaces are used. Some non-metallic materials are used for fabricating the non-lubricating bearings. These are Teflon, carbon graphite, ceramics, etc. In fact, almost all the maintenance practices that are followed for maintenance of centrifugal and reciprocating pumps also are followed for the maintenance of turbine flowmeter. It is advised that turbine flow meters should not be used for dirty liquids to avert the maintenance problems.

8. Target Flowmeter

The target flowmeter consists of a primary flow element and a force-balance transmitter. The primary element in this arrangement is a disc-shaped structure called target located in the middle of the fluid stream. The target forms an annular orifice at the centreline position of the pipe. The equation of flow through the target meter is given by:

(3.34)Q = K√F

where Q is the rate of flow, F is the force acting on the target ranging from 2 to 6 pounds and K is proportionality constant.

The integral force-balance transmitter converts this force into pneumatic or electrical output which is used for recording as well as control purposes.

The installation of target type flowmeter becomes simpler because primary sensing element and the transmitter are combined in a single unit. The primary sensing element is contained in a wafer to be mounted between companion flanges on the pipeline. In this installation 3-way valve manifold is not required. Since this type of flowmeter is used for viscous (heavy oil) flow measurement, frequent inspection and cleaning is required to be done under maintenance schedule. The removal of the target flowmeter from the pipeline for such inspection and cleaning become easier from the companion flanges.

The principle of operation of the integral force-balance transmitter has been shown in Figure 3.41. The force is exerted on the target by the flowing fluid and this force acts on the force bar. This force is transmitted through the flexure to the range bar pivoted on the range wheel. Movement of the range bar operates the standard flapper-nozzle system resulting in proportional output pneumatic signal. The feedback bellows does the balancing work for keeping the force bar under equilibrium.

The accuracy of this instrument is upto ± 0.5%. This is available in sizes of 2, 3 and 4 inches pipeline. The ratio of target to pipeline diameters is from 0.5 to 0.8. It can handle a pressure and temperature of 1500 psig and 750°C respectively.

Another type of flowmeter which is also called drag type flowmeter, works on the same principle as target flowmeter. In this design the impact force acting on the target is sensed by a strain gage-bridge circuitry. The arrangement has been shown in Figure 3.42. The design and installation are such that the transmitter unit can be taken out from the pipeline for periodic inspection and cleaning.

The drag force F of this design is given by:

(3.35)F = C A ρ (V²/(2g))

where C is the drag coefficient; A is area of the sensing part; ρ is the fluid density and V²/(2g) is the velocity head.

The accuracy obtainable of this design is between ± 0.5% and ± 3%. These are available in the sizes from 0.5 inch to 2 inches in tube design and from 4 inches to 60 inches for flange mounting design. It can handle a pressure and temperature of 5000 psig and 600°C respectively.

9. Annubar

Annubar is the most advanced primary flow sensor used for liquid, gas and steam flow measurement. It is designed to produce a differential pressure which is proportional to flow. Available in size ranging from 1 inch to 360 inches, the annubar provides high as well as long-term accuracy, low permanent pressure loss, low installed and operating cost.

Annubar, made of AISI 316 stainless steel consists of mainly four parts: (i) Sensor, (ii) Tube, (iii) Rear Port, and (iv) Instrument head (Refer Figure 3.43).

The sensor with four high pressure impact ports faces the upstream side of the flow and senses the impact pressure created by the flow velocity. The four high pressure and equal cross-sectional areas of the flow stream averages the impact pressure. The tube is acted upon by this impact pressure and after interpolation transmits the average pressure to the measuring system. The rear port which is located on the downstream side of the flow medium, senses the low pressure. The difference between these two pressures is proportional to flow rate. The instrument head which is the top portion of the arrangement transmits their differential pressure to a transmitter, recorder or controller for further processing. The sensor design is such that it produces a uniform flow pattern around the sensor resulting in high accuracy and repeatability.

Hydraulic equation of flow for annubar sensors is given by:

(3.36)Q_l = K A √(2g·(d.p.)/ρ)

and

(3.37)Q_g = K A y √(2g·(d.p.)/ρ)

where Q_l and Q_g are actual volume flow rates for liquid and gas respectively in ft3/sec; A is the cross-sectional area of the pipe in sq. ft; g is gravitational acceleration in ft/sec2; K is discharge coefficient; d.p. is differential pressure lbf/ft2; ρ is the density of the fluid, lbm/ft3 and y is the expansion factor of the gas.

Accuracy of measurement by Annubar which includes hysteresis as well is ± 1%. Unlike orifice plates, surface wear, nicks and burrs on sensor ports have negligible effects on the accuracy of Annubar. This results in maintenance of high degree and long-term stability on accuracy. Repeatability of Annubar measurement have been tested to be ± 0.1%.

10. Vortex Flowmeter

Vortex flowmeter measures flow rate on the principle of KARMAN vortices produced by vortex shredder. The vortex shredder is an especially shaped (trapezoidal-like) non-streamlined bluff body submerged completely in the fluid flow path as shown in Figure 3.44.

Karman vortices can be conceived as oscillating motion created by a flag fluttering in a clear wind. This is a natural process which can always be seen in a flowing stream whenever a bluff body is put inside the stream. The frequency of these vortices bear a definite proportionality relation with flow rate. If the flow velocity is v and the width of bluff body directly facing the flow stream is D, then the frequency F of the vortex is given by the expression:

(3.38)F = St·v/D

where St is Strouhal number. Strouhal number depends upon the shape of the bluff body. It is numerically equal to ratio between the vortex produced interval (L) and width of bluff body (D) as shown in Figure 3.45.

If the Strouhal number is constant, then the velocity of flow v, can be found out easily by counting the number of vortices only. The Strouhal number can be considered constant only when the Reynolds number (R_d) is within a specified range. As is indicated in Figure 3.46, the specified range of R_d is wide and hence the Strouhal number can be taken as constant. The flow rate measured by the equation 3.38 is independent of fluid pressure, temperature, density and viscosity. In some cases compensation for pressure and temperature may be required when the flow rate is measured with reference to some standard.

In Figure 3.46 the St axis is marked 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3; the measurable range of R_d is from 5 × 10³ to 7 × 10⁶, while the normal operating range is from 2 × 10⁴ to 7 × 10⁶.

The bluff body is made of AISI 316 stainless steel material. This is lifted each time the vortices are produced. These lifts create measurable stress which is detected by piezoelectric transducer element located inside the bluff body. The electric signals produced is sent to the transmitter where the flow rate is calculated.

Accuracy of vortex flowmeter is ± 1% of indicated value which is superior to orifice flow meters. Since the output of vortex flowmeter is directly proportional to flow rate, scale is linear which is in contrast with the square root scale of orifice flowmeter. There is no pressure loss as is found with orifice flowmeter.

In another design, the vortex shedding frequency is sensed by a pair of thermistors embedded in the upstream face. They are heated electrically to a temperature above that of the flowing fluid and sense the cooling effect of the vortex shedding by changing temperature/resistance at the vortex shedding frequency which is proportional to flow rate.

11. Mass Flowmeter

General type of engineering flowmeters are popularly known as volumetric flowmeters in the process industries. But the fact is that the control of processes and therefore, measurement of process variables as well are more related to mass balance equations. Due to this reason, mass measurement seems to be more logical for process control than volume measurement. For proper control of the processes, the mass is inferred from the volume flow rate with respect to pressure and temperature. The work becomes more tedious in case of gas flow system than with the liquid flow system.

Mass measurement is done directly because this is the basic and ideal standard which is not affected at all by changes in temperature, pressure, density, viscosity and conductivity. Design features of mass flowmeter are based upon a natural phenomenon called coriolis effect. It is the coriolis effect that the curvature appearance of a freely moving object on the surface of the earth can be attributed to. The instrument so designed is called coriolis/Gyroscopic Effect Mass Flowmeter.

Determination of exact amount of mass flowing through the meter is done by Newtons second law of motion. Figure 3.47 shows a flow tube assembly.

(13) Positive Displacement Meters (Oval-Gear Meter)

Positive displacement meters split the flow passage into small packets of known volume based primarily upon the contour of the splitting element. This is essentially a mechanical type of measuring equipment. The moving parts, which are oval-shaped gear rotors, are placed just in the path of the flow stream and physically separate the flowing fluid into small increments. Drive power to move the rotor is extracted from the flow stream itself and is reflected in terms of drop in pressure between the inlet and the outlet. The clearance between the moving rotor gear and the stationary part should be minimum possible whereas the path through which the liquid passes should have maximum possible length. The accuracy of such design increases further as the size of the equipment increases.

In actual construction, two oval-geared rotors are geared together and thereby maintain a fixed position relative to each other. These two gears are allowed to rotate in opposite directions inside the housing made of heavy cast steel or cast iron as shown in Figure 3.48. A fixed volume of liquid is displaced with respect to every revolution. A registering unit, essentially a mechanical system, is connected to one of the rotor gears through another separate gear system. Registering range is available of more than 60,000 litres/minute and the instrument is available in sizes from 50 to 600 mm.

In fertilizer plants as well as in power generating units, heavy oil having very high viscosity is used. In fertilizer units this oil is partially oxidised for producing hydrogen and carbon monoxide which are further processed to make ammonia and urea. In power generating units the oil is used as a flame support for boiler. In both units the fluid is metered by the oval-gear instrument described above. Due to the peculiar nature of the oil, which tends to solidify below 60 °C, other measurement systems fail and therefore the oval-gear meter is the obvious solution.

(14) Open-Channel Flowmeter

Flow of water for irrigation, flow of waste and sewage, and restricted flow of water from a dam are some examples of open-channel flow systems. Measurement of open-channel flow systems utilises head meter techniques but the structure, characteristics and readout arrangements are different.

Primary elements used for open-channel flow systems are weirs and flumes. Two types of weirs are in use: V-notch and rectangular notch, as shown in Figures 3.49 (a) and (b). A combination type popularly known as cippoletti weir is shown in Figure 3.50; the end contractions have been set at a 4 : 1 angle. Rectangular weirs are used for larger flows. The angle of the V-notch varies and the most common design angles are 30°, 60° and 90°; V-notch weirs are employed for lower flows.

When weirs are employed for open-channel flow measurements, the entire picture is as indicated in Figure 3.51. The profile of water over the weir, called Nappe, has an aerated area. This is the basic requirement of a weir measurement system for accuracy; if the design and installation of the weir is not perfect, the aeration area will not be sufficient.

As far as computations of flow are concerned, the following equations govern the relationship between the flow and the measured head. For V-notch weir:

(3.39)Q_v = 2.48 tan(0.5 α) H^(5/2)

where Q_v is the flow rate (ft³/sec) through the V-notch, α is the angle of notch (30°, 60° or 90°) and H is the measured head (feet).

Similarly, for a rectangular weir:

(3.40)Q_r = 3.33 (χ − 0.2 H) H^(3/2)

where Q_r is the flow rate (ft³/sec) through the rectangular weir and χ is the crest length (feet).

For a cippoletti weir:

(3.41)Q_c = 3.367 χ H^(3/2)

where Q_c is the flow rate (ft³/sec) through the cippoletti weir.

Flumes

This is a special type of venturi flume designed by Parshall and is therefore named Parshall Flume. Figure 3.52 shows it placed on the surface in the flow stream. A flume has all the advantages of a flow element that a venturi tube enjoys: the high velocity flushes the solids, wastes and other entrained materials, and it can be easily fabricated from available materials.

The accuracy obtainable with flumes may not be very high (only ± 2%) but the measurement is very reliable. This is an ideal flow measurement system for waste and sewages. The equation of flow is mainly dependent upon the width of the throat L (feet) and the head H. The equation applicable for throat width 1 to 8 feet is given by:

(3.42)Q_f = 4 L H (1.522 L)^0.026

When the width of the throat is more than 8 feet the equation changes slightly and becomes:

(3.43)Q_f = (2.5 + 3.69 L) H^1.6

3.4 Measurement of Level

All processing plants have a number of drums, tanks, vessels and reservoirs to store finished products or to process intermediate products for continuous operations. Hence accurate measurement of the contents of these vessels is very important and critical. Measurement of content in terms of level is the obvious choice. Level can be defined as the interface existing between two media: one is the liquid proper whose level is to be measured and the other is air when the tank is open, or the vapour of the liquid itself when the tank is closed.

The most common industrial method is the sight glass/gage glass, which works on the principle of the U-tube manometer. Figures 3.53 (a) and (b) show the glass for open tanks as well as for closed, pressurised tanks. As the level in the process vessel changes, the level in the transparent tube also changes and is read on the indicator scale. Isolation valves are provided for carrying out maintenance work on the sight glass indicator.

Therefore, the level becomes one of the four basic variables (pressure, temperature, flow, level) that are most important for any unit operation activity run in any process plant. In some cases two immiscible liquids also create an interface. In steam generating boiler, presence of saturated steam along with pressurized boiler feed water in the drum makes a dynamic interface of steam and water bubbles. The measurement of level constitutes the measurement of this interface carefully.

The most common industrial methods available for measurement of level are following:

(ii) Float and cable

Measurement of liquid level of an open tank can be done by the mechanical arrangement consisting of float and cable. The float rides on the surface of the liquid and changes its position according to the rise and fall of the liquid level. The cable which is essentially a metallic chain type of article and is connected to a weight of one end and to the float at the other end as shown in Figure 3.59.

The application range at such measurement system is upto 50 feet or even more. Almost all stable process fluids available in a process plant or even the sanitary water supply overhead tanks use this system of level measurement very effectively. The most striking feature of this system is that change in density of the fluid does not have any effect on the measurement so long the float is on floating condition on the fluid. The system is very simple in design and inexpensive.

Limitations imposed on this system are that the system fluid should be clean. In case of measurement of level of heavy oil (viscous oil), the requirement for the system is that sufficient fluidity should be maintained all the time otherwise the oil may stick to the float making it insensitive.

(iii) Purge system

The basic principle behind purge system of level measurement is dependent on generation of back pressure. When a clean fluid, dry air invariably is allowed to bubble out against a hydrostatic head, the head imparts a resistance to the flow of air thereby creating a back pressure. This back pressure is exactly proportional to the head and therefore, can be a measure of level of that liquid.

The purging air flows through a purge rotameter, especially designed low flow rotameter. There is an arrangement for adjustment of the quantity of air flow through the purge rotameter. The knob made for this purpose is in fact a needle valve which is operated by hand and in this way the amount of flow can be very minutely adjusted. The airflow has to be adjusted such that at 100% level minimum possible air should bubble out. This is normally one bubble per second which is a satisfactory adjustment. This is required in order to have maximum accuracy in measurement and hence minimum error. The arrangement has been shown in Figure 3.60.

The most striking advantage of this measurement system is that it is the most inexpensive method for level measurement for corrosive as well as dirty fluid containing solid particles. The only disadvantage that can be expected of this method is that the bubble pipe has to be replaced from time-to-time after it gets completely corroded or eroded.

(iv) Displacer/buoyancy type

The basic principle behind the buoyancy type level measurement system is that when a body is immersed in a liquid, the upthrust acted upon the body equals the weight of the liquid displaced by it. A cylindrical body called displacer or float is arranged to be submerged inside the liquid of which the level is to be measured. The displacer is in fact suspended in a float chamber located just outside the tank containing the liquid of which the level is to be measured. The float chamber is connected to the main tank as shown in Figure 3.61.

The upthrust so created shall be directly proportional to the level on the float upto which the liquid rises in the float chamber. In this way, the apparent weight of the float will go on decreasing as the liquid level will go on rising and vice versa. When the liquid is not present near the float, the complete weight of the float is taken by the free end of the float rod. This results in creation of a turning action given by product of weight of float and length of float rod. This is applied to the end of torque tube assembly. This turning action is balanced by the torsional stress created in the torque tube. Hence, the angle of shaft-turn shall be proportional to apparent weight of the float. As the liquid level rises near the float, the apparent weight of the float shall get reduced and hence the turning action on to the torque tube. The torsional stress set up in the torque tube shall rotate the shaft to a new angular position which shall be proportional to the apparent weight of the float.

The float is made up of stainless steel, hastelloy or k-monel and is connected to the float rod through ball and socket joint. The range of measurement is determined by the length of the float. The amount of upthrust is dependent upon the density of the liquid. Span of the upthrust or buoyant force Fb (newton) is given by the equation:

(3.44)F_b = 9.8 × 10⁻³ ρ V (l/L)

where ρ is the specific gravity of the liquid; V is the volume of displacer (cm³); l and L are working and total length of displacer respectively (mm).

Torque tube/displacer method of level measurement is superior. It can be used in case of high pressure vessel application very effectively. It has been observed that effective vertical motion of the float is very small with respect to change in level. Accuracy is not affected by change in temperature and hence density, because increased dimension of float is selected for use at elevated temperature.

To measure the level (interface) between two immiscible liquids having different specific gravity, displacer method is almost invariably employed. In that case, it is obvious that the difference in their specific gravities shall determine the change in upthrust since the level of interface changes through the range of float.

(v) Differential pressure type

Differential created by a rising level of a liquid with respect to fixed reference is the measure of level. In case of an open tank level measurement, the reference becomes the atmospheric pressure itself. On the other hand, the static pressure inside a closed tank becomes the reference for measurement of level of a closed tank. Thus, we observe that level measurement in fact becomes measurement of differential pressure created by the level head.

Therefore, a differential pressure transmitter (D.P. cell) shall be connected to the system in such a way that its low pressure side (L.P.) shall be left open to atmosphere and the high pressure side (H.P.) shall be connected to bottom entry to the open tank. In case with closed tank, the H.P. side shall be connected to the upper portion i.e. vapour space in the closed tank whereas the L.P. side shall be connected to the lower portion of the tank having liquid in its proper phase.

The measurement is accurate provided there is no density variation in the liquid. In case density changes, correction can be applied by multiplying the measured/calculated density change by the head.

The differential pressure transmitter has the same design feature as the pressure transmitter, we have already discussed earlier in this chapter. Even then, it has been shown in its simplest form in Figure 3.62. The instrument is calibrated with respect to the span of the level measurement.

The installation arrangement for open tank as well as closed tank level measurement schemes have been shown in Figure 3.63. The 3-valve manifold is used for putting the D.P. transmitter into operation systematically.

First the equalizer valve is kept open and then H.P. and L.P. sides are open one-by-one. Equalizer valve keeps the diaphragm of the transmitter at equal pressure from both sides and avoids damage of diaphragm due to unbalance of suddenly applied pressure. After opening H.P. and L.P. valves fully, equalizer valve is closed slowly and in this way the transmitter comes into operation. Two drain valves have been installed for flushing the impulse lines of the level instrument tapping for removal of any choking.

In some applications it is required that the liquid which may be of toxic nature may not be allowed to come into the direct contact of the transmitter internals. In that case a sealing liquid is advised to be used, but the liquid to be selected must have specific gravity greater than that of the liquid under measurement. It should be immiscible as well. For instance, in case of fuel oil of high viscosity, glycerene may be used as sealing liquid. A better solution is that the D.P. transmitter is itself modified and made suitable for the application. The portion which comes into direct contact with the liquid is having metallic diaphragm for isolation. For such a design refinement, two types of diaphragm can be applied. One is called extended diaphragm and the other one is called flat diaphragm as shown in Figure 3.64. The internal arrangements have been shown in Figure 3.65.

The extended diaphragm is installed on to the nozzle made on the tank bottom. This type of design removes dead cavities. This design is suitable for liquid which changes its phase due to change in temperature very easily. The flush design that is flat diaphragm design is installed directly to the tank isolation valve. In this installation, the low pressure (L.P.) connection is used as the case may be depending upon whether the installation is for open tank or closed tank.

(vi) Capacitance type

Capacitance type level measurement systems are in use in almost every process industry or in any storage depot. Some of the applications are enumerated like level measurement of a coal bunker, level measurement of urea solution (fertilizer) as well as prilled urea (solid state) placed in silo, level measurement of food grains in grain storage depots. The most important fact is that the performance of the system is not at all affected by either the properties of the material under measurement or the process variables.

The basic working of the system depends upon the variation in capacitance value when the material is filled up slowly into the tank. For actual measurement of capacitance, a probe is installed on the tank when the tank is empty as shown in Figure 3.66. In this condition there will be an initial capacitance value between the capacitance probe and the tank wall (say C0) which shall be consisting of capacitance of the probe (say Cp) and capacitance between probe and empty tank (say Ct).

The value of Cp depends upon the material of the probe (may be teflon or others) and remains constant for a particular design of probe. On the other hand, the value of Ct is governed by following equation:

(3.45)Ct = lρ / d₀

where l is the effective length of the probe, ρ is the dielectric constant of the material between the probe and the tank wall, and d0 is the distance measured between the probe and the tank wall.

As we observe here that the length of the probe and the distance between the probe and the tank wall are almost constant for a particular application, the capacitance value Ct is directly proportional to the dielectric constant, i.e. Ct αρ.

For keeping the d0 constant, the capacitance probe is installed rigidly on top of the tank. As the material is filled up in the tank, slowly it touches the lower part of the probe and thereafter it starts covering the probe. Due to covering of the material in the air gap of the tank the value of Ct starts increasing. When the increase in Ct becomes more than the value of Cp (which is practically 1% of Ct), the electronic circuit starts its operation. The electronic circuit may be of indicating type or an on-off switch type depending upon the requirement of the application.

The probe is an active element in the entire system. As the level of the material inside the tank increases, the indication of the level is detected by the electronic circuit by the help of change in Ct. The probe rod and the bush are made of stainless steel and are fully insulated with teflon (PTFE). The flexible rope which fastens the probe is made of galvanized steel with a coating of polyethelene. It is obvious that the probe material, insulation material and also the probe-length shall be selected for individual application as the case may be.

The electronic circuit consists of mainly of parts: (a) probe head having oscillator connected to capacitance probe, and (b) control unit having mains transformer, rectifier, sensing amplifier and relay (for on-off level meter) or transducer (for indicating type level meter) the entire system has been indicated in Figure 3.67.

Oscillator is boxed in a housing made of aluminium casting and is screwed on the top portion of the probe. Since this is installed in adverse environment in the plant, it has to be protected from dust, heat, moisture and other disturbing agents. Therefore, the circuit is fabricated on glass epoxy board and then encapsulated in a plastic pot filled with resin. This type of design shall waithstand temperature of 100C. If the temperature is more than 100C, the oscillator portion has to be installed away from the probe head where the temperature of the environment is within the limit. The frequency output of the unit is 100 kHz signal which is obtained as a result of change in capacitance due to change in level. The sensing amplifier amplifies this output and sends to operate the relay unit or transducer unit. The power supply unit supplies 10V D.C. power to all the individual unit for its operation.

(vii) Magnetic level gage

Magnetic level gage is in fact from the series of direct visual indicating type of level instrument. In process applications where toxic, corrosive or otherwise difficult liquid is handled, normal type of level gage glass is not acceptable. Magnetic level gage becomes obvious choice in such application.

The basic design consists of a non-magnetic denser float (cylindrical shape) as shown in Figure 3.68. A small magnet of the same shape is embedded inside the float so that the entire combination floats on the liquid and moves up and down according to the change in level as shown.

For obtaining visual indication outside the float chamber two types of designs are in practical use as shown in Figure 3.69 and 3.70. In the first design, the inner magnet imbedded with the float moves with the level of the liquid while the outer magnet of the float indicates the level when it moves along with the float. The other design possesses a wafer indicator which is attached outside the float chamber as shown.

The wafer indicator contains twin colour small wafers, one side of which is black and the other side is yellow. These wafers are so located that when the magnetic float moves, the wafers flips over 180 and in this way it changes the colour from yellow to black. For indication purpose, the black wafer indicates liquid level and yellow wafer indicates vapour space. Pressure and temperature ratings of these level measurement systems are upto 250 kg/cm2 and 400C respectively. These can be used cryogenic temperature also if required.

(viii) Nucleonic type

Nucleonic type of level measurement technique depends upon the radiation phenomena of radioactive element. The radioactive element disintegrates in due course and emits α, β and γ (Alpha, Beta and Gamma) rays. Out of these three radiations, γ consists of electromagnetic waves and is of prime concern for use in level measurement technique. The penetration power of γ is 10,000 times more than that of α and 100 times more than β and since γ rays cannot be deflected by electric or magnetic field as is the case with α and β rays, γ is the most suitable ray for level measurement system.

The two most important γ ray sources are cobalt (Co 60) and cesium (Cs 137). The half life of Co 60 is 5.3 years (rate of decay is 12.3% per year) and the half life of Cs 137 is 30 years but rate of decay is only 2.3% per year and therefore Co 60 is selected for level measurement source unit. This is because for level measurement works, high rate of decay indicates high emission rate which is required for high degree of penetration.

Nucleonic method of level measurement technique proves superior with respect to other methods where the service conditions are such that the liquid is not approachable or the equipment is of complicated nature not allowing other instruments to be installed or accurate measurement is not that important. Such situations prevail in almost all chemical industries. Some of the examples are hot tanks, feed coal or other bunkers, evaporators located at higher elevation, tankers (static or moving inside the plant). This method of measurement is not at all affected by corrosive atmosphere, reactivity to other chemicals, variable grain structures or voids present naturally in mixed phase liquids.

The neuclic radiation from source flask when allowed to traverse through the material (solid or liquid of which level is to be measured) gets attenuated. The change in radiation strength due to attenuation is detected by detector unit located on the opposite side of the level measurement tank. The control unit supplies power and receives the signal output proportional to level for indication.

The source flask in which Co 60 source is kept for radiation is designed as recommended by Bhabha Atomic Research Centre or other international regulatory authority recognized by the government. All inner parts including collimator are made of stainless steel. The outer casing is made of mild steel and is completely filled with lead (practically no effect of radiation on lead) for shielding the effect of radiations. The radiation source is put in an especially fabricated capsule and when in operation, a sharp pencil-type ray comes out and after attenuation through the tank content is detected at detector unit.

The detector unit consists of halogen quenched Geiger-Mueller tube which operates at 500 volt D.C. The amplifier unit for amplifying the signal received at the detector unit is located inside the detector itself. The detector unit is enclosed in a metal jacket for circulating the cooling water as shown in Figure 3.71. The output from the detector unit is connected to control unit having arrangement for discrete or continuous indication. The control unit is located at convenient place away from the source and detector unit.

Safety consideration for this type of installation is important. The radiation effect is hazardous for human beings and other living beings and therefore, long term exposure for the personnel is decided before hand by the user in consultation with the appropriate regulatory authority. The area is cordoned off and entry is prohibited by providing a DANGER mark as shown in Figure 3.72.
