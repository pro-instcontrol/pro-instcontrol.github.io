Hai loại phần mềm tính cỡ van cơ bản Two basic types of sizing software

There are two basic types of sizing software programs. The first lets you pick the valve type and then gives you the specifics selected from the database, such as rated Cv, FL, and Fd values, so you can do the right sizing calculations. The second type lets you enter only the flow conditions, and then the computer calculates the Cv and selects the right valve, usually the best economical choice.

There are problems with both methods. In the first case, you are forced to select a valve type and vendor before you have a chance to get a competitor's bid or evaluate a more economical valve type. In the second case, your sizing program must start with some “assumed” parameters, such as FL and Fd values. The computer then chooses the best valve type and size to fit this application. However, the chosen FL or Fd may be different from the assumed one and, as a result, you have to repeat the sizing calculations—not an ideal situation. Nevertheless, computers can be great time-savers, especially when it comes to calculating the effects of reducer losses or viscosity. Both require otherwise time-consuming iterations (repeat calculations in which you have to assume a valve size or Cv value, do your calculation, realize the assumed valve size was too small, pick a larger valve size or Cv value, and then start over, usually several times).

Những gì phần mềm cơ bản tính toán What basic programs calculate

Basic computer sizing software usually can calculate the required Cv value for maximum, normal, and minimum flow conditions. The software indicates whether the valve will cavitate or flash and can calculate the aerodynamic or hydrodynamic valve noise.

Do not be impressed by the computer's ability to calculate a Cv number to the sixth decimal place. This is meaningless. Correct results depend on an error-free program and selecting the right input data. I always recommend using a slide rule or a simple handheld calculator to double-check the computer's result, especially if you use brand-new software.

Chương trình tốt hơn: lựa chọn van và tính cỡ bộ truyền động Better programs: selection and actuator sizing

Better programs will go further and select the right valve size and type for your flow condition based on such required parameters as end connections (flanged, welded, screwed, etc.); leakage requirements (Class I, II, IV, V, or VI); whether the valve should be corrosion resistant (no, mild, very); the flow characteristic (equal percentage, linear); and perhaps others. Based on these factors, the computer will search the database and select the most suitable and economical choice. Some will even calculate the required actuator size and bench range. However, do not expect the program from Company A to select a valve from Company B. After all, there are limits to benevolence.

Chương trình tiên tiến: đặc tính lắp đặt và độ ổn định vòng điều khiển Advanced programs: installed characteristic and loop stability

More advanced programs go a step further and calculate the pressure drop in your piping system (you have to provide the input) and calculate the “installed” flow characteristic of the valve and the “gain” (dq/ds) of the valve under various flow conditions. This way you can determine whether the control loop will be stable.1

Footnote 1. Some people have the misconception that the gain of the control valve must be ±1. This is not necessary and typically is not possible (because you never use the full signal span). It is important for the gain (whatever it is) to stay reasonably constant with a change of flow conditions (see Chapter 8).

Danh mục kiểm tra khi chọn phần mềm Checklist for choosing a program

When selecting a program (most vendors offer free ones), check the following:

  • Valve sizing should be per the current ANSI/ISA-75.01.01 standard or the corresponding International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standards.
  • Noise equations should follow IEC 60534-8-3 (for gas) or IEC 60534-8-4 (for liquids). (Unfortunately, you will need vendor-specific factors in order to use these standards.) Or better yet, use the simpler ABC noise prediction methods (see Chapters 18 and 22), which work well for all valve types and makes.
  • The required maximum Cv should not be more than 85% of the rated Cv of the selected valve.
  • The minimum required Cv should be greater than the Cv of the selected valve listed at 5% of valve travel (if a positioner is used), or 10% if the packing friction may be high and no positioner is used.

Các cảnh báo cần lưu ý Warnings to heed

The program will also contain warnings such as these:

  • Do not be scared by the ominous “choked flow” warning. This has no meaning other than it requires different sizing equations.
  • Ignore calculations of inlet and outlet velocities. These mean nothing, except on aerodynamic noise where you should avoid fluid velocities in the valve outlet above 0.3 Mach.
  • Pay attention to cavitation or flashing warnings. They may spell trouble (see Chapter 14).
