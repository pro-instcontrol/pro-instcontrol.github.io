Will My Valve Be Too Noisy?

Hans D. Baumann · Control Valve Primer, 5th ed. (ISA / Wiley)

Ba nguồn tiếng ồn cơ bản The three basic noise sources

The three basic types of noise sources in valves are (1) mechanical vibration, (2) turbulence and cavitation with liquids, and (3) aerodynamic noise.

Rung cộng hưởng của nút van Valve plug resonant vibration

Noise caused by resonant vibration of a valve plug within the radial clearance between plug guide and guide bushing, or between plug skirt and seat ring, is usually the result of severe fluid turbulence and can easily exceed 90 dB of sound level at frequencies between 0.5 and 7 kHz. This phenomenon seems not to be related to pressure drop (fluid velocity) itself but rather to unstable fluid flow such as flashing or cavitating water. Fortunately, this is a rather rare occurrence. Based on my experience, resonance occurs more often where the undamped natural frequency of the mass (m, the weight of valve plug divided by the acceleration of gravity) is high in relationship to the stiffness Kn (the perpendicular deflection rate) of the valve stem. This typically happens when the undammed natural frequency (fn) is below 5000 Hz (cycles/s).

fₙ = 0.16 (Kₙ / m)^(1/2) cycles/s

A hollow plug will reduce the mass (m), while a shorter unsupported stem length, or a larger stem diameter, will increase Kn—all positive remedies. Decreasing the guide clearance will reduce the amplitude, but that is not a sure cure for the problem, which, when unchecked, can lead to fatigue breakage of the stem or plug guide. Fortunately, one can hear the resonant vibrations of a valve plug occurring at frequencies mostly between 100 and 1500 Hz and sound levels that can exceed 90 dBA. This then should be taken as a call for remedial action.

Giới hạn OSHA và thiệt hại do rung The OSHA limit and vibration damage

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) limit of 8 hours of exposure for workers at 90 dB is measured on the A-weighted scale, which is patterned after the frequency response curve of the human ear; therefore, limit = 90 dBA. This is one criterion. On the other hand, noise is nothing but audible vibration, and vibration can cause damage to valves, piping, and accessories.

Even if you ignore the OSHA limit by putting the valve underground, the pipe will typically break at a sound pressure above 130 dBA [1] measured 1 m from the pipe wall. Therefore, tolerate no more than 110 dBA without using “low-noise trim” or other internal noise abatement means even when the valve is buried!

Hệ số năng lượng EC The energy coefficient EC

Do not read on if your valve is handling gas or steam and has an energy coefficient (EC) of less than 1000. This number is derived by multiplying the absolute inlet pressure in psia by the calculated valve Cv and FL numbers. For example, you reduce 150 psia steam and the required valve Cv = 125 at an FL factor of 0.9. Here your EC = 150 × 125 × 0.9 = 16,875, which is potentially a noisy standard valve. On the other hand, a valve with a Cv of only 6 has an EC of 6 × 150 × 0.9 = 810 and, therefore, a sound level of less than the OSHA limit of 90 dBA, regardless of the pressure drop.

Dự đoán tiếng ồn: ISA-75.17 và IEC 60534-8-3 Noise prediction: ISA-75.17 and IEC 60534-8-3

ISA published a reasonably accurate prediction method in ISA-75.17, Control Valve Aerodynamic Noise Prediction, based on modified free jet noise theories [2]. This standard has now been replaced by an international standard, IEC 60534-8-3[3], which adopts most of the methodology in the ISA-75.17 standard. Note, The IEC method is empirically derived. It only works well with proprietary, vendor-specific, empirically derived factors fitting specific valve types, or some tabulated approximations thereof, where the typically accepted prediction accuracy of ±5 dB may not be met. Thus, the IEC method is not universally applicable if acceptable accuracy is desired.

The 46 plus equations in the IEC standard should not scare you if you possess a full-size computer; besides, the vast majority of vendors have computer programs in place or available to you. However, to make life easier, I have condensed the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) document to a handier level that can be used with a handheld calculator.

Giới hạn số Mach tại cửa ra Limit the outlet Mach number

Note: To avoid downstream noise problems, limit the Mach number (Md) at the valve outlet port.

(17-1)M_d = 0.20 P₁ Cᵥ F_L / (D² P₂ Cᵢ / 343)

where Cᵢ = velocity of sound of the used gas; D = valve outlet diameter in inches; P₁ = absolute inlet pressure inn psia; P₂ = absolute outlet pressure in psia.

If you think IEC 60534-8-3 is too complicated, I have developed a much simpler, yet accurate, method; it is called the ABC method and is addressed in the next chapter.
