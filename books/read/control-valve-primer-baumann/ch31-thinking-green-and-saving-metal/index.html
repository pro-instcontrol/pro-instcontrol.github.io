Thinking “Green” and Saving Metal Thinking “Green” and Saving Metal

A worldwide effort to save energy is currently underway. This effort can be supported by users and manufacturers of control valves alike. Besides assigning lower pressure drops across valves as outlined in Chapter 29, “Saving Energy,” emphasis could be placed on the design and selection of valve types (for an example, see Chapter 6). Most current designs are quite heavy, and much metal1 could be saved in cage valves, for example. Here the weight of the heavy bonnet flange could be cut in half with proper cage retention within the valve housing (see Figure 31-1). Many globe valves, too, can be replaced by rotary valve styles (not on-off types) that can perform the same job at less than half the weight. In addition, rotary control valves offer about 20% more flow capacity (Cv) over globe valves, thus enabling lower pressure drops and savings in the energy used to run pumps and compressors.

Such green thinking would not only drastically reduce the use of energy-consuming metals but would also reduce transportation (freight) expenses. Finally, such savings will also greatly improve the profit margins of both the manufacturer and the user.
