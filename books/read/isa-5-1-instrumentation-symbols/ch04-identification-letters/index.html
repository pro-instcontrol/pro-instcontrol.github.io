5.1 · Twenty-two notes for Table 1 The official explanations

These notes are not commentary but part of the standard: they decide the boundaries of letter usage, especially easily misused cases such as PSV/PCV, switch/controller/relay, or an LAH derived from a reverse-acting signal.

  1. A «user's choice» letter covers unlisted meanings used repetitively in a particular project. It may have one meaning as a first-letter and another as a succeeding-letter; the meanings need only be defined once in a project legend. N, for example, may mean «modulus of elasticity» as a first-letter and «oscilloscope» as a succeeding-letter.
  2. The unclassified letter X covers unlisted meanings used only once or to a limited extent; it may take any number of meanings in either position. Except with distinctive symbols, its meanings are expected to be defined outside a tagging bubble on a flow diagram. XR-2 may be a stress recorder, XX-4 a stress oscilloscope.
  3. The grammatical form of succeeding-letter meanings may be modified as required: «indicate» may read indicator / indicating, «transmit» transmitter / transmitting, etc.
  4. Any first-letter combined with modifiers D (differential), F (ratio), M (momentary), K (time rate of change), Q (integrate/totalize) or any combination of them represents a new, separate measured variable, and the combination is treated as one first-letter entity. Thus TDI and TI indicate two different variables: differential temperature and temperature.
  5. First-letter A (analysis) covers all analyses not described by a «user's choice» letter; the type of analysis is expected to be defined outside a tagging bubble.
  6. Use of first-letter U for «multivariable» in lieu of a combination of first-letters is optional; nonspecific variable designators such as U should be used sparingly.
  7. The modifying terms «high», «low», «middle»/«intermediate» and «scan» are optional.
  8. The term «safety» applies only to emergency protective primary elements and emergency protective final control elements. A self-actuated valve that prevents a fluid system operating above a desired pressure by bleeding fluid is a back-pressure-type PCV, even if not normally used. But a valve intended to protect against emergency conditions (hazardous to personnel and/or equipment, not expected to arise normally) is a PSV — regardless of whether its construction makes it a safety, relief or safety-relief valve. A rupture disc is PSE.
  9. The passive function G applies to instruments or devices providing an uncalibrated view, such as sight glasses and television monitors.
  10. «Indicate» normally applies to the analog or digital readout of an actual measurement. For a manual loader it may denote the dial or setting indication, i.e. the value of the initiating variable.
  11. A pilot light in an instrument loop is designated by a first-letter followed by succeeding-letter L: a light indicating an expired time period is KQL. A light outside a loop is designated the same way — a motor running light may be EL (voltage as the measured variable) or YL (operating status monitored). XL should not be used for motor running lights because they are commonly numerous; user's-choice letters M, N or O may be used once defined (with M, make clear it stands for a monitored state, not «motor»).
  12. A succeeding-letter U for «multifunction» instead of a combination of functional letters is optional; this nonspecific designator should be used sparingly.
  13. A device that connects, disconnects or transfers circuits may be a switch, relay, ON-OFF controller or control valve depending on application. If it manipulates a fluid process stream and is not a hand-actuated ON-OFF block valve, it is a control valve; using succeeding-letters CV for anything other than a self-actuated control valve is incorrect. Otherwise: a switch if hand-actuated; a switch or ON-OFF controller if automatic and the first such device in a loop («switch» for alarm, pilot light, selection, interlock or safety; «controller» for normal operating control); a relay if automatic but not the first such device, i.e. actuated by a switch or ON-OFF controller.
  14. Functions associated with succeeding-letter Y are expected to be defined outside a bubble when further definition is needed. No definition is needed when the function is self-evident, as for a solenoid valve in a fluid signal line.
  15. The modifying terms «high», «low» and «middle»/«intermediate» refer to values of the measured variable, not of the signal, unless otherwise noted. A high-level alarm derived from a reverse-acting level transmitter signal is still LAH, even though it actuates when the signal falls to a low value. The terms may be combined (see clause 6.9A).
  16. Applied to valve and other open-close device positions, «high» denotes the valve in or approaching the fully open position and «low» the fully closed position.
  17. The word «record» applies to any form of permanent storage of information permitting retrieval by any means.
  18. For «transmitter» versus «converter», see the definitions in clause 3.
  19. First-letter V», «vibration or mechanical analysis», performs for machinery monitoring the duty that A performs for more general analyses. Except for vibration, the variable of interest is expected to be defined outside the tagging bubble.
  20. First-letter Y is intended when control or monitoring responses are event-driven rather than time- or schedule-driven. In this position it can also signify presence or state.
  21. Modifying-letter K combined with a first-letter such as L, T or W signifies the time rate of change of the measured or initiating variable. WKIC, for instance, may represent a rate-of-weight-loss controller.
  22. Succeeding-letter K is a user's option for designating a control station, while succeeding-letter C describes automatic or manual controllers (see clause 3).
