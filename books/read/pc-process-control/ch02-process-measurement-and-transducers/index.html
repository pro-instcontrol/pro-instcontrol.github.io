Chapter 2 turns from vocabulary to hardware: how a physical quantity becomes a signal. It defines transducers and sensors, sets out the characteristics every sensor is judged by (accuracy, precision, sensitivity, resolution, repeatability, rangeability, span, hysteresis), then works through the measurement of temperature, pressure, flow and level — thermocouples, RTDs and thermistors; strain-gauge, resonant-wire, capacitive, LVDT and optical pressure sensors; orifice and magnetic flowmeters; and level transmitters — closing with signal conditioning, noise, linearisation and the smart transmitter.
