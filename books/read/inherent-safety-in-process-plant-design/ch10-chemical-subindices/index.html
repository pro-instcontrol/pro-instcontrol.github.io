10. Subindices of Chemical Inherent Safety Index 10. Chỉ số thành phần của chỉ số an toàn nội tại hóa học

The Chemical Inherent Safety Index deals with the hazards related to the chemical properties of the substances in the process. The Index has been divided into subindices for reaction hazards and hazardous substances.

10.1 Subindices of Reaction Hazards 10.1 Các chỉ số thành phần của mối nguy phản ứng

The Inherent Safety Index (ISI) deals with both the main reaction(s) and those side reactions taking place in the reactor which are meaningful. It also deals with chemical interaction, which describes unintentional chemical reactions that can take place among the chemicals in the plant.

10.1.1 Reaction Heat Subindex for the Main Reaction 10.1.1 Chỉ số nhiệt phản ứng chính

Since the possible violence of reactions lies in the heat liberated and the temperature which may be reached, the energy change during the reaction has been selected to present the reaction safety in the ISI. This is a feasible approach since the formation enthalpies are known for most substances.

The enthalpy released or absorbed in a process can be described by Equation 6 for constant-volume conditions and an isobaric process. While determining the safety subindex IRM, the heat release of the main reaction is calculated for the total reaction mass (i.e. both the reactants and the diluents are included) to take account of the heat capacity of the system, which absorbs part of the energy released:

((6))ΔHr = Σ(Hf)products − Σ(Hf)reactants

From the safety point of view it is important to know how exothermic the reaction is. The classification used by King (1990): the reaction is extremely exothermic (≥ 3000 J/g), strongly exothermic (< 3000 J/g), moderately exothermic (< 1200 J/g), mildly exothermic (< 600 J/g), thermally neutral (≤ 200 J/g) or endothermic. These values are also used for determining the scores of IRS (Table 9). If there are several main reactions, for instance a series reaction, the score of IRM is determined on the basis of the total reaction; if there are several reactors in the process, the score is determined on the reactor with the greatest heat release.

Heat of reaction / total reaction massScore
Thermally neutral (≤ 200 J/g)0
Mildly exothermic (< 600 J/g)1
Moderately exothermic (< 1200 J/g)2
Strongly exothermic (< 3000 J/g)3
Extremely exothermic (≥ 3000 J/g)4

Table 9 · Determination of the reaction heat subindices IRM and IRS.

10.1.2 Reaction Heat Subindex for the Side Reactions 10.1.2 Chỉ số nhiệt phản ứng phụ

The subindex IRS of the heat of side reactions is determined in a similar way to IRM. The heat release for each possible side reaction is calculated according to Equation 6 for the full reaction mass including diluents; the same safety scores as for the main reaction are used (Table 9). The greatest heat-of-reaction value of all side reactions is used for determining IRS.

10.1.3 Chemical Interaction Subindex 10.1.3 Chỉ số tương tác hóa học

Chemical interaction considers the unwanted reactions of the process substances with materials in the plant area. These reactions are not expected to take place in the reactor and are therefore not covered by the side-reaction subindex. The ISI has utilized EPA's matrix (Hatayama et al., 1980) to classify the hazards of chemical interaction; the worst interaction between the substances present in the plant area is used in the calculations.

In Table 10 the score limits for IINT range from 0 to 4. Fire and explosion are considered the most hazardous consequences of an interaction (score 4). The score value for the formation of toxic or flammable gas depends on the amount and harmfulness of the gas (score 2–3); likewise, the more heat is formed, the higher the score (1–3). Rapid polymerization is valued on the basis of the polymerization rate (2–3). Soluble toxic chemicals and the formation of harmless, nonflammable gases are considered less harmful — score 1.

Heat formation1–3
Fire4
Formation of harmless, nonflammable gas1
Formation of toxic gas2–3
Formation of flammable gas2–3
Explosion4
Rapid polymerization2–3
Soluble toxic chemicals1

Table 10 · Determination of the chemical interaction subindex IINT.

10.2 Subindices for Hazardous Substances 10.2 Các chỉ số thành phần cho chất nguy hiểm

The flammability, explosiveness and toxic exposure subindices (IFL, IEX, ITOX) are determined for each substance present in the process. These indices are summed for every substance separately and the maximum sum is used as the subindex value. The corrosiveness subindex is determined on the basis of the most corrosive material in the process.

10.2.1 Flammability Subindex 10.2.1 Chỉ số khả cháy

The flammability subindex describes the flammability of liquids e.g. in the case of a leakage. Flammability of liquids is measured by their flash points and boiling points. The classification used is based on the EU directive (Pyötsiä, 1994): substances are divided into non-combustible, combustible, flammable, easily flammable and very flammable (Table 11).

FlammabilityScore of IFL
Nonflammable0
Combustible (flash point > 55 °C)1
Flammable (flash point ≤ 55 °C)2
Easily flammable (flash point < 21 °C)3
Very flammable (flash point < 0 °C & boiling point ≤ 35 °C)4

Table 11 · Determination of the flammability subindex IFL.

10.2.2 Explosiveness Subindex 10.2.2 Chỉ số khả nổ

In the ISI the explosiveness is considered through a chemical property which is not directly the same as the process explosion hazard, but can serve as a fire estimate. The explosiveness subindex describes the tendency of a gas to form an explosive mixture with air. Explosive ranges, expressed in per cent by volume of fuel vapour in air, are the concentration ranges in which a vapour/gas mixture with air will burn when ignited; flame propagation occurs if a mixture within its explosive range is ignited. The range is indicated by the LEL (lower explosive limit) and UEL (upper explosive limit). This model is a coarse estimation but useful when most chemical properties are not available. The explosiveness is determined by the difference UEL − LEL of the substance, divided into four steps (Table 12).

10.2.3 Toxic Exposure Subindex 10.2.3 Chỉ số phơi nhiễm độc

Health hazards caused by chemicals are represented by the toxic exposure subindex ITOX. The evaluation is based on Threshold Limit Values (TLV), because TLV data is readily available for most substances in the process industry. TLV values express the harmful exposure limits of substances in the threshold time of 8 hours; the lower the TLV (the more toxic the substance), the higher the index value. It is important to use TLVs with the same threshold time so that the results are comparable. The score limits in Table 13 are based on the Mond Index (ICI, 1985).

10.2.4 Corrosiveness Subindex 10.2.4 Chỉ số khả ăn mòn

Corrosive materials include e.g. acids, acid anhydrides and alkalies. Such materials often corrode pipes, vessels and other process equipment, which may result in a loss of containment and a subsequent fire, explosion or toxic release. Danger from leaks depends on the properties of the fluids: some corrosive fluids are volatile, flammable and toxic; some react violently with moisture. Strong acids and alkalies cause burns and eye damage to personnel.

Corrosion is usually measured as corrosion rates mm/a; the material is selected so that the corrosion allowance is not exceeded during the lifetime of the equipment. However, the corrosion rates are not always known during predesign, though a rough type of construction material is often anticipated. Since the need for a better material most often indicates more corrosive conditions, a classification based on the type of construction material can be justified. In the ISI, corrosiveness is determined on the basis of the required construction material (Table 14): if carbon steel is enough, the index value is 0; for stainless steel 1; for all special materials 2. The estimation is made for each process stream separately, and the worst case gives the final index value.

Construction material requiredScore of ICOR
Carbon steel0
Stainless steel1
Better material needed2

Table 14 · Determination of the corrosiveness subindex ICOR.

The chemical subindex scores of Tables 9–14 assemble into ICI per Equation 4 (Chapter 8): IRM,max + IRS,max + IINT,max + (IFL + IEX + ITOX)max + ICOR,max, with a maximum of 28 points.
