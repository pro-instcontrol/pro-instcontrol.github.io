Electrical Conductivity and Resistivity

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 43 · Electrical Conductivity and Resistivity · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 43. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Resistivity ρ = R·A/l (Ω·m); conductivity σ = 1/ρ (S/m). For uniform samples R = ρ·l/A. Low-resistance materials need contact-resistance-free methods. The four-point probe passes current I through the two outer of four collinear equidistant probes (spacing s) and measures V across the inner pair: for a semi-infinite sample ρ = 2πs(V/I), and for a thin film the sheet resistance R_s ≈ 4.53·(V/I). Van der Pauw measures two resistance pairs on any arbitrarily-shaped thin sample (no holes) and solves exp(−πR₁/R_s)+exp(−πR₂/R_s)=1 for R_s, then ρ = R_s·t — shape-independent, widely used in semiconductor physics. Solution conductivity uses a conductivity cell where R = K/σ, K = l/A the cell constant, so σ = K/R; measured with an AC bridge/oscillator (1–10 kHz) to avoid electrode polarization and temperature-compensated (≈2%/°C).
