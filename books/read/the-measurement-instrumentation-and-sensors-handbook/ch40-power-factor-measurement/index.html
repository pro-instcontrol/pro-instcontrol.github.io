Power Factor Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 40 · Power Factor Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 40. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Power factor λ = P/S = cos φ, where φ is the phase angle between voltage and current at the fundamental frequency. Distinguish the displacement PF (cos φ₁, fundamental only) from the true PF = P/S which includes waveform distortion: true PF = cos φ₁ · (I₁/I_rms), where I₁/I_rms is the current distortion factor.

The watt–volt–ammeter method measures P, V, I and computes λ = P/(V·I) — a true PF if the wattmeter is accurate. The three-voltmeter method places a non-inductive resistor R in series with the load Z; with V₁ across R, V₂ across Z and V₃ the source, Kirchhoff gives V₃² = V₁²+V₂²+2V₁V₂cosφ ⇒ cosφ = (V₃²−V₁²−V₂²)/(2V₁V₂). A three-ammeter method is analogous. Phase meters convert the time delay Δt (∝φ) via a logic gate to a voltage ∝φ; power-metering ICs compute PF, P, Q, S in real time from instantaneous v·i. Only the instantaneous-multiplier approach yields true PF when harmonics are present.
