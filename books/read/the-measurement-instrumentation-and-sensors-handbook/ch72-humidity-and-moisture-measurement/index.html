Humidity and Moisture Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 72 · Humidity and Moisture Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 72. The table of the source is reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Humidity describes water vapor: absolute (g/m³), relative RH = (p_H₂O/p_sat)·100 (%), and dew point. Moisture content in solids is % wet or dry basis. Capacitive polymer sensors (a water-absorbing polymer dielectric) are the most common: small, cheap, linear 0–100% RH, −40…+180 °C, ±1%–3% RH, but drift and chemical contamination affect them. The chilled-mirror dew-point meter is the most accurate: cool a mirror until condensation forms; that temperature is the dew point (±0.1–0.2 °C, used to calibrate other sensors). Psychrometers (dry/wet bulbs) derive RH from the temperature difference; resistive sensors are cheap; NIR/IR absorption and microwave measure material moisture contactlessly (paper, grain, concrete); aluminium-oxide sensors reach ppm levels in industrial gases.
