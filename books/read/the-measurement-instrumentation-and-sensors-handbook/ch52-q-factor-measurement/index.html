Q Factor Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 52 · Q Factor Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 52. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Quality factor Q = ωL/R = 1/(ωCR) = f₀/B (B = −3 dB bandwidth) characterizes resonance sharpness; higher Q means lower loss and a narrower resonance. The Q-meter drives the unknown L_x in series with a standard C at V_in and, at resonance, reads the capacitor voltage V_C = Q·V_in, so Q = V_C/V_in and L_x = 1/((2πf₀)²C) — direct reading at kHz–MHz. The bandwidth method excites the resonator broadband and finds the −3 dB frequencies f₁, f₂, giving Q = f₀/(f₂−f₁); it is immune to absolute gain. The decay method rings the resonator then disconnects the source; amplitude decays as e^(−t/τ) with τ = Q/(πf₀), so Q = πf₀τ — ideal for very high-Q mechanical/resonator elements. Typical Q: coils 10–300, good caps 500–10⁴, quartz 10⁴–10⁶.
