Composition Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 70 · Composition Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 70. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Composition measurement determines the concentration of species in gas/liquid/solid mixtures via spectroscopic absorption (IR, UV), chromatography (GC, LC), mass spectrometry, electrochemistry, or physical properties. NDIR measures gases with characteristic IR absorption (CO₂, CO, CH₄, hydrocarbons) through a filter-selected band following Beer–Lambert I = I₀·e^(−ε·c·l) — the dominant CO₂ method for environmental, HVAC and medical (capnography) use. GC separates components by column retention time with FID/TCD/ECD detection — accurate multi-component lab and online process analysis. MS ionizes and analyzes by m/z with ppt sensitivity. Specific gases: O₂ by paramagnetic (reference method) or zirconia cell (flues, furnaces); CO/H₂S by electrochemical cells; H₂/He by thermal conductivity (TCD); metal-oxide sensors for low-cost combustible gas.
