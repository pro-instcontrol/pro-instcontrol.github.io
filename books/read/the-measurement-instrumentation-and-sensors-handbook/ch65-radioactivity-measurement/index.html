Radioactivity Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 65 · Radioactivity Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 65. The table of the source is reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Activity is the decay rate in becquerel (Bq = s⁻¹); the old unit is the curie (1 Ci = 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq). Decay follows N(t) = N₀·e^(−λt) with half-life T½ = ln2/λ. Radiation types: alpha (short range), beta (medium), gamma (penetrating). The Geiger–Müller counter is a high-voltage gas tube producing one large pulse per ionization — simple and cheap, counts only (no energy), the standard handheld survey meter. An ion chamber measures the mean ionization current, linear with dose rate — used for dose and environmental monitoring. A proportional counter operates at lower gain with pulse height ∝ energy, separating α/β. Scintillators (NaI(Tl), CsI, plastic) convert radiation to light read by a PMT/photodiode: low noise, good gamma sensitivity, NaI(Tl) the classic budget gamma spectrometer. Semiconductors: Si (α, β), HPGe (high-resolution gamma spectroscopy, needs cooling), CdZnTe; charge ∝ energy gives the best resolution (0.1%–0.2% at 662 keV).
