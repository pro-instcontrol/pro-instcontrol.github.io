Turbidity Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 62 · Turbidity Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 62. The table of the source is reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Turbidity is the loss of water clarity from suspended particles (silt, algae, bacteria, colloids) that scatter and absorb light; the standard unit is NTU against formazin standards. Drinking water must be < 1 NTU (WHO/EPA). The nephelometer measures 90° scattering (LED 860 nm or tungsten source, photodiode detector) — the standard method, least affected by color; a transmission channel often compensates. Transmissometry (I/I₀ over path length) suits high turbidity (wastewater, sludge); multi-angle scattering gives particle-size information; inline sensors control filtration/chemical dosing continuously.
