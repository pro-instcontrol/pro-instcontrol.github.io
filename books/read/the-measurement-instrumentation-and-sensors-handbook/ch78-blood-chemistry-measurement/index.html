Blood Chemistry Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 78 · Blood Chemistry Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 78. The source prose is not repeated; the governing relations and reference data are summarised below.

Blood chemistry relies on ion-selective electrodes (Nernst: E = E0 + 59.16/z mV per decade at 37 °C), the Severinghaus pCO₂ and Clark pO₂ electrodes with the Henderson–Hasselbalch relation, and enzymatic/photometric assays obeying Beer–Lambert A = epsilon*c*l. Glass ISE for Na⁺/pH, valinomycin for K⁺; co-oximetry resolves Hb species at multiple wavelengths. Distinguish direct (undiluted) from diluted ISE. Pre-analytical handling (heparin balance, anaerobiosis, haemolysis, storage) dominates the error budget; two-point calibration plus Levey–Jennings/Westgard QC is standard.

(78.1)E = E₀ + (2,303·RT/(zF))·log a_i ≈ E₀ + (59,16/z) mV·log a_i (37 °C)
(78.2)pH = 6,1 + log( [HCO₃⁻]/(α·pCO₂) ) , α = 0,0307 mmol·L⁻¹·mmHg⁻¹
(78.3)A = −log(I/I₀) = ε·c·l , I = I₀·e^(−ε·c·l)
