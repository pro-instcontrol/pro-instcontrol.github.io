Current Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 38 · Current Measurement · 1 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 38. Figures and tables of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Current I = dQ/dt (ampere = coulomb/second) spans pA (leakage, photo-current) to kA (power). An ammeter must be in series with the circuit so the full current flows through it, so its resistance must be small — the voltage burden V_burden = I·R_a is the characteristic error.

A moving-coil meter of resistance R_a and full-scale current I_fs is extended with a shunt R_sh = R_a·I_fs/(I−I_fs) (manganin, low tempco). Electronic ammeters use an op-amp transresistance converter: V_out = −I_in·R_f, with a virtual ground giving µV burden and a range from fA to A; feedback picoammeters/electrometers reach 10⁻¹⁶ A. AC current is measured with a current transformer (CT, AC only, isolated, saturating), a Rogowski coil (flexible, non-saturating, wide bandwidth, integrated output) or a Hall-effect sensor (DC+AC, the basis of clamp meters).

Low current (pA–µA) needs guarding, shielding and electrometer FET inputs; high current (kA) uses manganin shunts (µΩ) or isolated Hall/optical transducers. Burden voltage, insertion effect, frequency response and CMRR are the key error sources.
