Magnetic Field Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 48 · Magnetic Field Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 48. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Magnetic field: B (tesla) and H (A/m), with B = μ₀μ_r H. A search coil of N turns, area A, gives an induced emf e = −N·dΦ/dt = −N·A·dB/dt; integrating yields Φ and B — excellent for AC fields, but a static field needs mechanical rotation to create dΦ/dt. The Hall effect gives a transverse voltage V_H = I·B/(n·q·t) (n carrier density, t thickness), measuring DC and AC contactlessly and cheaply — ubiquitous in current sensing and position sensors, though temperature-compensated. A fluxgate uses a periodically saturated core; the external field distorts the flux waveform, producing even harmonics ∝ H, reaching pT–nT sensitivity for geomagnetics. The SQUID (Josephson quantum interference) is the most sensitive (fT = 10⁻¹⁵ T) for MEG/MCG. AMR/GMR/TMR sensors change resistance with field angle (disk drives, angle sensors); NMR measures B via the Larmor frequency f = γB and serves as the calibration standard.
