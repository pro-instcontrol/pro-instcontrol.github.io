Distortion Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 53 · Distortion Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 53. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Distortion is signal deformation through a non-ideal device: harmonic (nonlinearity), intermodulation, and amplitude/phase distortion. Total harmonic distortion THD = √(V₂²+V₃²+…)/V₁ (%). A distortion analyzer nulls the fundamental with a notch/synchronous filter, leaving the residual harmonics, which a true-RMS meter reads; the ratio to V₁ is THD. DSP/FFT computes the spectrum and THD directly. Intermodulation distortion (IMD) drives two tones f₁, f₂ and measures the intermodulation products 2f₁−f₂, 2f₂−f₁ (third-order) and f₁+f₂, |f₁−f₂| relative to the originals (dBc) — critical in audio and RF because the ear/receiver is very sensitive to it. Other metrics: SMPTE/DIN IMD (HF carrier modulated by LF), CCIF difference-tone, and SINAD = S/(N+D). High-quality audio/RF targets THD < 0.01%.
