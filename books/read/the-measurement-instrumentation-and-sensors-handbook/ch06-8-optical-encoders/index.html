Optical Encoder Displacement Sensors

J. R. René Mayer · École Polytechnique de Montréal

Section 6.8 · 14 figures · about 15,962 words

An optical encoder is a deceptively simple device: a grating, a light source, a detector, and a counter. Almost all of its engineering difficulty sits in the gap between the grating and the detector, and in what happens at the moment the beam crosses from one line to the next. This companion page follows the translation section by section and pulls out the points where the physics, the electronics and the metrology pull against each other.

Two questions decide everything Hai câu hỏi quyết định mọi thứ

Before any specification is written, two questions settle the architecture. First: must the position be known at power-on? If yes, the encoder is absolute and the scale must carry a code; if no, an incremental encoder plus a reference mark will do, at a fraction of the cost and with far finer resolution per unit of scale length. Second: is the motion bidirectional? If yes, quadrature signals are mandatory, because a single square wave carries no direction information at all.

The classification in Figure 6.66 is organised around those two answers, and it is worth noticing what the tree does not branch on: accuracy. Resolution and accuracy are different quantities, and the section is careful about it — increasing resolution by interpolation or by ×4 decoding does not improve trueness. A 20-bit interpolated reading from a scale whose lines are unevenly spaced is a precise measurement of a wrong position.

Absolute codes: why binary is not enough Vì sao mã nhị phân trực tiếp không dùng được

The direct binary scale is the obvious design and it fails for one reason: at position 7 → 8 all four bits change at once. If the four read apertures are not perfectly aligned, the intermediate states 0111, 0011, 0001, 0000 will all be read somewhere along the transition, and some of them are valid codes for other positions. The error is not a small offset; it is a transient jump to a completely different number.

Two cures are given. Vee-scan staggers the read points so that the transition on each track is detected at a different longitudinal position, and combinational logic reconstructs the word; the tolerance on each transition relaxes to ±1/8 of that track's cycle. Gray code removes the problem at the source by making consecutive words differ in exactly one bit, at the cost of a conversion — a chain of exclusive-ORs, or a ROM.

The pseudorandom code is the cleverest of the four and the hardest to explain. A maximal-length sequence of length 2ⁿ − 1 has the property that every window of n consecutive bits is unique, so a single track carries the whole position — no parallel tracks, no alignment between them. The price is that the word must be decoded: the reverse-feedback shift register is run until it returns to the initial sequence, and the number of steps taken is the position. That is a computation, not a lookup, and it takes time. It is also why the n + 1 heads and the synchronisation track exist: to guarantee that the window is read at the middle of a record rather than across an edge.

Quadrature: four edges, one count Bốn sườn, một xung đếm

A quadrature cycle contains four edges, and the simple circuits count only one of them. That is a factor of four thrown away for nothing. The ×4 decoders in Figure 6.73 recover it by looking at the present and previous states of A and B together — which is exactly the Gray-code view of the same signals that Kuzdrall proposes. Once you see the four states as a 2-bit Gray code, the decoding logic writes itself.

The oscillation problem deserves a moment. If the shaft dithers across a single edge, a naive counter increments and decrements alternately and the count wanders. Kuzdrall's circuit arranges that the up and down counts happen at the same encoder position, so the dither cancels instead of accumulating. That is a logic fix to a mechanical problem, and it is the reason his circuit is still copied.

The clock rule f ≥ 8·N·S is not arbitrary. The shift register needs to see each of the four states at least once, and the demultiplexer needs a settled 4-bit word, so eight clock periods per quadrature cycle is the practical minimum. Run the clock slower and transitions are simply missed; the count then loses a step silently, which is the worst failure mode a position sensor has.

Interpolation: buying resolution with analog quality Nội suy: đổi chất lượng tương tự lấy độ phân giải

Benzaid's network is a trigonometric identity turned into resistors. sin(θ + α) = cos α · sin θ + sin α · cos θ, so a weighted sum of the two encoder signals is a phase-shifted copy of them. Eight such sums, squared and exclusive-ORed in even and odd groups, give A′ and B′ at eight times the original frequency. The elegance is that the amplitudes do not matter — only the zero crossings do — which is why the weights can be rounded to convenient resistor values at the cost of a little duty-cycle error.

The limit is not the resistor tolerance but the quality of the incoming sine and cosine. Mark-space ratio, quadrature separation and jitter all propagate straight through into the interpolated phase. At high interpolation factors the analog front end, not the digital counter, becomes the specification.

Hagiwara's two-dimensional table replaces the analog network with memory. Digitise A and B to m bits each, use the pair as an address, and store the phase code for that cell. The table can also store a correction, which is the real advantage: known periodic errors in the encoder signals — eccentricity, graduation error, quadrature mismatch — can be compensated cell by cell. Mayer's arctangent processor does the same thing with arithmetic instead of memory and reaches the highest interpolation factors, but the caveat in the text is the important part: the ADC sets the theoretical limit, the analog signal quality sets the practical one.

Three optical principles, three regimes Ba nguyên lý quang, ba chế độ

Geometric masking is ray optics: light goes straight, the two gratings act as a shutter, and the detector sees the overlap area. It is valid for pitches above roughly 10 µm and it is what every absolute encoder uses. The index grating in the read head is there for a practical reason — a single slit passes very little light, so a row of slits is used to raise the flux onto the detector without changing the period.

Moiré is the regime where the grating period is comparable to the wavelength but the designer chooses to treat diffraction as a nuisance rather than a tool. Two gratings of equal pitch, slightly rotated, produce fringes with period P/γ — a magnification of the grating by a factor of 1/γ. A displacement of half a pitch moves the fringe pattern by half a fringe, so the detector sees a full cycle for a half-pitch move. That magnification is the whole point: the detector integrates over many lines and averages out local pitch errors.

The radial case is worth remembering because it is the one that bites in practice. Two radial gratings with displaced centres give circular fringes through both centres; if the centres coincide the fringes fluff out to infinite width and the whole field goes dark and light together. That is exactly the eccentricity error of a real encoder disk, and it is why the Canon rotary design reads at two diametrically opposed points — the eccentricity then shifts one reading up and the other down, and the average cancels it.

Diffraction is the regime where the wave nature is used deliberately. Each slit radiates circular wavefronts; along the directions where those wavefronts are in phase, a plane wave leaves at angle sin θ_m = mλ/P. Move the grating by x and the m = +1 and m = −1 wavefronts shift by +xλ/P and −xλ/P respectively, so their relative phase changes by 2xλ/P. Interfere the two orders and you have subdivided the grating pitch optically, with no interpolation electronics at all.

The Sony hologram encoder doubles that again by passing each beam through the grating twice, which is where the factor of four per pitch comes from. The condition 2P sin ψ = λ is a Bragg condition: the successive plane mirrors inside the volume hologram must return wavefronts that add in phase. It is a reminder that in this regime the grating is not a shutter at all — it is a diffractive optical element, and the encoder is really a small interferometer.

Choosing one Chọn một thiết bị

The ten selection criteria in the text are ordered by how often they decide the purchase, and the order is instructive. Resolution, repeatability and accuracy come first because they are what the datasheet quotes; speed comes second because it is what the datasheet omits. Environment comes third and is usually the real constraint — a scale that reads to 0.1 µm in a laboratory reads to nothing at all in a coolant mist. Friction torque and inertia matter for a small servo axis and not at all for a slow positioning table.

The commercial tables are a snapshot of 1999 and the prices should be read as orders of magnitude, not quotes. What has not dated is the spread: three orders of magnitude in resolution and two in price across encoders that all claim to measure position. The difference is almost never the counter — it is the grating, the optics and the care with which the read head was aligned.
