Radioactivity Measurement (part 2)

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 66 · Radioactivity Measurement (part 2) · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 66. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Gamma spectrometry quantifies each isotope via its characteristic lines (Cs-137: 662 keV; Co-60: 1173 and 1332 keV). Energy calibration with standard sources (Am-241, Cs-137, Co-60, Eu-152) maps channels to energy; efficiency calibration with a known-activity source gives ε(E) = N_counts/(A·t·I_γ), and sample activity follows A = N_counts/(t·ε(E)·I_γ). Efficiency depends on sample geometry (distance, density, container), so standards must match. Natural background (cosmic, K-40, U/Th in soil, machine X-rays) limits sensitivity; shielding is 10–15 cm lead with an inner copper/steel layer to absorb lead characteristic X-rays, plus anticoincidence/guard detectors for cosmic rejection; background is measured separately and subtracted. Decay is Poisson: σ = √N, relative error 1/√N — a 10× error reduction needs 100× the counting time.
