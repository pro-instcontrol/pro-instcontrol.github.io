Immittance Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 51 · Immittance Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 51. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Complex impedance Z = R + jX = |Z|∠θ (R real, X reactive). Immittance generalizes to Z, admittance Y = 1/Z = G + jB, and the anti-impedances. At frequency f, Z_R = R, Z_L = jωL, Z_C = 1/(jωC). Bridges (Wheatstone, Maxwell, Hay, Schering, Wien, Owen) balance an unknown arm against standards; balance requires both real and imaginary parts to vanish, so each bridge best fits a component family. The modern complex I–V method drives a known current I and measures the complex voltage V (high-speed ADC) to get Z = V/I, Y = 1/Z = G + jB; an auto-balancing bridge in an LCR meter controls the current so the voltage across a standard resistor gives it directly, separating R and X at any test frequency with 0.05%–0.1% accuracy. At RF/microwave, reflection methods use a directional coupler to measure the reflection coefficient Γ = (Z−Z₀)/(Z+Z₀) (Z₀ = 50 Ω) and back out Z; a VNA sweeps frequency to plot |Z(f)|, θ(f) and power absorption.
