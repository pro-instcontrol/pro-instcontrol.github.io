Noise Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 54 · Noise Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 54. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Noise is random signal that sets the smallest measurable limit. Principal sources: thermal (Johnson–Nyquist), shot, flicker (1/f) and emission noise. Thermal noise on resistance R at temperature T has spectral density S_V(f) = 4kTR (V²/Hz) so V_n,rms = √(4kTRB) with B the bandwidth — white (flat) noise. Noise is usually stated as spectral density (nV/√Hz). The noise figure NF = SNR_in/SNR_out (dB = 10log₁₀) measures added noise; NF is found by the Y-factor method comparing output with a hot (T_h) vs cold (T_c) source through a known attenuator, or with a calibrated noise source. Measurement methods: true-RMS over a known band (back out density), spectrum/FFT analysis (separates white and 1/f), substitution against a standard noise source, and equivalent-input-noise (output noise / gain). SNR = 10log₁₀(P_s/P_n).
