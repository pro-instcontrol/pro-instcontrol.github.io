pH Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 71 · pH Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 71. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

pH = −log₁₀(a_H⁺) on a 0–14 scale at 25 °C (7 neutral). The glass electrode develops a potential across its H⁺-sensitive membrane following Nernst: E = E₀ − (2.303RT/F)·pH = E₀ − 0.0592·pH at 25 °C, so one pH unit ≈ 59.2 mV; the slope is temperature-dependent, hence automatic temperature compensation (ATC). A reference electrode (Ag/AgCl or calomel) provides the stable potential. Calibration uses standard buffers (4.01, 7.00, 10.01) at two or three points with buffer-temperature compensation; electrode slope decays with age (replace below ~95% of theoretical or when response slows); calibrate daily or before each series. Combination electrodes (glass + reference in one body) are most common; flat, micro and double-junction variants suit surfaces, small samples and dirty media. Applications: water/wastewater, food, pharma (GMP), bioreactors, soil.
