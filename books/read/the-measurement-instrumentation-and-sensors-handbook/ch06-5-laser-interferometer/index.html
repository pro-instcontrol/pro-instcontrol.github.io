Laser Interferometer Displacement Sensors

Bernhard Günther Zagar · Technical University Graz

Section 6.5 · 7 figures · about 3,728 words

Section 6.5 is the precision-metrology entry, and its argument is narrower than it first looks. A laser interferometer does not measure length; it counts wavelengths. Everything that follows — the laser, the stabilisation, the refractometer, the fringe logic — exists because the wavelength in air is not quite the wavelength in vacuum, and the difference is larger than the precision the instrument is trying to deliver.

The numbers set the scale of the problem. Since 1983 the metre is fixed by c = 299,792,458 m/s exactly, so the vacuum wavelength is as good as the laser’s frequency stability. A Zeeman-stabilised He–Ne reaches 2 × 10⁻⁸; an iodine-stabilised one reaches 10⁻⁹. But the refractive index of ambient air is only known to about 5 × 10⁻⁸, and that single term dominates the error budget of any interferometer working in air.

The laser Laser

The He–Ne discharge is a textbook energy-transfer chain: electrons excite helium into two long-lived metastable states; those states lie close enough to excited levels of neon that collisions transfer the energy almost exactly; the neon then emits by stimulated emission. Helium contributes no light at all — it is there only to pump. The cavity condition L = mλ/2 selects which wavelengths can oscillate, and because the gain envelope is gigahertz wide, several longitudinal modes usually oscillate at once. Those modes beat against each other, and the beat destroys the temporal coherence an interferometer needs.

Two ways to stabilise Hai cách ổn định

The Zeeman method is elegant because the error signal is the thing being measured. A longitudinal magnetic field splits the single oscillating mode into right- and left-circular components about 1.5 MHz apart, and that splitting peaks exactly when the laser frequency sits on the atomic transition. Lock the cavity length so the splitting stays at its maximum and the frequency is pinned. The cavity is adjusted two ways: a heater for the slow, large excursions, and a piezoelectric actuator for the fast, sub-wavelength corrections.

The iodine method is better by an order of magnitude and much harder. A ¹²⁷I₂ absorption line at 632.9913981 nm sits close enough to the He–Ne line to lock onto, and the resulting relative uncertainty is ±10⁻⁹. It is used only where that last order of magnitude matters.

A detail worth noticing: the CO₂ term. Dry air is 0.035 % CO₂, the concentration is rising about 1.4 ppm per year, and an industrial site can vary it locally by far more than that. Edlén’s 1966 formula assumed a fixed value; the Birch–Downs and Ciddor updates do not. For work at the 10⁻⁸ level, which formula you use is itself a decision with metrological consequences.

Three interferometers Ba kiểu giao thoa kế

The Michelson of 6.49 is the reference design and also the one with the obvious flaw: the returning beams re-enter the laser and form an external cavity that detunes it. Commercial instruments fix this with corner-cube retroreflectors and optical isolators. The retroreflector has a second virtue — it returns the beam parallel to itself regardless of small tilts, which is what keeps the fringe contrast up when the carriage moves.

The heterodyne version of 6.50 turns the measurement into a frequency comparison. Two modes f₁ and f₂ are separated by polarisation, one sent to the measuring arm and one to the reference arm, and the detector sees f₂ − f₁ − f_D. Because the carrier is 1.5 MHz rather than dc, the electronics can tell forward from backward directly from the sign of the count — no quadrature gymnastics required. The price is equation 6.75: the Doppler shift must stay below the mode spacing, which caps the closing velocity at 0.474 m/s. Moving away has no such limit.

The homodyne version of 6.51 gives up the frequency offset and gets back the velocity range. It pays for that with the signal-processing problem the rest of the section is about: a single output I(t) ∝ 1 + cos Φ(t) cannot tell +Φ from −Φ, and its amplitude drifts as the laser ages. Equations 6.78–6.80 answer both objections by generating sin Φ, −cos Φ and −sin Φ alongside cos Φ. Four signals, four detectors, and the phase becomes unambiguous over a full 2π and immune to intensity drift at the zero crossing.

Where the resolution actually comes from Độ phân giải đến từ đâu

  • λ/2 — counting fringes, one count per half wavelength of mirror travel. The basic Michelson.
  • λ/4 — quadrature detection with direction sensing, the homodyne instrument as built.
  • λ/512 — electronic phase interpolation of the sinusoidal intensity, the best quoted for a Michelson with sophisticated processing.
  • ≈160 nm — the ×4 interpolation of the conditioning circuit in Figure 6.52, which is what a practical counter does before any digital subdivision.

The last line of the section is the one to remember: none of these numbers mean anything without air bearings and a controlled environment. A fringe counter that resolves 160 nm is worthless if the air along the beam changes temperature by 0.1 °C between measurements, because that alone moves the answer by 10⁻⁷ of the path length.
