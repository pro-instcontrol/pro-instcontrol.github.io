Energy Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 42 · Energy Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 42. The table of the source is reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Energy E = ∫ p(t)dt, in watt-hours (1 kWh = 3.6 MJ). An induction (disk) meter spins an aluminum disk at a speed ∝ P; a mechanical counter counts revolutions and a meter constant (rev/kWh) converts them to energy — robust and cheap, but records only real energy and is affected by temperature and stray fields. Electronic meters multiply v(t)·i(t) instantaneously and integrate digitally to a pulse count ∝ energy; ADE7xxx-class ICs compute P, Q, S, active/reactive energy and PF. Smart meters add two-way communication (PLC/RF) for time-of-use billing and grid monitoring, and measure harmonic energy via the instantaneous multiplier. Three-phase energy totals the phases via two/three measurement elements; accuracy classes run 0.2%–2%.
