Measurement Accuracy

Ronald H. Dieck · Pratt & Whitney

Chapter 4 · 2 figures, 2 tables
What this page is. A companion to Chapter 4: the argument, the equations and both tables, with the two figures redrawn. The full text lives in Ch04.pdf in the library catalogue and is not republished here. The complete Vietnamese translation is here.

Measurement error is unknown and unknowable, so what an uncertainty statement actually claims is a limit at a confidence. Chapter 4 builds that statement: the normal and uniform distributions, the standard deviation of the average (not of the data), the ISO A/B classification versus the engineering random/systematic classification, root-sum-squaring with sensitivity coefficients, the Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, and Student’s t at 95 %. A worked temperature example shows both models landing on ±0.44 °F.

The two models agree

With independent sources and sensitivities of one, the systematic term is BR = √[(0.06/2)² + (0.07/2.18)² + (0.10/2)²] = 0.065, the Type A term is 0.21 and the Type B term 0.058. Welch–Satterthwaite gives 22 degrees of freedom, so t95 = 2.07, and both UR,ISO and UR,ENG come out at ±0.44 °F at 95 % confidence — which is the point of the chapter: the classification changes what you learn about the experiment, not the number you report.
