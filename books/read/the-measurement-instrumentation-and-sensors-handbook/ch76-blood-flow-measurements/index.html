Blood Flow Measurements

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 76 · Blood Flow Measurements · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 76. The source prose is not repeated; the governing relations and reference data are summarised below.

Blood flow is obtained either from velocity (electromagnetic induction e = B*l*v, or Doppler shift df = 2*f0*v*cos(theta)/c with c ≈ 1540 m/s and theta ≤ 60°) times vessel area, or from indicator dilution: CO = 60*I/integral C(t) dt (Stewart–Hamilton) and the Fick relation CO = VO2/(CaO2 − CvO2). Electromagnetic perivascular probes are the most accurate but require surgery; transthoracic/ultrasound Doppler is non-invasive yet angle- and area-sensitive; pulmonary-artery thermodilution remains the bedside reference for cardiac output. Laser Doppler and laser speckle report relative perfusion units.
