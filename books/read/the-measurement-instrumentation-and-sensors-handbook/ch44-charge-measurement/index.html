Charge Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 44 · Charge Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 44. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Charge Q = ∫ i dt = C·V (coulomb; elementary charge e ≈ 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C). Because charges of interest (pC–nC) easily leak through insulation resistance and pick up noise, measurement needs very high-input-impedance electrometers, shielding and guarding. The feedback electrometer integrates Q_in on a feedback capacitor C_f giving V_out = −Q_in/C_f; the virtual ground keeps leakage negligible, reaching 10⁻¹⁵–10⁻¹⁶ C sensitivity — the standard for tiny currents and radiation charge. The charge–voltage (qV) method transfers unknown Q from sample capacitor C_x to a standard C_f and reads V_f (Q = C_f·V_f); dithering repeats to improve resolution (piezoelectric sensors). Applications: piezoelectric Q = d·F; photoelectric/radiation charge counting; Kelvin-probe surface charge (work function); coulometry where Q = nF (Faraday constant) gives moles reacted.
