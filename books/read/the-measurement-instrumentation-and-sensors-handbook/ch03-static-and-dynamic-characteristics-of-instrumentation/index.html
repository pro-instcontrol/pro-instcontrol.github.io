Chapter 3 is the measurement-theory chapter of the handbook. It separates the three concepts that are routinely confused — discrimination (the smallest distinguishable increment), precision (the spread of repeated readings, i.e. repeatability and reproducibility) and accuracy (closeness to the true value) — then treats the static characteristics of a block (input/output slope, offset, span and dynamic range, drift, hysteresis and backlash, saturation and crossover distortion, bias, and the error of nonlinearity in its four best-fit conventions) and the dynamic characteristics (forcing functions, the characteristic equation, and the zero-, first- and second-order responses with their time constant, damping ratio and resonance). It closes with calibration, illustrated by a gravimetric flow-meter rig.
