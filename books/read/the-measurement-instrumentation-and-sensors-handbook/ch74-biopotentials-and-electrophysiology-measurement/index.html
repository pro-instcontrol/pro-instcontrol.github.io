Biopotentials and Electrophysiology Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 74 · Biopotentials and Electrophysiology Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 74. The table of the source is reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Excitable cells (cardiac, muscle, neural) generate membrane potentials. Surface measurements: ECG (0.1–5 mV, 0.05–100 Hz), EEG (10–100 µV, 0.5–100 Hz), EMG (50 µV–5 mV, 20–2000 Hz), EOG, EGG. Signals are tiny with high source impedance (kΩ–MΩ), so an instrumentation amplifier with very high input Z (>100 MΩ), high CMRR (>100 dB to reject line common-mode) and 500–1000× gain is required; patient isolation (optical/transformer) per IEC 60601. Ag/AgCl electrodes with conductive gel are the standard (low, stable contact potential); skin preparation keeps contact impedance <10 kΩ. Main noise: 50/60 Hz line (common and differential mode), electrode drift, motion; mitigations: shielded leads, patient isolation from ground, high-pass 0.05–0.5 Hz, band-limited low-pass, 50/60 Hz notch; ECG diagnostic band 0.05–150 Hz vs monitoring 0.5–40 Hz.
