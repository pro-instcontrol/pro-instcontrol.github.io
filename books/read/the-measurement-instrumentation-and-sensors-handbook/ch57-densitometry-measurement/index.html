Densitometry Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 57 · Densitometry Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 57. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Optical density OD = log₁₀(I₀/I) with I₀ the incident and I the transmitted (or reflected) intensity. OD 1 = 10% transmission, OD 2 = 1%. Density is proportional to concentration via Beer–Lambert (A = ε·l·c). Transmission densitometers illuminate a small aperture (1–3 mm) of film (X-ray 0–4 OD) and read with a photodiode against a white standard; reflection densitometers measure ink density on print. Spectral filters (visual, UV, CMY) suit print applications. Medical: DXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) uses two X-ray energies to report bone mineral density in g/cm² (1%–2%). Chemistry: a spectrophotometer measures solution OD per wavelength for quantitation (±0.005 OD).
