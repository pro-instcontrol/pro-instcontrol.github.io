Vision and Image Sensors

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 64 · Vision and Image Sensors · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 64. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Image sensors (CCD, CMOS) convert light into a charge/voltage array. Per pixel: full well capacity (e⁻), quantum efficiency QE = electrons/photon, dark current (e⁻/s), read noise (e⁻); image quality is set by SNR = S/√(S + N_dark + N_read²) and dynamic range = full well / read noise (stops = log₂). CCDs shift charge to one readout node — uniform, low noise, QE > 90% (back-illuminated) — used in science, astronomy and medical imaging. CMOS integrates an amplifier and ADC per pixel — fast, low power, integrable — dominant in cameras, machine vision and automotive, but with more fixed-pattern noise (FPN) requiring calibration. Measured quantities: QE with calibrated sources, dark current vs time/temperature (doubles every ~6–8 °C), read noise via the photon-transfer method (two dark frames), MTF (spatial response via slanted edge), FPN via dark/flat calibration. Applications: digital cameras, surveillance, industrial inspection, remote sensing, microscopy, astronomy, automotive and medical.
