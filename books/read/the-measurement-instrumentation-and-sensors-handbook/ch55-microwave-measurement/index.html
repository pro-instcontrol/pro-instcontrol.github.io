Microwave Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 55 · Microwave Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 55. Equations and table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

The microwave band (~1 GHz to tens of GHz) has wavelengths comparable to circuit dimensions, so currents and voltages are no longer locally defined; transmission-line theory with characteristic impedance Z₀ (usually 50 Ω) applies, and measurements use scattering (S) parameters instead of R, L, C. The reflection coefficient Γ = (Z−Z₀)/(Z+Z₀) quantifies mismatch; VSWR = (1+|Γ|)/(1−|Γ|), with |Γ| = (VSWR−1)/(VSWR+1). A directional coupler separates forward/reflected waves to measure Γ/VSWR, while a vector network analyzer (VNA) measures S₁₁, S₂₁ directly across frequency. Power is sensed by a bolometer/thermistor (resistance changes with absorbed heat), a diode detector (fast, low power) or a calorimeter (primary standard), calibrated into a power meter (W or dBm). Frequency uses a counter or cavity wavemeter; phase is measured by the VNA. Permittivity/permeability at microwaves use resonant cavities or free-space methods (Ch. 46).
