Voltage

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 37 · voltage · loading · dual-slope DMM · true-RMS · 1 figure, 1 table

This is the companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 37. The figures and tables of the source are reproduced below; the translation prose is not repeated here.

Voltage (potential difference) is the work per unit charge to move between two points in an electric field — one of the most measured electromagnetic quantities. The SI unit is the volt (V); practical ranges span nV (bio-signals) to hundreds of kV (power transmission). A voltmeter connects in parallel with the points of interest, so it always draws some current — the loading effect.

Loading: with source resistance Rₛ feeding load Rʟ, the no-meter voltage is V₀ = E·Rʟ/(Rₛ+Rʟ). Adding a meter of input resistance Rᵥ in parallel gives R′ʟ = Rʟ∥Rᵥ and V = E·R′ʟ/(Rₛ+R′ʟ). The relative error is roughly −Rʟ/(Rᵥ+Rʟ); keeping error under 1% needs Rᵥ ≳ 100·Rʟ, under 0.1% needs Rᵥ ≳ 1000·Rʟ. This is why electronic voltmeters (1–10 MΩ, up to GΩ) replaced moving-coil meters (kΩ/V sensitivity).

Analog meters scale a moving-coil movement with a series multiplier resistor (sensitivity in Ω/V). Electronic voltmeters use an op-amp voltage follower for very high input impedance (10⁶–10⁹ Ω) and pA–nA input current, isolating the meter from the circuit. Digital voltmeters (DMMs) use a dual-slope or sigma-delta ADC: V_in = V_ref·T₂/T₁ makes the reading depend only on a time ratio, rejecting capacitor and clock errors and line-frequency common-mode noise. Resolution is 3½–6½ digits, input Z ≈ 10 MΩ.

AC voltage is characterized by peak, RMS (V_rms = V_peak/√2 for sine) and rectified-average. Average-responding meters read RMS correctly only for sine; true-RMS meters measure the thermal equivalent of any waveform via V_rms = √(1/T ∫ v²(t)dt). High-voltage measurement uses resistive or capacitive voltage dividers; low-voltage (µV–mV) measurement needs shielding, guarding, thermal-EMF compensation, and averaging. The null/potentiometer method gives laboratory accuracy by balancing against a calibrated reference with zero current drawn.
