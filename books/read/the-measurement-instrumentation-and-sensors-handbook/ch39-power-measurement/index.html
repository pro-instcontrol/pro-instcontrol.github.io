Power Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 39 · Power Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 39. Equations and the table of the source are reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Instantaneous power is p(t)=v(t)·i(t). For DC, P=V·I. For sinusoidal AC with phase angle φ, P = V_rms·I_rms·cosφ; the reactive power Q = V_rms·I_rms·sinφ and apparent power S = V_rms·I_rms, with S²=P²+Q². Units: W, var, VA.

An electrodynamic wattmeter uses a current coil in series (carrying I) and a potential coil in parallel (carrying current ∝ V); the torque ∝ v·i, so the pointer reads the average — the real power. Errors come from the potential-coil phase error and the current-coil burden; at high frequency or low power factor a phase-compensated or electronic meter is needed.

Three-phase power uses the two-wattmeter method (P_tot = W₁+W₂, with W₁=V_L I_L cos(30°+φ), W₂=V_L I_L cos(30°−φ)); the ratio yields φ and PF. A four-wire (three-phase + neutral) system uses three wattmeters. Electronic wattmeters multiply v(t)·i(t) in real time (analog multiplier or ADC) and low-pass filter the average; dedicated ICs (e.g. ADE7xxx) compute P, Q, S, energy and PF. With harmonics, P = Σ VₙIₙcosθₙ — the instantaneous multiplier reads the true total, unlike fundamental-only methods.
