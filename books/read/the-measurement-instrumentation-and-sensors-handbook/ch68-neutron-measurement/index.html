Neutron Measurement

John G. Webster (chủ biên) · CRC Press, 1999

Chapter 68 · Neutron Measurement · 0 figures, 1 tables

Companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 68. The table of the source is reproduced below; the prose is not repeated.

Neutrons carry no charge and must be detected indirectly via nuclear reactions producing charged particles. Energy classes: thermal (<0.5 eV), intermediate (0.5 eV–0.1 MeV), fast (>0.1 MeV); sources include reactors, accelerators and isotopes (Am-Be, Cf-252). The BF₃ counter uses ¹⁰B-rich gas: the ¹⁰B(n,α)⁷Li reaction ionizes the gas with a large pulse — the classic cheap thermal-neutron standard. The ³He counter (³He(n,p)³H) offers higher efficiency and is now the most common. ⁶Li scintillators (Li glass, ⁶LiI) via ⁶Li(n,α)³H support pulse-shape discrimination (PSD) against gammas. Fast neutrons are measured by proton recoil (elastic scattering on hydrogen in plastic scintillator, pulse ∝ energy) or fission chambers (²³⁵U/²³⁸U — very large fragments, easy gamma rejection, reactor use). Spectrometry: Bonner spheres (a small ³He counter inside polyethylene spheres of graded size; unfolding gives the spectrum), time-of-flight (E = ½m(L/t)²), and recoil telescopes.
