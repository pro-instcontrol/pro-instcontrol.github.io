Angle Measurement

Robert J. Sandberg · University of Wisconsin

Chapter 14 · 3 figures, 3 tables, sine relation sin A = H/D

This chapter is a companion to the Vietnamese translation of Chapter 14. The three figures of the source (14.1 vernier protractor, 14.2 sine bar, 14.3 two-ball taper measurement) are redrawn as vectors, and the large equipment tables (14.2, 14.3) are reproduced. The translation prose is not repeated here.

An angle is the figure formed by two intersecting lines or planes. The units are degrees (1/360 of a circle) and radians (1/2π of a circle; 1 radian = 57.29578°). A degree splits into 60 minutes (′), and a minute into 60 seconds (″) — a second of arc is tiny for machined parts but ~30 m on a great circle of the Earth and ~9 km on the Moon seen from Earth. Table 14.1 lists the defining terms (acute/obtuse/right angle, azimuth, latitude, longitude, taper, twist, goniometer, and so on).

Section 14.4 is the protractor. A direct-reading protractor is graduated in 1°; precise ones carry a vernier reading to 5′ of arc. Reading it (Figure 14.1): if the vernier zero is right of the main zero, use the right side of the vernier, find the vernier line best aligned with a main-scale line, count lines from zero (each = 5′), and add that to the degrees shown by the main-scale mark just left of the vernier zero. In the example the fourth line aligns, so add 20′.

Section 14.5 is the sine bar — a hardened, ground steel bar with two equal cylindrical rolls whose axes are parallel to each other and to the bar's top face. Gage blocks under one roll raise that end by H, and the governing relation is sin A = H/D (Figure 14.2), where D is the roll-center distance (250 mm / 5 in. or 500 mm / 10 in.). The workpiece is usually clamped in a precision vice. Section 14.6 extends this to the sine plate (a bottom plate and side straps, one roll acting as a hinge); a compound sine plate sets true compound angles in two orthogonal planes. Section 14.7 measures a taper (Figure 14.3) with two balls (or gage pins) of known diameter: measure the depth to the top of each ball from a common reference, and with the known radii apply the geometry to recover the taper half-angle φ.

Section 14.1 (angle gage blocks) and 14.2 (clinometers) cover the simpler setting/measuring aids: right-triangle steel blocks in sets of 1°–30° (or 1″ steps) for durable, cheap angle setup, and electronic clinometers giving ±0.01° resolution against gravity over a ±45° (full 360°) range, with floating zero. Section 14.3 describes optical comparators — hand-held units with a calibrated reticle and projection models (10×–100× magnification, 0.3–1 m screens) that overlay a part's silhouette on a mask for quality control, best used in a darkened room.
