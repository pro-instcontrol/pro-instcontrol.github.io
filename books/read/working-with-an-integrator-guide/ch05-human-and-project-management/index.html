Human Resources and Project Management criteria

CSIA · Guide Vol. 1 (2000) · §§4.2–4.3

Chapter 5 · 7 source pages · 2 figures · companion notes
Key terms. Turnover rate — one of the running example criteria from §2. · Scope — the work to be done. · Deliverables — the items handed over. · WIP / in-progress documents — unfinished work and documents. · Methodology — execution method; §4.3 stresses it does not judge the method, only whether it is documented and followed.

The next two criteria families go into people and how a project is run. §4.2 opens with a blunt observation: the most valuable asset any company has is the employees who perform the work. §4.3 is longer and states the single most important principle of the project criteria: it does not judge the methodology, only whether that methodology is documented and followed.

4.2 · Human Resources Four concerns

“The management of human resources is a vital component of a successful control systems integrator.” The source lists four concerns: meeting legal requirements, the recruitment process, employee development and retention programs. This is also the family directly tied to the “low turnover rate” example (weight 7) in the §2.2 scoring steps.

4.3 · Project Management The no-judgment principle

«The purpose is not to judge the actual methodology, but to ensure the methodologies are documented and followed as related to the project scope.»

CSIA Guide, Volume 1, §4.3, page 19

The stated scope is broad: clarity of scope, thoroughness of work planning, project team structure, practices/standards, project execution methodologies, documentation, quality procedures, testing and commissioning. The seven sub-groups below follow the project lifecycle in order.

4.3.7 · User Defined

Questions with a double edge. Several 4.3.1 questions ask both about the process itself and about client involvement — approval of functional requirements, approval of schedule / cost / work changes, and a risk-management procedure that involves the client. This is how the standard turns “project management” into a verifiable two-way relationship.
  • Link to §2.3. “Documented and followed” is precisely the boundary between level 2 and level 3 on the performance scale.
  • Link to §4.7.4. The 4.3.1 G risk-management procedure supplies the evidence that step 7 of §2.2 needs.
  • Safety and security. §4.3.6 carries a bold note: “Special care should be given to safety and security issues” — echoing the “all accidents are preventable” belief in Chapter 1.
