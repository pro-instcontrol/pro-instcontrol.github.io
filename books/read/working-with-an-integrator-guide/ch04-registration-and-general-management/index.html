CSIA registration and General Management criteria

CSIA · Guide Vol. 1 (2000) · §3, §4.1

Chapter 4 · 6 source pages · 2 figures · companion notes
Key terms. Registration — CSIA recognition following an independent third-party audit. · Audit — assessment against the Best Practices & Benchmarks criteria. · Suggested criteria — the §4 question set, not a pass/fail test. · User Defined — blank A–D rows for client-added criteria. · GAAP / WIP — generally accepted accounting procedures / work in progress (Chapter 6).

This is the volume's turning point: from how to decide to what to decide about. §3 offers a shortcut — if a CSI is CSIA Registered, the client may skip much of the analysis table. §4 then lists seven families of suggested criteria, starting with General Management. Remember: the §4 items are questions for the client to weigh, not a mandatory pass list.

3 · CSIA Registration Program Independent audit instead of self-assessment

  1. Immediate evaluation. CSIA registration gives the client an effective method for immediate evaluation of a CSI. The criteria were jointly developed by CSIs, clients and manufacturers to reflect the requirements of the parties involved in implementing control systems.
  2. Independent third party. An independent third-party organisation conducts the registration audit of a CSI against the CSIA Best Practices and Benchmarks criteria.
  3. Public register. Members who successfully achieve registration are identified on the CSIA website (www.controlsys.org).
  4. Less effort, cost and risk. Registration identifies those CSIs that have met an established industry metric.
  5. Scope of the criteria. Registration addresses General Management, Human Resources & Professional Development, Project Management, Quality Management, Financial Management and Business Development — i.e. §§4.1 through 4.6.

4 · Suggested criteria Seven families, with blank rows for the client

§4 “outlines the suggested criteria that the client should take into consideration during the selection decision-making process”. Immediately below the heading the source leaves blank space with the note: “Clients can use blank space to add their own criteria and definitions”. Every sub-group therefore ends with a User Defined item carrying blank A, B, C, D rows.

Read the scoring table together with §4. The numbers in the first column of the §2.5 sample table and the Appendix B blank form are these clause numbers. 4.1.1 A in the table corresponds to the “Defined/Clear Strategic Objectives” question below.

4.1 · General Management Purpose, long-range plans, objectives and goals

The 4.1 introduction (repeated at 4.1.1) sets the scope: criteria associated with the CSI's purpose, long-range plans, objectives and goals. Six sub-groups run from strategy, organisational structure, facilities, computer-system management and insurance to a user-defined tail.

4.1.6 · User Defined

The source leaves four blank rows A–D. This is where clients insert the project-specific requirements the guide cannot cover — for example information-security certification, localisation requirements or a sector-specific standard. The disclaimer on page i reminds us that users are solely responsible for their own criteria.

  • Recurring theme: documentation. Procedures manual, written computer policy, software purchase proofs, project archive — all ask “is it recorded?”, matching the 0–5 scale in §2.3.
  • Recurring theme: continuity. Succession planning, off-site backups, key-man insurance and errors & omissions cover are all questions about the supplier's ability to survive the project lifecycle.
  • Link back to §2.4. Larger CSIs need more infrastructure, so the same question carries a different suggested “Good” score at Level 1 / 2 / 3.
