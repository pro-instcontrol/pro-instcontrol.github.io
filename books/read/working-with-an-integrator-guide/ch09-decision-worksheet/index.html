Appendix B — Kepner-Tregoe decision analysis worksheet

CSIA · Guide Vol. 1 (2000) · Appendix B

Chapter 9 · 11 source pages · 3 figures · companion notes
Key terms. Decision analysis worksheet — the blank form clients fill with their own criteria, musts, weights and scores. · User Musts «M» — the mandatory-condition column. · User Wants Weights (1–10) — the desired-criterion weights. · Suggested Good Score / Performance Maximum — recommended target and ceiling scores. · SECTION TOTAL / GRAND TOTAL — group and overall totals. · NO GO — a candidate eliminated at the musts screen, with no total score.

Appendix B is the working form: the same table as the §2.5 sample with all illustrative numbers removed, leaving only the column structure, criterion numbers and level-specific suggested scores. It occupies the volume's final eleven pages so clients can photocopy it or rebuild it in a spreadsheet. This chapter closes Volume 1 by explaining how to use the form and tying it back to the whole process.

Every Appendix B page repeats the same header lines: “Item numbers correspond to section numbers in Section 4.0 explaining criteria” and “User Weight × Avg. Perf. Score = Total”. That is why the form cannot be used on its own — it is an empty frame to be filled from §2 (process), §4 (criteria) and the real data for each candidate.

Reading the totals rows From the §2.5 example

  1. GOAL SCORE = 347. The sum of the “Suggested Performance Maximum” column for the whole General Management group; it is the 100% reference.
  2. CSI A = 190 (55%), CSI B = 172 (50%). Although CSI B wins the simple §2.2 example, in the full table CSI A ranks higher — a reminder that the outcome depends on the criteria and weights the client chooses.
  3. CSI C = NO GO. No total is computed because it was eliminated at the musts screen; the GRAND TOTAL therefore reads “NO GO” rather than a number.
Do not conflate the two examples. §2.2 uses a two-criterion toy example (turnover, application experience) to teach the arithmetic; §2.5 uses the full table with dozens of criteria. They were not designed to produce the same winner — they illustrate two levels of detail of one method.

Using the worksheet end to end Eight steps, one sheet

  1. Write the decision statement at the top of the form (§2.2 step 1).
  2. Add client-specific criteria into the A–D / “User Defined” rows (step 2; §§4.1.6, 4.2.3, 4.3.7, 4.4.5, 4.5.6, 4.6.4).
  3. Mark M against the must criteria (step 3).
  4. Assign 1–10 weights to the wants (step 4).
  5. Mark «X» in the appropriate Level 1 / 2 / 3 column (§2.4).
  6. Screen GO / NO GO through the musts and strike out eliminated candidates (step 5).
  7. Have three judges score independently, round the average up, multiply by the weight, and sum to SECTION TOTAL and GRAND TOTAL (step 6).
  8. Record risks and mitigations in the margin, then decide (steps 7–8). Keep the completed sheet as a defensible audit record.

Closing Volume 1 Six parts, one defensible decision

Volume 1 moves from understanding the market (§1), through structuring the decision (§2), the registration shortcut (§3), the seven criteria families (§4), the provider taxonomy (Appendix A), to the sheet that records every judgment (Appendix B). Its lasting value is not a score but a defensible decision: which criteria were chosen, how they were weighted, who scored them, and which risks were accepted and owned by whom.
