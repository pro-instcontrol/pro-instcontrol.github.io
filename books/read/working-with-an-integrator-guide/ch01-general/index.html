General — CSIA and the CSI

CSIA · Guide Vol. 1 (2000) · Introduction and §1

Chapter 1 · 11 source pages · 4 figures · companion notes
Key terms. CSIA — Control System Integrators Association. · CSI — Control Systems Integrator, an independent value-added engineering organisation. · Best Practices & Benchmarks — the CSIA self-evaluation and industry-benchmark programme. · Registration — CSIA recognition after an independent audit. · Client — the party procuring integration services, distinguished here from the end customer.

Volume 1 of the CSIA guide is not a control-engineering textbook but a decision handbook for the buyer. It answers three questions: what a CSI is, how to evaluate and select one, and which criteria to use. This opening chapter lays the groundwork: the purpose of the documents, the association's beliefs and mission, and the formal definition of a CSI.

Purpose of the documents Three purposes, two volumes

The introduction states three major purposes. Only the first belongs to Volume 1; the other two are assigned to Volume 2. Reading Volume 1, it is worth remembering that this is the evaluation and selection part, not the request-for-proposal (RFP) development or project-implementation management part.

VolumePurpose
Volume 1Provide the client with a method of identifying criteria to evaluate and select a Control Systems Integrator.
Volume 2Provide the client with an effective method and guideline for developing a Request for Proposal and evaluating the proposals.
Volume 2Provide a suggested method to more effectively manage project implementation.

From the INTRODUCTION, page i of the source.

The documents were written by all three sides. Industry professionals from user companies, product manufacturers and control systems integrators jointly developed the Registration Program & Criteria and this Guide (collectively, the “Documents”). The §4 criteria therefore reflect the requirements of several parties involved in implementing control systems, not only the supplier's point of view.

CSIA Guide, Volume 1, page i (capitalised in the original)

The two sentences immediately before that warning matter just as much: the documents are NOT ALL ENCOMPASSING and cannot reasonably or solely be relied upon to cover every unique requirement of a project or company. They are a tool, not a mandatory checklist — a theme that recurs in §2.3 and §4.4.10.

Beliefs, mission and objectives The benchmarking programme's front matter

Before any criteria appear, the source devotes four pages (iv–vii) to the association's quality culture: ten beliefs, a mission statement, five objectives and an acknowledgements list. These are not decoration. The beliefs explain why §4 asks about prevention rather than correction, safety training, measurable improvement and regulatory compliance.

  • The end customer must be the focus and ultimate beneficiary of our efforts.
  • Prevention is more productive than correction — quality efforts pay off more when directed at prevention.
  • People are the greatest resource: knowledge, skill, creativity and productivity.
  • All accidents are preventable; every employee has the right to a safe working environment — the root of criterion 4.3.6.
  • Benchmarks raise the industry's standard — their use lifts the standard of excellence across the industry and its markets.

1.1 · What is CSIA? Three roles

  1. Industry forum. CSIA is the premier organisation dedicated to the control systems integration industry, giving members a unique forum for education and discussion of the business, marketing and technical issues they face.
  2. Promoting capability. CSIA supports its members by promoting the capabilities, accomplishments and professionalism of CSIs to users, vendors, media and related trade, technical and industry groups.
  3. Benchmarks and registration. CSIA provides Best Practices & Benchmarks so CSIs can evaluate and continuously improve themselves, and officially registers CSIs that have been audited and meet key criteria (see Chapter 4).

1.2 · Definition of a CSI The formal definition

«A Control Systems Integrator (CSI) is an independent (or an independent profit/loss division) value-added engineering organization that focuses on industrial control systems, manufacturing execution systems and plant automation that requires application knowledge and technical expertise for sales, design, implementation, installation, commissioning and support.»

CSIA Guide, Volume 1, §1.2, page 1

The source stresses that most CSIs evolve from a single service into a full range of services, taking on many different appearances along the way — and it is precisely this that confuses the marketplace. Appendix A exists to remove that confusion: it groups CSIs and providers into “types” by the share of revenue derived from integration services versus hardware, using a 61% threshold. See Chapter 8.
