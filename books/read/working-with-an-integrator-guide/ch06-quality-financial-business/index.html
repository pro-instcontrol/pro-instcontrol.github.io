Quality, Financial and Business Development criteria

CSIA · Guide Vol. 1 (2000) · §§4.4–4.6

Chapter 6 · 9 source pages · 3 figures · companion notes
Key terms. GAAP — Generally Acceptable Accounting Procedures. · WIP — work in progress. · A/R — accounts receivable. · Job cost / forecast-to-complete — project cost system and the estimate of cost still to be incurred. · Hold harmless clause — a clause shifting liability away from one party. · Intellectual property ownership — ownership of the application software delivered.

These three criteria families test the health of the supplying organisation itself: its quality system, financial discipline and how it wins work. They span pages 24–32 and complete the scope of CSIA registration (§§4.1–4.6). §4.7 — the technical family — falls outside the registration scope and is covered separately in Chapter 7.

4.4 · Quality Management Five groups, one loop

ClauseGroupRepresentative question
4.4.1LeadershipDoes the CSI conduct meetings on quality?
4.4.2Effective CommunicationIs quality information clearly communicated across the firm?
4.4.3Continuous, Measurable ImprovementsDoes the CSI verify and document that the client is satisfied with the project?
4.4.4Customer ServiceIs there an established complaint procedure, with follow-through to ensure resolution to the customer's satisfaction?
4.4.5User DefinedFour blank A–D rows for client-specific criteria.

The five §4.4 criteria groups, condensed from pages 24–25.

The belief echoes here. “Quality efforts are more productive when directed toward prevention rather than correction” (Chapter 1) explains why 4.4.3 requires satisfaction to be verified and documented rather than merely asking whether complaints are received. It is also exactly the difference between scores 4 and 5 on the §2.3 scale: continual improvement must be demonstrated.

4.5 · Financial Management Manage, control, report and plan

“The financial and accounting aspects of the control systems integrator should focus on the specific means required to manage, control, report, and plan the company's financial resources.” Six sub-groups run from the accounting system, planning, billing, project financial reporting and tax policy to a user-defined tail.

4.5.1 · Measures of Financial Performance

ItemCriterionQuestion to ask
AGAAP Financial System in OperationAre financial systems in operation providing an easy-to-follow audit trail for verification of all financial records using GAAP?
BTimely Accounting Methods / ReportsDo accounting methods (cash or accrual basis) correspond with financial reports generated in a timely, periodic manner?

§4.5.6 (User Defined) closes the family with four blank A–D rows. The WIP, percent-completion and forecast-to-complete questions are especially valuable to clients: they are early signals that a project is overrunning before the next invoice arrives.

4.6 · Business Development Essential, but not overzealous

«An effective business development program is required for the survival of every control systems integrator. New opportunities must be forthcoming at a reasonably steady rate and on terms that will provide the control systems integrator with financial stability. At the same time, overly zealous promotion can disrupt other aspects of a control systems integrator's practice.»

CSIA Guide, Volume 1, §4.6, page 29
ClauseGroupRepresentative question
4.6.1Business Development PracticesDo marketing materials accurately represent the company and its capabilities? Are individual resumes accurate and consistent? Are relationships with representatives, VARs and partners clearly defined?
4.6.2Development of ProposalsWhen and why does the firm say “no”? Are there guidelines for defining the precise scope of work and supply? Do estimating and pricing standards exist, along with an administrative review and approval policy?
4.6.3Negotiating and ContractingAre standard commercial documents used regularly and reviewed by legal counsel? Are client-prepared RFPs, contracts and purchase orders reviewed for wording that unfairly allocates liability (hold harmless, termination, warranty, limitation of liability, dispute resolution)?
4.6.4User DefinedBlank rows for client-specific criteria.

The four §4.6 criteria groups, condensed from pages 29–31.

  1. Software ownership. 4.6.3 D and E ask specifically for contract language managing the liability risk of delivering electronic files and program changes, and for language stating ownership of application software. On automation projects this is often the hardest negotiation point.
  2. Straightforward contracts without hidden costs. 4.6.3 C states this as a standalone requirement — a transparency criterion, not merely a legal one.
  3. Volume 2's job. 4.6.2 stays inside the CSI; developing the RFP and evaluating proposals from the client's side belongs to Volume 2.
