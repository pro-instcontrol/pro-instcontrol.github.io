Performance scoring, levels and the sample table

CSIA · Guide Vol. 1 (2000) · §§2.3–2.5

Chapter 3 · 4 source pages · 4 figures · companion notes
Key terms. Performance score — the 0–5 maturity scale for policy / practice / standards. · CSI level — Level 1 / 2 / 3 by annual sales, headcount and engineer ratio. · Suggested Performance Maximum — the recommended ceiling score. · Good Score — the recommended “good” score for each level. · GO / NO GO — the must-screening outcome. · Three-judge average — the basis of the performance score in the sample table.

If Chapter 2 supplies the process, Chapter 3 supplies the yardstick. The last three clauses of §2 define the 0–5 performance scale (2.3), the three CSI size levels (2.4) and the sample criteria/analysis table (2.5) — the table to which all of §4 and Appendix B refer. It is also the most table-heavy chapter in the volume.

2.3 · Performance scoring of alternatives One scale, six levels

The user may define a scoring system that helps decision makers evaluate candidates fairly and consistently. The CSIA offers the system below “for your edification” — it is not mandatory. The key point of this clause is the authors' very practical admission:

«Since the range of criteria is so broad, any one scoring system will have its moments of questionable applicability to the criteria in question. The decision-maker(s) should use their best judgment and move on.»

CSIA Guide, Volume 1, §2.3, page 7
ScoreShort meaning
0No evidence of policy, practice or standards “systems” in place, and no evidence of any work being initiated in this area.
1Basic policy, practice or standards “elements” initiated.
2Basic elements partially implemented.
3Basic policy, practice or standards mostly implemented with supporting documentation.
4Higher elements implemented with good supporting documentation.
5Higher policy, practice or standards are well thought out, documented and implemented to the point of outstanding.

Condensed from the §2.3 description table; full wording is in Figure 3.1.

  • Levels 0–2 are mostly about the existence of policy and documentation; level 3 is the “minimally adequate” threshold.
  • Levels 4–5 shift to effectiveness and continual improvement: 4 strives but has not demonstrated, 5 has demonstrated.
  • This grading explains why §4 repeatedly asks “is it documented”, “is there training”, “is improvement measured”.

2.4 · CSIA classification levels Compare like with like across sizes

“Larger CSIs require more business infrastructure than smaller ones. To compensate for this issue, we suggest evaluating CSIs by level.” That is why the scoring table carries three separate suggested-score columns for Level 1 / 2 / 3 — do not compare a twelve-person shop with an eighty-engineer firm against the same ceiling.

How to use it in the table. §2.2 directs you to select the appropriate “Suggested Performance Maximum / Pick Level 1, 2 or 3” for the level of CSI being evaluated. When comparing CSIs at different levels, pick the number that best fits the client's requirements for those criteria. §2.5 repeats this in its NOTE.

2.5 · The “Sample” Criteria / Analysis Table The volume's central table

The table spans two pages (9–10) with columns for the criterion number (matching the §4.0 clause numbers), a “Musts M” column, want weights 1–10, suggested maximum, “Good” score, three level-specific suggested scores, and CSI A*, CSI B*, CSI C* score columns. Each group ends with a SECTION TOTAL row and the table ends with a GRAND TOTAL. Blank rows are provided for client-specific criteria.

  1. Criterion numbers in the table are §4.0 clause numbers — e.g. 4.1.1 A is “Defined/Clear Strategic Objectives”. The table and the criteria explanations must therefore be read together.
  2. Much of §4 can be skipped. Verbatim: “Users may wish to skip Section 4.1 through 4.6.5 of analysis table if the CSI being evaluated is CSIA Registered!” — see §3.0. This is the volume's most important practical shortcut.
  3. A blank form is supplied in Appendix B; the asterisk (*) marks columns the user fills in.
Note on the sample. “Three resources judged performance of CSI A, CSI B and CSI C. In this sample, CSI C did not pass the screen for ‘musts’.” That is why the CSI C column contains only GO / NO GO values and no total score.

Reading the three numbers in one cell The 1,2,2 → (4) example

This is the sample table's most easily misread part, so the source explains it separately. For criterion 4.1.1 A, three judges score CSI A as 1, 2 and 2. The sum 5 divided by 3 is 1.66, rounded up to 2. Multiplying by the user's want weight (2) gives 4 — the number in parentheses. A group's TOTAL column is therefore not the sum of the three raw scores but the sum of the weighted products.
