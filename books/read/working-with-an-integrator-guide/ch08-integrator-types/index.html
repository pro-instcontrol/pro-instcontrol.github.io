Appendix A — Types of integrators and providers

CSIA · Guide Vol. 1 (2000) · Appendix A

Chapter 8 · 11 source pages · 11 figures · companion notes
Key terms. CSI — Control Systems Integrator; integration services > 61% of revenue. · CSP — Control System(s) Provider; integration services < 61%, focused on selling products. · The 61% threshold — construction revenue excluded. · MES / SCADA — manufacturing execution systems / supervisory control and data acquisition. · Project mix of services — the revenue share of each service group in a typical project.

Appendix A exists to clear up a confusion flagged as early as §1.2: “most CSIs evolve out of singular services to a full range of services and take on many different looks along the way, confusing the marketplace”. It positions every organisation into a type by the share of revenue from integration services versus hardware, using a 61% threshold, and illustrates each with a typical project service-mix chart. All ten charts are redrawn as vectors in this chapter.

No type is better than another. Verbatim in sections 2 and 4: “No type is better or worse than another! It is up to the client to decide which type or combination of types best fits their needs”. Most CSIs / CSPs are a combination of several types rather than a pure one.

1 · Control Systems Integrator (CSI) Above the 61% threshold

The focus is value-added engineering services in complex integrated solutions. Revenue from integration services versus hardware is greater than 61%, not inclusive of construction revenue. These organisations deliver a full range of services for integrated control and/or information solutions: consulting, design, construction, project management, development, commissioning, specifications, panel wire & assembly, training, testing and ongoing support.

2 · Types of Control Systems Integrators Four types, four charts

“Different markets (segments) place different demands on focus, engineering disciplines and mix of services. Below are several types of CSI's in no particular order.”

· Design/Build Integrator/Services

Turn-key design/build of automation equipment and systems; process, mechanical, electrical and control disciplines; multi-discipline project management from concept to commissioning, including construction.

· Control Systems Integrator/Services

Focuses on complex shop-floor integrated control and information systems; process knowledge, electrical, control and information disciplines. Multi-discipline project management, installation support and/or management of electrical construction.

· Manufacturing Execution Systems Integrator/Services

Focuses on complex shop-floor integrated manufacturing execution systems; the discipline is mainly information technology with some SCADA knowledge. Project management from concept to commissioning; ongoing support and system administration are typical extensions.

· Information Technology (IT) Systems Integration/Services

Complete services for traditional integrated IT systems; mainly information technology with some MES knowledge. Ongoing support, system administration and outsourcing are typical extensions.

3 · Control System Provider (CSP) Below the 61% threshold

Uses value-added application engineering to facilitate the sale of products into a more complete solution. Revenue from integration services versus hardware is usually less than 61%, not inclusive of construction revenue. The focus is selling equipment, hardware and/or software products alongside a full range of application engineering services.

  • Services listed in section 3: consulting, design, installation support, project management, development, start-up assistance, specifications, panel wire & assembly, training, construction, testing and warranty support.
  • Compared with a CSI, the CSP mix shifts markedly toward Purchase Equipment / Hardware / Software (30%) and Installation (20%).

4 · Types of CSPs Four types, four charts

· Equipment Solutions Provider

Sells customised pieces of equipment with the engineering to customise them; process, mechanical, electrical and control disciplines; multi-discipline project management from concept to commissioning.

· Control Solutions Provider

Sells customised control hardware or software products with value-added engineering; electro-mechanical, electrical and control disciplines. Warranty support is a typical extension.

· Control Panel Shop Provider

The largest hardware/software purchase share in all of Appendix A (55%), plus 15% panels/stations — accurately reflecting a business weighted toward physical fabrication and supply.

· Construction / Control Systems Provider

Construction accounts for 30% of the mix and project management 12% — the most construction-heavy CSP type, yet still below the 61% integration-services threshold.

Reading the charts correctly. Labels such as <5%, <10% and <1% are upper bounds, not exact values. To redraw them as a donut, each “less than” value is taken at its bound and the series is normalised to 100%; the slice angles are therefore illustrative while the printed legend figures remain verbatim from the source.
