Heater Treaters

Heater treaters speed up crude oil/water separation by applying heat to break emulsions.

How a heater treater works

Heater treaters help facilitate crude oil/water separation by speeding up emulsions separation by applying heat. Most are low-pressure, 3-phase separators equipped with firetubes, and they separate gas from the incoming emulsion, and then separate the emulsion into a water phase and an oil phase.

Accurate Temperature Measurement for Heater Treater Process Control

As with almost every heating process, temperature measurements are required at several strategic points. Emerson's combination of a Rosemount 3144S Temperature Transmitter with a Rosemount 214C RTD Temperature Sensor and a Rosemount 114C Thermowell (Figure 18) provides accurate and robust measurements, even in harsh environments. The transmitter's ReadyConnect Technology, which enables plug-and-play sensor configuration, and Bluetooth Technology provides easy, error-free configuration and diagnostic coverage. The transmitter's built-in RTD Measurement Protection also helps avoid loss of measurement and downtime by converting your sensor configuration from a 4-wire to a 3-wire if a wire is broken or damaged.

Monitoring Pressure Relief Events to Minimize Gas Loss and Emissions

Since heater treaters are pressure vessels, PRVs are required. When they open during upsets, operators lose product, generate emissions, and face regulatory reporting obligations. Emerson's Rosemount 708 Wireless Acoustic Transmitter (Figure 19) monitors PRVs continuously by detecting relief events and leakage, communicating alerts via WirelessHART. When paired with the Plantweb Insight Pressure Relief Valve Application, event data is automatically logged and analyzed to quantify gas loss, simplify compliance reporting, and drive faster corrective action, transforming PRV activity from a hidden risk into actionable insight.

FIGURE 19 Rosemount 708 Wireless Acoustic Transmitter mounts with clamps on a PRV discharge pipe to capture releases and leakage.

Reliable Fuel Gas Measurement for Heater Treater Efficiency

Heater treater fuel is often raw natural gas. This must be monitored for environmental purposes and overall efficiency data. Measuring gas flow with a mass flow meter provides an accurate picture of energy consumption, rather than just gas volume, since density relates to energy content. Emerson's Micro Motion F-Series Compact Coriolis Flow and Density Meters combined with a Micro Motion 4700 Transmitter (Figure 20) provide a highly accurate mass flow reading across a wide pressure range, with easy integration to a host system.
