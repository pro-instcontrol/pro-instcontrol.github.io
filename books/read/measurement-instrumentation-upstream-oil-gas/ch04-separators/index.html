Separators

Oil separators receive a three-phase mix of oil, water, and gas from the wells and separate them so they can be processed and recovered.

Why separation control matters

Oil separators are the mainstay of upstream petroleum operations. Their task is relatively simple yet crucial for site operations. The oil separator vessel receives a three-phase mix of oil, water, and gas from the wells and separates them so they can be processed and recovered. When process control is upset, product can be lost and downstream equipment can be fouled, often requiring throughput to be significantly reduced to improve control.

Ensuring Accurate Oil Leg Flow Measurement for Allocation Integrity

Gas entrainment and water carryover distort oil flow measurement, causing over-reading and density errors when fluid properties change over time, requiring meters that handle shifting conditions without bias. Emerson's Micro Motion F Series Compact Coriolis Flow and Density Meters (Figure 12), combined with a Micro Motion 4700 Transmitter with Advanced Phase Measurement (APM), distinguish oil from water and detect entrained gas or water, delivering highly accurate mass flow across a wide pressure range, with optional Bluetooth Technology improving safety through wireless configuration.

Reliable Produced Water Flow Measurement for Environmental Compliance

Oil carryover into the water leg leads to production loss, downstream treatment challenges, and potential compliance risks. The water stream also often carries paraffin, scale, sand, and other contaminants that foul sensors and degrade performance over time. This makes reliable water flow measurement and durable instrumentation critical for both process and disposal needs. Emerson's Rosemount 9732 Magnetic Flow Transmitter and Rosemount 9205 Magnetic Flow Sensor (Figure 13) provide reliable measurement, advanced diagnostics, and ease of use, engineered to handle demanding oil and gas water applications.

Where a process penetration is not practical, Emerson's Flexim FLUXUS F731PW Permanent Flow Meter for Process Water (Figure 14) is a non-intrusive, clamp-on dual-channel ultrasonic flow meter, rated for water applications in hazardous areas. With volumetric flow accuracy better than ±1% of reading, it provides required data without piping modifications for water injection and produced water handling.

Managing Gas Outlet Flow Measurement Under Variable Separator Conditions

Gas at the separator outlet is rarely truly single-phase because condensate and liquid droplet carryover are common due to wet gas, causing single-phase gas meters to overstate actual dry gas volumes, introducing errors into sales allocation and emissions reporting. Gas composition, pressure, and temperature vary at the outlet, and upset conditions can also introduce oil into the gas line, making accurate measurement difficult with conventional technologies. Emerson's Rosemount 4088 MultiVariable Transmitter (Figure 15) compensates for these changing conditions to provide reliable readings for production monitoring.

FIGURE 15 Rosemount 4088 MultiVariable Transmitter is well suited to measuring gas flow at a separator.

Optimizing Three-Phase Separator Performance with Reliable Level Measurement

Reliable level measurement is essential for safe, efficient, and stable three-phase separator operation. The Rosemount 5300 Guided Wave Radar delivers continuous level and interface measurement to optimize separation performance, and the Rosemount 9930 Magnetic Level Indicator provides local visual verification for quick field validation (Figure 16). Together, these technologies form a comprehensive measurement and safety solution that helps maximize separation efficiency, prevent process upsets, improve safety, reduce maintenance costs, and extend asset life.

FIGURE 16 Rosemount 5300 Guided Wave Radar and Rosemount 9930 Magnetic Level Indicator provide level and interface monitoring.

Monitoring Sand Accumulation to Maintain Separator Performance

Sand accumulation in the separator reduces effective volume and can plug outlets or damage internals, often going undetected without continuous level monitoring, especially in unconventional wells where frac sand and fines are highest during early flowback. Real-time sand level detection enables proactive cleanup to protect vessel integrity and maintain separation efficiency. Emerson's Rosemount 2140 Level Detector Vibrating Fork (Figure 17) provides a reliable alert when sand reaches the sensor, indicating that cleanout is required.

FIGURE 17 Rosemount 2140 vibrating fork can differentiate between water and sand and signal when too much sand has accumulated.
