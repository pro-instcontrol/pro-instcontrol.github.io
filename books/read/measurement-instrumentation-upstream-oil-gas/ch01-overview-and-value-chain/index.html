Measurement Instrumentation Across the Upstream Value Chain

From wellhead to custody transfer, reliable measurement reduces uncertainty, protects people and assets, safeguards revenue, and improves performance across the upstream value chain.

Why measurement matters

The world's energy consumption patterns and supply chains are constantly changing, driven by availability, cost, and geopolitical disruptions. This affects the upstream segment particularly as it often requires rebalancing production across the globe. In these conditions, maintaining safe, reliable, and profitable operations depends on accurate, consistent insight into what is happening across production assets.

Reliable measurement instrumentation plays a critical role to meet these challenges by reducing uncertainty, improving visibility, and maintaining control across upstream operations. Across production, five common challenges consistently impact performance, safety, and revenue.

Protecting People and Assets in High-Risk Environments

All links in the energy production chain are hazardous, especially the upstream segment. Well sites deal with flammable products, high pressures, and toxic gases such as hydrogen sulfide (H2S), so safety must remain the top priority. Operators must have the tools to detect abnormal conditions early to prevent incidents, protect personnel, and avoid unplanned downtime.

Improving Efficiency Through Accurate Allocation Measurement

Many well sites have inadequate instrumentation, leading to uncertain production data. When oil, gas, and water measurements are unreliable, it impacts revenue, production optimization, and reservoir understanding. With continuous and accurate allocation measurement, decision-making and revenue reporting are more accurate.

Advancing Sustainability Without Sacrificing Performance

While upstream sites are reducing overall methane emissions, they still account for about 75% of all methane lost within the industry. This has environmental effects, but also represents lost product, so reducing fugitive emissions, minimizing flaring, and improving energy efficiency are critical responsibilities. Operators need reliable measurement to identify losses, verify improvements, and demonstrate compliance.

Reducing Reservoir Uncertainty Through Better Well Testing

Production strategy for individual wells depends on frequent well testing to verify reservoir models. Where testing is inaccurate or haphazard, production can suffer unnecessarily. Continuous, reliable measurement enables better long-term field planning.

Minimizing Human Error Through Standardization & Guided Maintenance

Producers are constantly dealing with the effects of remote locations, workforce turnover, and manual processes. These work together to increase operational risk and increase costs. Deploying instrumentation to provide standardized, automated measurements with diagnostic data reduces reliance on manual intervention and helps optimize assets.

Scope of this eBook

The oil & gas production chain begins in the upstream segment, covering exploration and initial production. This eBook will concentrate on sites already in production, focusing on these three main areas: well operation and management, including various enhanced recovery techniques; initial separation into crude oil, natural gas, and water streams via a conventional separator or heater treater; and custody transfer as oil and gas move to their respective next stages.
