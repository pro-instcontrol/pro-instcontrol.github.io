Natural gas fiscal metering governs custody agreements between operators and midstream partners.

Why small errors become large disputes

Natural gas fiscal metering governs custody agreements between operators and midstream partners, defining measurement standards, recalibration frequency, and providing financial settlement terms. Systematic errors, meter drift, and poor volume correction quietly accumulate into significant volume discrepancies, driving lost and unaccounted for gas (LAUF) between contracting parties. Upstream piping configuration, control valve turbulence, flow disturbances, and contamination can distort velocity profile measurements, introducing systematic bias that directly impacts custody transfer accuracy and LAUF calculations.

Maintaining Gas Custody Transfer Accuracy Across Integrated Systems

Natural gas transactions are settled on an energy or volume basis, so inaccurate composition measurement directly misstates custody transfer billing. In commingled gathering systems, rapidly changing composition requires a chromatograph that can track these shifts, otherwise systematic errors and disputes with midstream partners can occur. Multi-vendor setups introduce integration gaps, calibration mismatches, and split accountability, complicating commissioning, certification, and ongoing LAUF resolution.
