Why reliability matters more than sensitivity alone

Flammable and toxic gases are present at every unit operation, so continuous monitoring is a regulatory requirement. A single undetected release can escalate to catastrophic loss of containment with consequences far exceeding any detection system cost. Frequent false alarms erode operator trust and create dangerous complacency, so reliable detection with minimal nuisance trips is equally as critical as sensitivity. Many well-run and well-maintained production sites, outfitted with appropriate safety equipment and staffed by conscientious people at all levels, compile a long record of safety.

Detecting Flammable and Toxic Gas Hazards in Remote Production Facilities

Emerson offers a range of flammable and toxic gas detection solutions to meet the needs of any upstream oil and gas application. Point gas detectors like Rosemount 925FGD and Rosemount 625IR (Figure 26) are typically installed at specific high-risk locations. For facilities where wired infrastructure is impractical, the Rosemount 928 Wireless Gas Monitor offers a WirelessHART solution for detecting flammable and toxic gases with long battery life and high network reliability. If larger area coverage is needed, the Rosemount 935 and Rosemount 936 Open Path Gas Detectors are an excellent solution for long-range line-of-sight flammable and toxic gas detection.

Detecting Pressurized Gas Leaks

Another way to detect gas leaks is to use Emerson's Incus Ultrasonic Gas Leak Detector (Figure 27). It is an acoustic detector, listening for high-frequency sound created by a compressed gas leak of any kind and alerting plant personnel so that they can respond effectively. The Incus is a great choice to use in combination with gas detectors because it is unaffected by wind, rain, or other environmental conditions and does not need to be in contact with the gas cloud to detect a leak.

Ensuring Rapid Flame Detection for Incident Response

Steam, dust, and solar radiation can trigger false alarms while obscuring real flame signatures, and mismatched spectral response can delay or miss detection. Rapid detection of unexpected fires is critical in oil & gas facilities to prevent equipment damage and protect personnel. Emerson's Rosemount 975 Flame Detector with QuadSense Technology (Figure 28) uses four IR sensors to detect hydrocarbon fires within seconds while remaining highly immune to common false alarm sources, such as sunlight, arc welding, and lighting.
