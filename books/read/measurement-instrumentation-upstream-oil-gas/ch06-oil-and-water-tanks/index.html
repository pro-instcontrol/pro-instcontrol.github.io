Oil and Water Tanks

Production tanks store separated crude oil and produced water before transfer or disposal.

Why tank measurement carries financial and legal risk

Production tanks store separated crude oil and produced water before transfer or disposal, with measurement forming the basis for production accounting, royalty calculations, and regulatory reporting. Measurement integrity at this stage carries direct financial, legal, and compliance risk because undetected overfills, leaks, or errors can lead to spills, regulatory exposure, and environmental liability, making reliable level monitoring and high-level alarms essential.

Reliable Level Measurement for Hydrocarbon Recovery and Produced Water Storage Tanks

Reliable level and interface measurement is essential for maximizing hydrocarbon recovery, maintaining production accountability, and preventing overfills in crude oil and produced water storage tanks. The Rosemount 3408 Non-Contacting Radar Level Transmitter (Figure 21) delivers accurate gross level measurement with Smart Echo Supervision technology for dependable performance in challenging conditions, including produced water applications where vapors, foam, chemical injection, and turbulent process conditions can interfere with measurement. Because it is non-contacting, the Rosemount 3408 avoids the fouling and mechanical wear risks associated with probe-based technologies, making it well suited for produced water service and installations where inserting an electrode is difficult.

For interface measurement, the Rosemount 5300 Guided Wave Radar provides highly accurate, SIL 2-certified level and interface measurement to support product quality and minimize losses. For independent overfill protection and Emergency Shutdown (ESD) functions, the Rosemount 2120 Vibrating Fork Level Switch provides reliable point-level detection as an additional layer of protection. Together, these technologies help optimize product recovery, improve safety, reduce environmental risk, and support production accounting and regulatory compliance.
