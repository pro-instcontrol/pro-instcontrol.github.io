Well Pad and Individual Wellheads

First-point measurement at the wellhead sets the ceiling for everything downstream: safety, allocation and revenue.

Measurement at the well pad

The well pad is the primary interface between the subsurface reservoir and surface production, with core wellhead equipment (casing head, tubing head, Christmas tree) providing mechanical control, pressure containment, and first-point flow management in onshore, offshore, and subsea environments. Measurement made here directly influence safety, production allocation, and downstream revenue, yet are complicated by changing flow regimes, extreme pressure and temperature, abrasive solids, and corrosive H2S, CO2, and chlorides. As the first opportunity to detect anomalies such as well loading, choke erosion, or pressure excursions, wellhead instruments must deliver reliable, accurate data under harsh conditions.

Reading the instrument map

The source diagram places PT1 and PT2 pressure transmitters on the tubing and casing/flowline, TT for non-intrusive temperature, SM for entrained sand, FT for multiphase flow, GD and UGLD for gas and ultrasonic leak detection, FD for flame detection, and CM for corrosion and erosion. Together they form the first line of measurement and protection on the well pad.

Managing Rapid Wellhead Pressure Changes Across Remote Assets

Wellhead pressure can change rapidly during choke adjustments, startups, and shut-in events, making control and issue detection difficult across remote multi-well pads. Wired infrastructure is costly and time intensive to deploy at every wellhead, limiting visibility as operations grow. Emerson's Rosemount 4051S Pressure Transmitter (Figure 2) addresses this issue by delivering fast, accurate pressure measurement of tubing, casing, and flowlines, even during transient conditions. With 0.025% span accuracy and 20-year installed stability, the Rosemount 4051S provides reliable data to run, control, and monitor wells — while detecting pressure imbalances caused by leaks.

Where wellheads are distributed across a large well pad, Emerson's Rosemount 3051S Wireless Pressure Transmitter can be used with the Emerson Wireless 1410S Gateway to capture the same fast-changing pressure data without the need for cabling (Figure 3). This allows operators to maintain visibility and control across multiple installations while reducing installation time and infrastructure costs.

Monitoring Critical Temperature Changes in Harsh, Remote Wellhead Environments

Produced fluid temperature at the wellhead is a key indicator of well health and flow assurance, but reliable monitoring is difficult in remote, harsh environments with limited maintenance access. Temperature shifts can indicate well loading issues, hydrate risk, or changes in reservoir inflow, while large temperature swings can stress equipment and increase failure risk. Emerson's Rosemount X-well Technology measures process temperature without a thermowell or process penetration, reducing mechanical risk and enabling flexible placement (Figure 4). When used with the Rosemount 3144S Temperature Transmitter, which features Bluetooth connectivity and industry-leading 0.05°C accuracy, obtain stable, accurate temperature measurements over long service intervals, with simplified configuration and diagnostics.

FIGURE 4 Rosemount X-well Technology reads temperature through a pipe or vessel wall non-intrusively.

Reliable Multiphase Flow Measurement in Topside and Subsea Conditions

As reservoir conditions change, gas fraction, water cut, and liquid flow rates shift, making real-time multiphase measurement critical for allocation and production optimization. Emerson's Roxar 2600 Multiphase Flow Meter (Figure 5) measures oil, water, and gas inline before separation, supporting reliable allocation without test separators. Its compact design and use of impedance, venturi, and gamma density technologies deliver consistent data even in the most challenging and varied flowing conditions, for both single-well and multi-well applications. In subsea wellheads, where separators are unavailable and intervention costs are high, long-term reliable and repeatable measurements are essential. Emerson's Roxar Subsea Wetgas Meters provide accurate gas, water, and condensate flow rate measurements with unparalleled formation water detection sensitivity, supporting flow assurance in inaccessible subsea environments.

FIGURE 5 Roxar 2600 (topside, left) and Roxar Subsea Wetgas Meters (seafloor, right) measure oil, water and gas without separating the flow.

Monitoring Sand Production to Protect Wellhead Integrity

Undetected sand production drives rapid erosion of chokes, piping, valves, and instrumentation, and damage is often only discovered after failure, especially since sand rates spike during startups, and pressure changes and point-in-time sampling is not sufficient. Continuous monitoring is needed for effective choke management and erosion control. Emerson's Rosemount SAM42 Acoustic Particle Monitor (Figure 6) measures entrained sand in real time without process penetration, enabling installation without shutdown, and allowing operators to adjust velocity to limit metal loss while maintaining production.

FIGURE 6 Rosemount SAM42 detects conditions that threaten equipment life; it mounts without a process penetration, so no shutdown is necessary.

Ensuring Corrosion and Erosion Insight for Wellhead Asset Protection

H2S, CO2, and produced water create corrosive conditions that degrade pipe walls and instrument surfaces, with sand-driven erosion often occurring simultaneously. Without continuous monitoring, metal loss can go undetected until integrity is compromised, making it important to track corrosion and erosion separately to identify the dominant damage mechanism. Emerson's Rosemount Wireless ET410 Corrosion and Erosion Transmitter (Figure 7) provides real-time metal thickness data using a non-intrusive, battery-powered ultrasonic design, enabling continuous monitoring of corrosion and erosion impacts on assets.
